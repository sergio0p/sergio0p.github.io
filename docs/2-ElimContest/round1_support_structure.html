Round-1 Support Structure

Why IPV gives simple supports — the monotone posterior, the mirror symmetry, and the signaling obstruction

4-player, 2-round all-pay elimination contest with $K$ i.i.d. types, prior $f$; the solved instances are $K{=}2$ (binary): $v\in\{\ell,h\}=\{1,3\}$, equal prior, and $K{=}3$: $v\in\{\ell,m,h\}=\{1,2,3\}$, uniform prior.

Round 1 looks like an ordinary all-pay contest, but it hides a twist: the prize you win for advancing depends on how you bid, because your bid is a signal of your type. This note is the entry point to the model and its results, stated for a general number of types $K$, with the two solved instances presented side by side throughout — $K{=}3$ in §6, $K{=}2$ in its mirror §6′. It explains how the equilibrium is found by guess and verify; why the private-values (IPV) structure makes the bid supports simple single intervals, unlike Turocy's multi-interval supports (§3); where the naive analysis gets stuck (§5); and how the obstruction dissolves (§6): the continuation prize is strategy-independent, and the equilibrium posterior curve is elementary in closed form on the whole support — an $\operatorname{arctanh}$ branch on each side of a single kink.

The big picture — guess and verify

The whole pipeline on one page; §1–§6 and the $K{=}2$ mirror §6′ fill in the details.

The model. Four players, two rounds. Each player's type is an i.i.d. draw from $K$ ordered values $v_1<\cdots<v_K$ with prior $f=(f_v)$; the solved instances are $K{=}2$: $v\in\{\ell,h\}=\{1,3\}$, equal prior (§6′), and $K{=}3$: $v\in\{\ell,m,h\}=\{1,2,3\}$, uniform prior (§6). Round 1 is two pairwise all-pay auctions (higher bid advances); all Round-1 bids are then publicly revealed, and the two winners meet in an all-pay final worth $v$ to its winner. Because bids are revealed, a finalist's Round-1 bid is a signal of their type, and Zhang & Wang (2009) rule out any symmetric separating equilibrium. So we look for a non-separating equilibrium, and we find it by guess and verify: guess the support architecture and a monotonicity property, solve exactly under that guess, then check every assumption on the solved object.

Two facts that cost nothing

Two structural facts hold in any symmetric SPNE, before anything is guessed.

  1. (F1) The final is an IPV all-pay auction à la Siegel, with priors = the Round-1 posteriors. Types are i.i.d. and Round-1 mixing is independent across players, so conditional on the revealed bids the finalists' types are independent. The final is therefore an asymmetric-prior independent-private-values all-pay auction, solved exactly by Siegel's construction: its payoff $\Pi_2(v;p,q)$ is piecewise affine in the two finalists' posteriors $p,q$, with 2 linear regimes at $K{=}2$ (§6′b) and 10 at $K{=}3$ (§1, §6b). Round 2 is completely known as a function of beliefs; all the difficulty lives in Round 1.
  2. (F2) The opponent finalist's quantile law is strategy-free: $\Psi(t)=t^2$. Work in the bid quantile $t=G(b)$. Your final-round opponent is a Round-1 winner, so their quantile is uniform size-biased by the win probability — density $2s$, CDF $t^2$ — whatever the equilibrium strategy is (§6a). This is the hinge of the whole solution: it makes the continuation prize a functional of the posterior curve alone, dissolving the fixed point that blocks the naive approach (§5).

The guess

  1. (G1) Support architecture. Every type's support is one interval with common floor $0$: a mass point at bid $0$ (quantile weight $t_0$) carried by every type except the top one; above it, nested ceilings — the lower types exit one by one, and the top two types both reach the very top (no pure-top tail). At $K{=}2$: the atom is Low alone — the composite mass point in its smallest, one-type instance — and no type exits: both mix to the top (§6′e). At $K{=}3$: the atom is Low+Mid; Low exits at $\beta_\ell$; Mid and High mix to the top. Like (G2), this architecture is a maintained hypothesis — assumed, and checkable, not established; in particular the composite mass point (every non-top type pooled at bid $0$) is part of the guess, and "The verify" below records which of its checks have actually been run.
  2. (G2) Monotone posterior. The posterior $p(b)$ is FOSD-increasing in the bid: the top share rises, the bottom share falls. This is a maintained hypothesis, not a theorem — assumed going in on deep-learning evidence, verified ex post on the solved curve (§3).
  3. (G3) Regime census. On the equilibrium path the posterior pair $(p,q)$ visits only a short list of the regimes of the piecewise-affine $\Pi_2$; which regimes, and over which segments of the opponent-quantile axis, is fixed by the census (§3a) and re-checked after solving. At $K{=}2$: both of the 2 (§6′b). At $K{=}3$: 4 of the 10.

The solve

  1. Change of variables $t=G(b)$. Trade the bid for its quantile $t\in[0,1]$; the bid-$0$ mass point spreads over $[0,t_0]$ via the tie-break lottery, and the posterior is constant there. The unknown becomes the posterior curve — the posterior $p(b)$ of (G2), read in the quantile coordinate: $$P(t)\;=\;p\big(G^{-1}(t)\big),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad P\big(G(b)\big)\;=\;p(b),\qquad\text{\small(P)}$$ a point of the simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$ with components $p_v(t)$ (at $K{=}2$: $(P_\ell,P_h)(t)$ with $P_\ell=1-P_h$, one free coordinate; at $K{=}3$: $(P_\ell,P_m,P_h)(t)$); on the atom $[0,t_0]$, $G^{-1}$ is the quantile inverse ($\equiv0$ there), so (P) returns the constant atom posterior, matching the tie-break convention. The curve is the equilibrium: reading Bayes' rule backwards, type $v$'s bid-quantile density is $p_v(t)/f_v$, and every other equilibrium object — bids, $G$, densities, per-type CDFs — is recovered from the curve by the explicit pipeline of step 5. Nothing else needs to be solved for.
  2. The prize becomes a functional of the curve. By (F1)+(F2), $W(v,P(t))=\int_0^1 \Pi_2\big(v;P(t),P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds$ — the strategy no longer appears, so the indifference conditions close on the curve alone: genuine ODEs, not fixed-point equations (§5, §6a–§6c).
  3. Equate the two computations of the continuation gap $D_{uv}$. For an active pair of types $u,v$, the gap $D_{uv}(t):=W\big(u,P(t)\big)-W\big(v,P(t)\big)$ can be computed in two independent ways: indifference gives its equilibrium value — the first integrals (FI); the Round-2 payoffs give its expression in the curve — equation (D). Equating the two, band by band from the floor up, and differentiating once yields a determined first-order ODE system for $P(t)$; the two panels below display the equations and assemble the full system. At $K{=}2$ the system is scalar — one active pair, one free share — and closes in one $\operatorname{arctanh}$ quadrature (§6′c). At $K{=}3$ it triangularizes into a scalar $\operatorname{arctanh}$ ODE for the top share $P_h$, plus the payoff-gap identity $k_{hm}=t_0$ (§6c, §6f).
  4. Bayes consistency closes the system. The curve must average back to the prior: the $K-1$ moment conditions $\int_0^1 p_v(t)\,dt=f_v$ — equivalently, each type's quantile density $p_v/f_v$ integrates to one. These pin the free scalars: the atom width $t_0$ and the floor posterior $P(t_0)$, and with them the payoff gaps $k_{uv}$ and every threshold. At $K{=}2$: $\int_0^1 P_h\,dt=\tfrac12$ pins $t_0$ alone, and nothing else is free (§6′d). At $K{=}3$: $\int_0^1 P_h\,dt=\tfrac13$ pins $t_0$ alone; $t_0P_m(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^1 P_m\,dt=\tfrac13$ pins $P_m(t_0)$; then $k_{h\ell},k_{m\ell},t^\ast,\beta_\ell$ follow (§6d).
  5. Recover the equilibrium from the curve. With $P$ solved, everything else is explicit, in order: the prizes $W(v,P(t))$ are quadratures of knowns ($\Pi_2$ regime forms $+$ the law $\Psi$); the anchor $U_{v_1}=0$ — bid $0$ costs nothing and the bottom type's atom prize is identically zero (at $K{=}2$ and $K{=}3$ alike: $W(\ell,P(t_0))=0$, §6′d, §6d) — turns the payoff gaps into levels, $U_v=k_{vv_1}$; the bid map is read off the level identity, $b(t)=t\,W(v,P(t))-k_{vv_1}$, the same function for every active $v$ — that is exactly (FI) (at $K{=}2$: $b(t)=t\,W(\ell,P(t))$ on all of $[t_0,1]$; at $K{=}3$: the same formula on Low's support — in both, Low is the bottom type, so the subtracted constant is $0$); then $G=b^{-1}$, with the atom reappearing as $G$'s jump of size $t_0$ at bid $0$, the bid density $g(b(t))=1/b'(t)$, and the per-type CDFs $G_v(b(t))=\tfrac1{f_v}\int_0^t p_v(s)\,ds$.
two computations of the same gap — the equations that determine the curve general K

Fix a band: an interval of quantiles on which the active set $\mathcal A(t)$ (the types still mixing) and the regime census are constant. Under (G1) the active set only shrinks as $t$ rises, one exit at a time. Along the curve write $D_{uv}(t):=W\big(u,P(t)\big)-W\big(v,P(t)\big)$ for the continuation gap of a type pair $u,v$, and $k_{uv}:=U_u-U_v$ for the corresponding payoff gap — a constant, since the levels are. Payoff gaps add, $k_{uw}=k_{uv}+k_{vw}$, and levels are the gaps against the bottom type, $U_v=k_{vv_1}$, since $U_{v_1}=0$. The gap of an active pair can then be computed in two independent ways — neither computation uses any ingredient of the other:

  • Its equilibrium value — from indifference. Subtracting the two level identities of §2 at a common bid (one line, no calculus) gives the first integrals $$t\,D_{uv}(t)\;=\;k_{uv}\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathcal A(t)\ \text{— constant along the band.}\qquad\text{\small(FI)}$$ So in equilibrium $D_{uv}(t)=k_{uv}/t$: a known number at each $t$, up to the constants $k_{uv}$. Round 2 plays no part in this computation.
  • Its expression in the curve — from the Round-2 payoffs. Substitute the prize formula of solve step 2 into the definition $D_{uv}(t)=W\big(u,P(t)\big)-W\big(v,P(t)\big)$. Every symbol on the right is already in hand — $\Pi_2$ is the Round-2 payoff of (F1), $2s$ the opponent-quantile density of (F2), $P$ the curve of step 1: $$D_{uv}(t)\;=\;\int_0^1\Big[\Pi_2\big(u;\,P(t),\,P(s)\big)-\Pi_2\big(v;\,P(t),\,P(s)\big)\Big]\,2s\,ds .\qquad\text{\small(D)}$$ This computation never mentions indifference: (D) is an explicit integral of the curve, depending on the point value $P(t)$ through the first (own-posterior) slot of $\Pi_2$ and on the whole arc $s\mapsto P(s)$ through the second. Its structure is read off in three steps. Split: by the census (G3), as $s$ runs $0\to1$ the posterior pair $\big(P(t),P(s)\big)$ passes through finitely many regimes of $\Pi_2$, so the integral breaks into one piece per visited regime. Affine forms: on each piece $\Pi_2$ is affine in its two posterior slots (F1), so the integrand is affine in $P(t)$ and $P(s)$. Integrate: the $P(t)$-part is constant in $s$ and pulls out of its piece multiplied by the segment's $\Psi$-mass $\int 2s\,ds$; the $P(s)$-part integrates to moments of the curve, $\int 2s\,p_w(s)\,ds$ over the segment. So (D) is affine in $P(t)$, with coefficients built from the segment $\Psi$-masses, plus moments of the curve. The split is carried out in full in §3a; §6′c writes out the single $K{=}2$ gap (one moment, $A_3$), §6c the two $K{=}3$ gaps (moments $A_2,A_3$).

Substituting the expression (D) into the value equation (FI) gives the equations of the solve: on every band, for every active pair, the integral (D) evaluated on the unknown curve must equal $k_{uv}/t$. The unknown is always the curve $P$, never $D_{uv}$ — the gap's value is already (FI); the entire content of the equation is that the expression (D) of the curve must return that value. And the count is right: exited types have $p_v\equiv0$ and the shares sum to one, so $P(t)$ has $|\mathcal A(t)|-1$ free coordinates — exactly the number of independent pair equations the active set supplies.

One differentiation turns the equations into a genuine ODE system. Differentiating (FI) gives, for each active pair, the first-order equation

$$\frac{d}{dt}\,D_{uv}(t)\;=\;-\,\frac{k_{uv}}{t^{2}}\,,\qquad\text{\small(ODE)}$$

and on the left, differentiating the expression (D), the integral character of the equation cancels itself. The Leibniz rule produces boundary terms only where the pieces of (D) meet — at the regime crossings — and each such term vanishes because $\Pi_2$ is continuous across every regime boundary, so the two adjacent affine forms agree at the crossing (§3a, step 4). In the expanded form of (D) the same cancellation reads: the endpoint derivatives of the moments, $2t\,p_w(t)$, cancel against the derivatives of the $\Psi$-masses — §6c performs this explicitly, the moments $A_2,A_3$ dropping out. What survives is exactly the chain-rule term in $P'$: $$\nabla_pD_{uv}\cdot P'(t)\;=\;-\,\frac{k_{uv}}{t^{2}}\,,\qquad \nabla_pD_{uv}\;=\;\sum_{j}\psi_j(t)\,\Big(\gamma_u^{[c_j(t)]}-\gamma_v^{[c_j(t)]}\Big),$$ which is the gradient formula $(\nabla)$ of §3a applied to the pair: $j$ runs over the segments of the census, $\gamma_v^{[c]}$ are the constant regime gradients tabulated in §3a (step 5), and $\psi_j(t)$ the segment $\Psi$-masses (step 6). So (ODE) is linear in $P'(t)$ with elementary coefficients — a determined first-order system in the curve, not an integro-differential or fixed-point equation.

The solve is then mechanical in outline: start at the floor posterior, whose top share vanishes under (G1), and integrate upward. But "the determined system" deserves to be spelled out in full — the panel below assembles it: every unknown, every equation, and the order of the march.

the system, assembled — every unknown, every equation general K

The equations above were introduced one at a time; here is the complete system the solve integrates. Everything is stated on a band — an interval of quantiles on which the active set $\mathcal A(t)$ and the visited-regime list (G3) are constant.

  1. The unknowns. At each quantile $t$: the $|\mathcal A(t)|-1$ free shares of the posterior $P(t)$, and the positions $s_j(t)$ of the marginal opponents (next item). Nothing else is unknown along a band.
  2. The marginal opponents, and their defining equations. As the opponent quantile $s$ runs $0\to1$, the opponent finalist's believed strength rises (G2), and the final changes character at finitely many opponent quantiles. One crossing is the diagonal $s=t$, where the believed-stronger role changes sides; its position is known, so it contributes no unknown. The others are marginal opponents: quantiles $s_j(t)$ at which the pair of posteriors reaches a regime boundary of the final — economically, an opponent against whom two types, one on each side of the final, are just equally willing to contest it, so the final's regime is about to change. Each marginal opponent adds one unknown function and brings exactly one defining equation — the two affine forms of $\Pi_2$ adjacent at the crossing agree there: $$\Pi_2^{[c_j]}\big(v;\,P(t),\,P(s_j(t))\big)\;=\;\Pi_2^{[c_{j+1}]}\big(v;\,P(t),\,P(s_j(t))\big),\qquad\text{\small(B)}$$ one scalar affine relation between $P(t)$ and $P\big(s_j(t)\big)$ — continuity of $\Pi_2$ makes the crossing common to all types $v$, so any $v$ gives the same relation. The $K{=}3$ instance of (B) is the boundary equation $(\omega)$ of §6c.
  3. The differential rows. For each adjacent pair of active types, one row — a single equation in two equivalent forms: integrated, it is the first integral (FI), $t\,D_{uv}=k_{uv}$; differentiated, it is (ODE) with the gradient $(\nabla)$, linear in $P'(t)$ with coefficients elementary in $t$ and in the positions $s_j(t)$ (the regime gradients $\gamma$ are constants; the $\Psi$-masses $\psi_j$ are differences of squares of the crossing positions). That is $|\mathcal A(t)|-1$ rows. Together with one equation (B) per marginal opponent, unknowns and equations balance: the system is square.
  4. Start. At the floor $t_0$ the state is the mass-point posterior of (G1) — top share zero. From there, integrate upward in $t$.
  5. Crossings and exits along the way. A marginal opponent may already be present at the floor; along the way they appear and disappear only through the two ends of the opponent axis. Through the top $s=1$, the segment's $\Psi$-mass vanishes smoothly — no kink. Through the mass point at the bottom, a segment of positive $\Psi$-mass opens at once: the curve stays continuous — the two adjacent affine forms agree at the crossing (§3b) — but its slope jumps: a kink. At an exit $\beta_v$, located by $p_v(\beta_v)=0$, type $v$ leaves the pool: its share and its pair row leave together, so the system stays square to the very top; that the exited type strictly prefers to stay out is checked in the verify.
  6. Non-locality, and when it dissolves. (B) links the curve at two quantiles, $t$ and $s_j(t)$. A marginal opponent behind ($s_j<t$) references a stretch of curve already built; one ahead ($s_j>t$) references the curve ahead of the front — but read from the other side, the same relation says that $t$ is itself the marginal opponent of that higher quantile, so no information beyond (B) is ever needed (§6c reads $(\omega)$ both ways). The system is therefore a functional-differential system, determined band by band. Whether the non-locality dissolves outright is instance-specific: at $K{=}2$ no marginal opponent ever arises, and at $K{=}3$ the relation (B) first-integrates to a linear relation between the two quantiles (§6c) — see the instances below.

Left out deliberately: the constants. The integration treats the floor $t_0$, the floor posterior $P(t_0)$, and the payoff gaps $k_{uv}$ as given parameters. They are pinned after it, by the bottom type's zero-payoff anchor $U_{v_1}=0$ and Bayes consistency with the prior — solve steps 4–5 above and the closure tables §6d, §6′d. None of that belongs to the differential system.

The two solved instances. K=2 One active pair on all of $[t_0,1]$, one free share, and no marginal opponent ever arises: the system is the single row (FI), a scalar separable ODE, closed by one $\operatorname{arctanh}$ quadrature (§6′c). K=3 Two rows. Their difference — the adjacent High–Mid row — happens to be univariate in the top share $P_h$: (ODE) collapses to the scalar $P_h'(t)=3t_0/\big(t^2(3-t^2)\big)$, an $\operatorname{arctanh}$ quadrature, with the payoff-gap identity $k_{hm}=t_0$ as a by-product (§6c, §6f). With $P_h$ in hand, the Mid–Low row and the relation (B) — the $(\omega)$ of §6c — return $P_m$, elementary except on the short band before $\beta_\ell$; on the top two-type interval, $P_m=1-P_h$ closes the system (§6f). Whether the adjacent rows stay univariate at general $K$ — the triangularization — is the central structural question for scaling up.

The verify

The guessed ingredients are then checked on the solved object: the first-integral residuals $|t\,D_{uv}-k_{uv}|$ hold uniformly (at $K{=}2$: quadrature-exact, $5\times10^{-13}$, §6′d; at $K{=}3$: $1.3\times10^{-7}$, the gate, §6d); the posterior is FOSD-increasing ex post (G2 ✓); every exited type strictly prefers to stay out beyond its ceiling (at $K{=}2$ no type exits, so there is nothing to check; at $K{=}3$: $U_\ell<0$ above $\beta_\ell$, so the exclusion is admissible, §6f); the on-path regime census matches (G3 ✓). Two things this does not address. Bid-$0$ incentive compatibility of the atom (part of G1): OPEN at $K{=}3$ — never checked. The gate tests the interior first integrals along the curve; no check has been run that every type pooled at bid $0$ is willing to stay there. At $K{=}2$ the same check closes: the atom's only carrier is the bottom type, whose atom prize $W(\ell,P(t_0))$ is identically zero, so bidding $0$ earns exactly $U_\ell=0$ however the tie-break lottery weights the atom (§6′d). And uniqueness: the construction delivers an equilibrium, not a proof that it is the only one. The claim-by-claim proved/machine-checked ledger is docs/STATE.md + docs/proofs.tex.

what scales, what must be redone the K-ladder

Already general in $K$: the final is a Siegel all-pay auction with piecewise-affine $\Pi_2$ (F1); the opponent-quantile law $\Psi(t)=t^2$, strategy-free (F2, §6a); the smoothing of $\Pi_2$'s kinks into a $C^1$ prize (§3b); the gradient formula $(\nabla)$ — one constant vector per visited regime (§3a); the cone principle giving fixed-sign signaling channels for the two extreme types (§3a); and the band-by-band determinacy of the assembled system (FI)/(ODE)/(B) above.

To be redone at each $K$: the regime complex of $\Pi_2$ and its on-path census (at $K{=}2$: 2 regimes, both visited; at $K{=}3$: 10 regimes, 4 visited); sign verdicts for the $K-2$ interior types, on which the cone argument is silent (§3a); the cancellation that makes the adjacent gaps univariate — hence the closed form (at $K{=}2$ the single gap is univariate by itself; at $K{=}3$: the $D_{hm}$ cancellation, then the $\operatorname{arctanh}$); and admissibility at each exit. The guess (G1)–(G3) is itself the pattern of the two solved instances $K{=}2$ and $K{=}3$, proposed for general $K$.

1. The object: a signaling all-pay auction

In the pipeline: defines the objects — $G$, the posterior $p(b)$, the prize $W$, the interim payoff (U).

general K

Fix a player of type $v$ among $K$ types. Within a group, two players bid in an all-pay auction; the higher bid advances to the final. Write

  • $G_v(b)$ — the CDF of type $v$'s Round-1 bid, with density $g_v(b)=G_v'(b)$ — capital letters for bid CDFs, matching lowercase for their densities;
  • the chance of winning Round 1 is the rival's bid CDF, $G(b)=\sum_u f_u\,G_u(b)$, with density $g(b)=\sum_u f_u\,g_u(b)$, where $f_u$ is the prior probability of type $u$; the profile of densities $\sigma=(g_v)_{v=1}^K$ is the Round-1 strategy we solve for;
  • the bid is a signal: winning with bid $b$ induces a posterior over your own type, $$p_v(b)=\frac{f_v\,g_v(b)}{\sum_u f_u\,g_u(b)}=\frac{f_v\,g_v(b)}{g(b)}\qquad\text{\small(post)}$$ a point in the type simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$ ($K{-}1$ free coordinates, since $\sum_v p_v=1$); K=2 with the equal prior, $f_v\equiv\tfrac12$ cancels in (post) the same way and the simplex is $\Delta^1$ — a segment, one free coordinate $p_h$; K=3 with the uniform prior used from §2 on, $f_v\equiv\tfrac13$ cancels in (post) and the simplex is $\Delta^2$;
  • the continuation value of reaching the final as type $v$ with posterior $p$ (the belief vector $p=(p_v)_{v=1}^K$ your winning bid induced) is $$W(v,p)=\int \Pi_2\!\big(v;\,p,\,q\big)\,d\Psi(q),\qquad\text{\small(W)}$$ where $q$ is the opponent finalist's posterior, $\Psi$ its distribution, and $\Pi_2$ the Round-2 payoff; K=2 $\Pi_2$ is piecewise-affine over a 2-regime complex — own ahead / own behind, split at the diagonal (§6′b); K=3 $\Pi_2$ is piecewise-affine over a 10-regime complex (derived in docs/tropical_cell_structure.html / memory three-type-r2-tropical).

The interim Round-1 payoff of bidding $b$ as type $v$ is

$$U_v(b)\;=\;\underbrace{G(b)}_{\text{win prob}}\cdot\underbrace{W\big(v,\,p(b)\big)}_{\text{prize}}\;-\;\underbrace{b}_{\text{all-pay cost}}.\qquad\text{\small(U)}$$
the twist general K

The prize $W(v,p(b))$ depends on your own bid through the belief $p(b)$. This is not a Siegel contest, where the prize is a fixed number. It is a signaling all-pay auction — the genus of Hörner & Sahuguet, Costly Signalling in Auctions (2007). Siegel's clean support theory does not apply off the shelf; the question is whether IPV restores enough structure to recover his conclusions anyway.

2. Indifference: the level identity and its derivatives

In the pipeline: the primitive identity behind (FI) — the equilibrium value of the gap, the indifference half of the big-picture panel — and, as its derivatives, the marginal conditions $(\star_v)$, $(\star_{uv})$ used from §3 on.

general K

Where a type mixes, every bid in its support must give the same payoff: $U_v(b)\equiv U_v$, a constant. Read this constancy in the quantile coordinate of solve step 1 — $t=G(b)$, with $b(t)$ the bid at quantile $t$ — using (U) and (P). Above the atom ($t>t_0$) it is the level identity:

the level identity — the primitive fact general K $$U_v\;=\;t\,W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;-\;b(t)\qquad\text{for every }t\text{ in }v\text{'s support above the atom.}\qquad\text{\small(lev)}$$

In an all-pay round the cost $b(t)$ is sunk and $t\,W(v,P(t))$ is the expected continuation prize, so the right side is just (U) read at quantile $t$; mixing makes it flat in $t$. Everything §2 owes the pipeline follows from (lev) by subtraction or differentiation.

First the subtraction — it is the whole derivation of (FI). Take two types $u,v$ both active at the same bid $b(t)$ and subtract their level identities: the common sunk cost cancels, and with it every trace of the strategy:

(FI) in one line — no calculus general K $$U_u-U_v\;=\;\big[t\,W\big(u,P(t)\big)-b(t)\big]-\big[t\,W\big(v,P(t)\big)-b(t)\big]\;=\;t\,D_{uv}(t)\;=:\;k_{uv} .$$

No densities, no cancellation of $g$, no smoothness of $W$: only two constants subtracted at a common bid. The constant $k_{uv}$ is the same on every band where the pair is active, because the levels $U_u,U_v$ are global constants. This is the equilibrium value half of the big-picture panel; the Round-2 expression (D) is the other half.

The derivative forms are consequences, kept because §3–§6 use them ($W$ is $C^1$ along the curve, §3b, so they exist). Differentiating $U_v(b)\equiv U_v$ — the bid-space form of (lev) — splits the marginal incentive into two channels:

$$\underbrace{g(b)\,W\big(v,p(b)\big)}_{\text{win-probability channel}}\;+\;\underbrace{G(b)\,\nabla_p W\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b)}_{\text{signaling channel}}\;=\;1.\qquad\text{\small($\star_v$)}$$

Subtracting one type's condition from another cancels the $1$ and leaves the pairwise indifference (single-crossing) conditions $(\star_{uv})$ — the difference of $(\star_u)$ and $(\star_v)$ for an active pair of types $u,v$ (two plain letters: primes are reserved for derivatives). Just as $(\star_v)$ is the $b$-derivative of (lev), $(\star_{uv})$ is exactly the $b$-derivative of (FI):

$$g(b)\,\big[W(u,p)-W(v,p)\big]\;+\;G(b)\,\big[\nabla_p W(u,p)-\nabla_p W(v,p)\big]\cdot p'(b)\;=\;0.\qquad\text{\small($\star_{uv}$)}$$

Finally, differentiating (FI) itself in $t$ gives the system (ODE) of the big-picture panel: $\frac{d}{dt}\big[t\,D_{uv}\big]=0$ unpacks to $D_{uv}'=-D_{uv}/t=-k_{uv}/t^2$. So the logic runs levels first, derivatives second: (lev) gives (FI) by subtraction; $(\star_v)$, $(\star_{uv})$, (ODE) are its derivatives. Nothing is lost in either direction — $(\star_{uv})$ is (FI)'s derivative, so integrating it back merely recovers (FI).

K=2 there is a single pair, and the single-crossing fact is $\nabla_p\big[W(h,p)-W(\ell,p)\big]<0$ (roadmap §7); the entire §6′ solve is the single instance $t\,D_{h\ell}=k_{h\ell}$ of (FI), equated to its Round-2 expression (D). K=3 the active pairs are $(h,\ell)$ and $(m,\ell)$; the entire §6 solve is the two instances $t\,D_{h\ell}=k_{h\ell}$, $t\,D_{m\ell}=k_{m\ell}$ of (FI), equated to their Round-2 expressions (D). Everything below is about understanding the two ingredients in $(\star_v)$: the signaling channel (governed by the monotone posterior) and the fact that $W$ is the same unknown as the strategy (the obstruction).

3. The monotone posterior — what IPV buys

In the pipeline: the monotone posterior (G2) and the reductions it buys, including the regime census (G3).

the monotone posterior (G2) — its status K=2 and K=3 — derived; general $K$ — maintained hypothesis

The posterior is FOSD-increasing in the bid. At $K{=}2$ and $K{=}3$ this is a consequence of the indifference conditions rather than an input to them: the strongest active type's share strictly rises and the weakest active type's share strictly falls, and at $K{=}3$ the tail sums are exactly $p_h$ and $1-p_\ell$, so those two facts are FOSD. Both come from the adjacent-pair system, which unravels from the top (companion note triangular_cascade.html); at $K{=}2$ the whole content is the single share, $P_h'(t)=3t_0/\big(t^2(3-2t^2)\big)>0$ on all of $[t_0,1]$. High bids signal high types. The solved curves say the same thing quantitatively — at $K{=}3$, $P_h$ rises $0\to0.769$ and the Low share falls $0.588\to0$; the deep-learning Round-1 run has $p_h(b)$ climbing $0.05\to0.65$ and $p_\ell(b)$ falling $0.59\to0.10$ as the bid rises (the middle share $p_m(b)$ pinned by $p_\ell{+}p_m{+}p_h{=}1$). Beyond $K{=}3$ it is a maintained hypothesis: assume it, solve under it, and check it on the solved curve afterwards. What it buys outright is the regime census (G3) — which regimes the posterior pair visits, and over which segments of the opponent-quantile axis; the fixed-sign reductions below stand on their own footing.

3a. The signaling channel has a fixed sign

general K, specialized to K=2 and K=3 below

Fix a bid $b$ and look at the second term of $(\star_v)$, the signaling channel $$\nabla_p W\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b).$$ This is one type's own channel, and it is what governs whether that type's bid rises with the rung it occupies — hence whether its support is a single interval. The pairwise indifference conditions do not deliver it: those fix the differences $\nabla_p\big(W(u,\cdot)-W(v,\cdot)\big)\cdot p'(b)$, which carry no information about any single type's channel. So it has to be signed on its own. We want conditions under which it has a sign that does not change as $b$ sweeps the support. Two ingredients enter: the velocity $p'(b)$ of the belief path, and the gradient $\nabla_pW(v,\cdot)$ of the prize. Monotonicity of the posterior constrains the first; the Round-2 economics constrains the second. The argument below is stated for a general number of types $K$, then specialized to $K=2$ (where it degenerates to something already familiar) and to $K=3$ (where it has real content).

The state space, and why its dimension is the whole story

With $K$ types the posterior $p=(p_v)_{v=1}^K$ lives in the simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$, so $\sum_v p_v=1$ and the velocity satisfies $\sum_v p_v'=0$. The tangent space is therefore of dimension $K-1$:

$K=2$$p_h$, with $p_\ell=1-p_h$ eliminated$\dim=1$ — a lineexactly two: $\pm1$
$K=3$$(p_h,p_\ell)$, with $p_m=1-p_h-p_\ell$ eliminated$\dim=2$ — a planea whole circle

This dimension count is what separates the two cases below.

what monotonicity actually gives general K

In component form, the monotone posterior above is the statement that the High share rises and the Low share falls with the bid: $$p_h'(b)>0,\qquad p_\ell'(b)<0\qquad\text{throughout the mixing region.}\tag{M}$$ This is a statement about the signs of the components, i.e. about which orthant the velocity occupies. Equivalently: there is a fixed convex cone $\mathcal{C}$ in the tangent space with $$p'(b)\in\mathcal{C}\quad\text{for every }b.$$ K=3 $\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-)\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+)$, an open quadrant. K=2 only the first inequality survives and $\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-$, an open half-line. Note what (M) does not say: nothing about the ratio of the components, hence nothing about where inside $\mathcal{C}$ the velocity points.

The principle: two cones in duality

general K

Write the channel as an inner product in the tangent space and recall the definition of the polar (dual) cone, $$\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\;=\;\{z:\ \langle z,x\rangle\le 0\ \ \text{for all }x\in\mathcal{C}\}.$$

the correct principle — any $K$ $$\left.\begin{aligned}p'(b)&\in\mathcal{C}\\ \nabla_pW(v,p(b))&\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\end{aligned}\right\}\ \text{for all }b \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \nabla_pW(v,p(b))\cdot p'(b)<0\ \text{ for all }b,$$

and symmetrically $\nabla_pW\in\operatorname{int}(-\mathcal{C}^{\circ})$ gives a strictly positive channel — immediate from the definition of the polar cone. The hypotheses are membership in the two orthants only: no constancy of the direction $p'/|p'|$, no magnitude bound on either vector. Smoothness of $W$ (§3b) is what makes $\nabla_pW$ exist so that it can be placed in a cone at all.

Specialization I: $K=2$ — the degenerate case

The tangent space is a line, so $p'(b)$ and $\nabla_pW(v,p)$ are scalars and the channel is a product of two numbers, $$\nabla_pW\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b),\qquad p'(b)>0 .$$ Its sign is simply the sign of $\nabla_pW(v,p)$, and the cone condition $\nabla_pW\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{C}^\circ=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+$ is the statement $\nabla_pW(v,p)<0$. The solved binary case supplies both signs explicitly (roadmap §7, with $\Psi=G^2$ and $\Delta=v_h-v_\ell$):

$$\nabla_pW(h,p)=-\Delta\big(1-\Psi(p_h)\big)\;\le\;0,\qquad\qquad \nabla_pW(\ell,p)=\frac{v_\ell\Delta}{v_h}\,\Psi(p_h)\;\ge\;0 .$$

(The first is the identity quoted in §3b, at $(v_h,v_\ell)=(3,1)$.) High negative, Low positive — the fixed-sign channel, obtained with no cone machinery because in one dimension there is nothing for the machinery to do. Both types are extreme when $K=2$, so the argument covers the whole type space and the binary story is complete.

Specialization II: $K=3$ — where the content is

Now the tangent space is a plane; write $\nabla_pW(v,p)=\big(\partial W/\partial p_h,\ \partial W/\partial p_\ell\big)$ in the coordinates $(p_h,p_\ell)$. The cone $\mathcal{C}$ is the quadrant $(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-)\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+)$, and its polar is $$\mathcal{C}^{\circ}=\mathbb{R}_{-}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{\partial W}{\partial p_h}\le0,\ \ \frac{\partial W}{\partial p_\ell}\ge0 .$$ The gradient is available in closed form. The derivation is six short steps, and worth following in full: each step cashes in a result established earlier in this note.

  1. From the opponent's posterior to the opponent's quantile. Along the equilibrium the opponent finalist's posterior is a function of their bid quantile, $q=P(s)$ with $s\in[0,1]$, and by §6a their quantile carries density $2s\,ds$. So $(W)$ reads $$W(v,p)=\int_0^1 \Pi_2\big(v;\,p,\,P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds .$$ Here $p$ is free: $\Pi_2(v;p,q)$ is defined for any pair of posteriors and $\Psi$ is a fixed measure over the opponent's $q$, so $W(v,p)$ is a genuine function of $p$ on the simplex and $\nabla_p$ has something to act on.
  2. Partition the opponent's quantile axis by the regime the pair lands in. Fix the own quantile $t$, so $p=P(t)$. As $s$ runs $0\to1$ the pair $\big(P(t),P(s)\big)$ travels through the Round-2 complex, staying inside one regime except at finitely many boundary-crossings $$0=s_0(t)<s_1(t)<\cdots<s_{J(t)}(t)=1 .$$ On the open segment $I_j(t)=\big(s_{j-1}(t),s_j(t)\big)$ the pair sits in a single regime $c_j(t)$ of the complex, so there $\Pi_2$ equals that regime's affine form, written $\Pi_2^{[c_j(t)]}$. Thus the superscript index $j=1,\dots,J(t)$ enumerates the segments — equivalently the ordered list of regimes the opponent's trajectory visits as $s$ increases — and $$W\big(v,P(t)\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{J(t)}\int_{s_{j-1}(t)}^{s_j(t)}\Pi_2^{[c_j(t)]}\big(v;\,P(t),\,P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds .$$ Generically $J(t)=2$, split at the diagonal $s_1=t$ (own ahead of the opponent for $s<t$, own behind for $s>t$). The exact census (census_exact_curve.py) shows the on-path trajectory also grazes two thin extra regimes near the ends of $[0,1]$; these are carried explicitly, not dropped (step 6 and the segment table below).

summary of the logic K=2 vs K=3

Monotonicity (M) confines the velocity to a fixed cone $\mathcal{C}$. The Round-2 economics of §3c confines the gradient to the polar cone $\mathcal{C}^\circ$ (High) or to $-\mathcal{C}^\circ$ (Low). Duality of the two cones fixes the sign of their pairing at every bid, with no reference to a direction and no magnitudes. At $K=2$ the cones are half-lines and the whole thing collapses to $\nabla_pW(h,p)<0$, $\nabla_pW(\ell,p)>0$; at $K=3$ it delivers the two extreme types and stops.

What this does and does not deliver. A constant-sign channel for the extreme types is the single-crossing input an interval-support argument wants, and it is consistent with the finding (§6e) that each support is one connected interval $[0,\beta_v]$. It is not by itself a proof that the supports are intervals: for $K=3$ that would additionally require a verdict for Mid, which the cone argument does not supply. For $K=2$, where both types are extreme, the corresponding gap does not arise — another reason the binary case reads as easier than it is. Treat "supports are single intervals" as strongly indicated by the fixed-sign channel at the extremes together with the solved curve, not as established by §3a alone.

3b. The prize is smooth, even though $\Pi_2$ is kinked

The Round-2 payoff $\Pi_2$ is full of kinks (the $\min(p_h,q_h)$, the $(p_h-q_h)^+$). One might fear those kinks make the indifference ODE ill-posed. They do not — because the prize is an average:

key general K $$W(v,p)=\int \Pi_2(v;p,q)\,d\Psi(q)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad W(v,\cdot)\ \text{is}\ C^1,\quad \nabla_p W=\Psi\big|_{\text{kink locus}}.$$

Integrating a kinked function against an atomless $\Psi$ smooths the kinks away: the kink locus (the set of opponent posteriors $q$ where $\Pi_2$ bends — e.g. the diagonal $p=q$) has $\Psi$-measure zero, so it contributes nothing to the average; the kink cancels and only its slope survives, which is the content of $\nabla_p W=\Psi|_{\text{kink locus}}$ (an envelope / Leibniz computation). This matches the $K=2$ identity $\nabla_pW(h,p)=-2\big(1-\Psi(p_h)\big)$ with $\Psi=G^2$. The kinks of $\Pi_2$ never reach the bidder. So $(\star_v)$ is a genuine, well-posed first-order ODE.

4. The mirror symmetry

In the pipeline: the own-vs-opponent symmetry of the Round-2 payoff, used throughout the solve.

K=3 — uses the specific 10-regime Round-2 complex and its regime forms

needed for the solve

The two groups A and B are interchangeable. Let $\tau$ be the involution that swaps the two finalist roles (own $\leftrightarrow$ opponent, A $\leftrightarrow$ B). On Round-2 regime words it flips every $\mathtt{A}\leftrightarrow\mathtt{B}$.

Under $\tau$, the two dominant Round-2 regimes swap into each other (and the thin extra pair the exact census adds, BBAAA ↔ AABBB, is likewise a $\tau$-pair):

regime 1  [B,A,B,A,A]   $\xrightarrow{\ \tau\ }$   regime 2  [A,B,A,B,B]

The diagonal $p=q$ (own posterior equals opponent posterior) is the fixed locus of $\tau$. In a symmetric equilibrium, both finalists draw their posterior from the same distribution $\Psi$, so the whole configuration is invariant under $\tau$. Three consequences:

  • the equilibrium payoff is invariant under own $\leftrightarrow$ opponent;
  • regimes 1 and 2 carry the same payoff form, related by the swap:
    regime$\Pi_2(h;p,q)$  (own aggregate posterior $A2{=}p_m{+}p_h$, $A3{=}p_h$; opponent $B2{=}q_m{+}q_h$, $B3{=}q_h$ — so this row equals the §6b regime-1/regime-2 High entry.)
    regime 1$2 - B2 - \tfrac13 A3 - \tfrac23 B3$
    regime 2$2 - A2 - \tfrac23 A3 - \tfrac13 B3$

Why it matters for solving. We can still solve on one side of the diagonal and reflect — the configuration is $\tau$-invariant, so gluing across $p=q$ is legitimate exactly as in the binary case. Each side is not a single regime: the exact census puts a thin extra segment on each side (the $\texttt{BBAAA}/\texttt{AABBB}$ pair). So the reduction is to a mirror-symmetric piecewise payoff in one own-vs-opponent variable — dominantly the regime-1/regime-2 pair, with two $\approx1.2\%$-weight segments at the two ends. It remains the single biggest simplification available, legitimate because the equilibrium is symmetric and stays off the corners.

5. The obstruction

In the pipeline: why naive integration fails — the fixed point that fact (F2) dissolves.

main focus general K

The trouble is not the kinks (smoothed away, §3b) and not monotonicity (clean, §3a). It is that the prize and the belief are the same unknown.

Look again at what the prize is built from:

$$p_v(b)=\frac{f_v\,g_v(b)}{\sum_u f_u\,g_u(b)}\quad\text{and}\quad \Psi=\big(\text{pushforward of }G\text{ through }p(\cdot)\big).$$

Both the posterior $p$ and the opponent-posterior law $\Psi$ are made out of the very densities $g_v$ that the indifference ODE $(\star_v)$ is trying to solve for — and the pairwise indifference conditions $(\star_{uv})$ inherit the same circularity. So $(\star_v)$, and with it $(\star_{uv})$, is a fixed-point ODE, not a quadrature: you cannot integrate $W$ as if it were an exogenous prize, because $W$ is defined through the unknown strategy. This circularity is the structural signature of a signaling equilibrium: the interim payoff $G(b)\,W(v,p(b))-b$ looks like an ordinary all-pay objective, but the prize $W$ silently depends on the very strategy being chosen.

How the binary case escaped

K=2

In the binary case the continuation turned out to be strategy-independent: $\Psi=G^2$ pinned $W$ independently of the strategy. The fixed point dissolved, leaving the roadmap's §7 separable ODE — restated in this note's general notation in §6′c — with a first integral and a single scalar

$$t_0=0.2221\quad\text{pinned by}\quad \int_0^{1}P_h\,dt=\tfrac12,\qquad U_h=k_{h\ell}=2t_0,\quad U_\ell=0.$$

The entire solvability of the binary model rested on that one fact. (The roadmap's §7 writes $G_0$ for this atom quantile; here it is $t_0=G(0)$, matching §6a and keeping $G$-subscripts free for the type CDFs $G_v$.)

the question at $K\ge3$ — and the answer K=3

Does strategy-independence survive with more types? Yes — and this owes nothing to the regime count. In $t=G(b)$ a finalist's opponent is a Round-1 winner, so $\Psi(t)=t^2$ (§6a) whatever regimes the payoff visits; $\nabla_p W$ enters $(\star_v)$ only as $\Psi$-weights on the visited regimes' constant gradients (the $\sum_j$ form $(\nabla)$ of §3a). The prize is pinned by the curve alone, and the supports close in the binary form: a few scalars pinned by moment conditions like $\int p\,d\Psi=\text{prior}$, each type's support the interval $[0,\beta_v]$. The full curve — one $\operatorname{arctanh}$ branch on each side of the single kink at $t^\ast$ — is in §6.

Read from the other side, $(\omega)$ at $\omega^{-1}(t)$ says $P_h\big(\omega^{-1}(t)\big)=P_h(t)+\tfrac32P_m(t)$: the far-behind threshold is the same boundary solved for the other coordinate, which is why the two extra segments of §3a are governed by one function and its inverse. It is not. The boundary first-integrates: differentiate $P_h\big(\omega^{-1}(t)\big)=\Phi(t)$ with $\Phi:=P_h+\tfrac32P_m$ and substitute both ODEs — the symmetric factor $(3-t^2-(\omega^{-1})^2)$ cancels, leaving $t_0(\omega^{-1})'/(\omega^{-1})^2=k_{m\ell}/t^2$, i.e. $t_0/\omega^{-1}-k_{m\ell}/t=c$ constant (lem:wallfirstint). So $\omega^{-1}$ is a Möbius map for every $c$ — and $c=0$ (thm:linwall), so the boundary is linear: $\omega^{-1}(t)=t/t^{\ast\ast}$, $\omega(t)=t\,t^{\ast\ast}$ with $t^{\ast\ast}=k_{m\ell}/t_0=0.4350938650$ and $t^\ast t^{\ast\ast}=t_0$. 6′e. Support structure
