Round-1 Support Structure

Why IPV gives simple supports — the monotone posterior, the mirror symmetry, and the signaling obstruction

Pedagogical companion to round1_support_structure_handoff.md. 4-player, 2-round all-pay elimination contest; types $v\in\{L,M,H\}=\{1,2,3\}$, uniform prior.

Round 1 looks like an ordinary all-pay contest, but it hides a twist: the prize you win for advancing depends on how you bid, because your bid is a signal of your type. This note is the pedagogical entry point to the model and its current results. It explains how the equilibrium is found by guess and verify (the pipeline below), what the twist does to the bid supports — the private-values (IPV) structure makes them simple single intervals, unlike Turocy's multi-interval supports — where the naive analysis gets stuck (§5), and how the obstruction is resolved (§6): the continuation prize is strategy-independent, and the equilibrium posterior curve is elementary in closed form on the whole support — an $\operatorname{arctanh}$ branch on each side of a single kink at $t^\ast$ — with one short band near the top where only the Mid/Low split lacks an elementary form.

The big picture — guess and verify

The whole pipeline on one page; §1–§6 fill in the details.

The model. Four players, two rounds. Types $v\in\{L,M,H\}=\{1,2,3\}$ are i.i.d., uniform prior. Round 1 is two pairwise all-pay auctions (higher bid advances); all Round-1 bids are then publicly revealed, and the two winners meet in an all-pay final worth $v$ to its winner. Because bids are revealed, a finalist's Round-1 bid is a signal of their type, and Zhang & Wang (2009) rule out any symmetric separating equilibrium. So we look for a non-separating equilibrium, and we find it by guess and verify: guess the support architecture and a monotonicity property, solve exactly under that guess, then check every assumption on the solved object.

Two facts that cost nothing

Two structural facts hold in any symmetric SPNE, before anything is guessed.

  1. (F1) The final is an IPV all-pay auction à la Siegel, with priors = the Round-1 posteriors. Types are i.i.d. and Round-1 mixing is independent across players, so conditional on the revealed bids the finalists' types are independent. The final is therefore an asymmetric-prior independent-private-values all-pay auction, solved exactly by Siegel's construction: its payoff $\Pi_2(v;p,q)$ is piecewise affine in the two finalists' posteriors $p,q$, with 10 linear regimes at $K{=}3$ (§1, §6b). Round 2 is completely known as a function of beliefs; all the difficulty lives in Round 1.
  2. (F2) The opponent finalist's quantile law is strategy-free: $\Psi(t)=t^2$. Work in the bid quantile $t=G(b)$. Your final-round opponent is a Round-1 winner, so their quantile is uniform size-biased by the win probability — density $2s$, CDF $t^2$ — whatever the equilibrium strategy is (§6a). This is the hinge of the whole solution (the working notes call it $\sigma$-invariance): it makes the continuation prize a functional of the posterior curve alone, dissolving the fixed point that blocks the naive approach (§5).

The guess

  1. (G1) Support architecture. A mass point at bid $0$ played by Low and Mid (quantile weight $t_0$); above it, one common mixing interval — Low mixes up to $\beta_L$, Mid and High to the very top — and no pure-High tail.
  2. (G2) Monotone posterior. The posterior $p(b)$ is FOSD-increasing in the bid: $p_H$ rises, $p_L$ falls. This is a maintained hypothesis, not a theorem — assumed going in on deep-learning evidence, verified ex post on the solved curve (§3).
  3. (G3) Regime census. On the equilibrium path the posterior pair $(p,q)$ visits only 4 of the 10 Round-2 regimes; which regimes, and over which segments of the opponent-quantile axis, is fixed by the census (§3a) and re-checked after solving.

The solve

  1. Change of variables $t=G(b)$. Trade the bid for its quantile $t\in[0,1]$; the bid-$0$ mass point spreads over $[0,t_0]$ via the tie-break lottery. The unknown becomes the posterior curve $P(t)=(\ell,m,h)(t)$; the bid is recovered afterwards from indifference itself, $b(t)=t\,W(v,P(t))-U_v$ for any active type $v$.
  2. The prize becomes a functional of the curve. By (F1)+(F2), $W(v,P(t))=\int_0^1 \Pi_2\big(v;P(t),P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds$ — the strategy no longer appears, so $(\star_v)$ is a genuine ODE, not a fixed-point equation (§6a–§6c).
  3. Pairwise indifference integrates to first integrals. Where the types mix together, $(\star_{HL})$ and $(\star_{ML})$ integrate to $t\,D_{HL}=k_1=U_H$ and $t\,D_{ML}=k_2=U_M$. Their difference is $m$-free, giving a scalar ODE for $h$: an $\operatorname{arctanh}$ closed form on $[t_0,t^\ast]$ and the same form (with $\sqrt3\mapsto\sqrt3/\kappa$) above, plus the payoff-gap identity $k_1-k_2=t_0$ (§6c, §6f).
  4. Bayes consistency closes the system. The posterior curve must average back to the prior: $\int_0^1 h\,dt=\tfrac13$ and $t_0m_0+\int_{t_0}^1 m\,dt=\tfrac13$. These pin the free constants $t_0,m_0$ — and with them $k_1,k_2,t^\ast,\beta_L$ (§6d).

The verify

Every guessed ingredient is then checked on the solved object: the first-integral residuals hold uniformly to $1.3\times10^{-7}$ (the gate, §6d); the posterior is FOSD-increasing ex post (G2 ✓); Low strictly prefers to stay out above $\beta_L$ ($U_L<0$ there, so the exclusion is admissible, §6f); the on-path regime census matches (G3 ✓). What this does not address: uniqueness — the construction delivers an equilibrium, verified, not proved unique. The claim-by-claim proved/machine-checked ledger is docs/STATE.md + docs/proofs.tex.

The open gate below — does the continuation stay strategy-independent with three types? — is now closed. 1. The object: a signaling all-pay auction

In the pipeline: defines the objects — $G$, the posterior $p(b)$, the prize $W$, the interim payoff (U).

general K

Fix a player of type $v$ among $K$ types. Within a group, two players bid in an all-pay auction; the higher bid advances to the final. Write

  • $H_v(b)$ — the CDF of type $v$'s Round-1 bid, with density $h_v(b)$;
  • the chance of winning Round 1 is the rival's bid CDF, $G(b)=\sum_u f_u\,H_u(b)$, where $f_u$ is the prior probability of type $u$; the profile of densities $\sigma=(h_v)_{v=1}^K$ is the Round-1 strategy we solve for;
  • the bid is a signal: winning with bid $b$ induces a posterior over your own type, $$p_v(b)=\frac{f_v\,h_v(b)}{\sum_u f_u\,h_u(b)}\qquad\text{\small(post)}$$ a point in the type simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$ ($K{-}1$ free coordinates, since $\sum_v p_v=1$); K=3 with the uniform prior used from §2 on, $f_v\equiv\tfrac13$ cancels in (post) and the simplex is $\Delta^2$;
  • the continuation value of reaching the final as type $v$ with posterior $p$ (the belief vector $p=(p_v)_{v=1}^K$ your winning bid induced; the working notes call it the reputation) is $$W(v,p)=\int \Pi_2\!\big(v;\,p,\,q\big)\,d\Psi(q),\qquad\text{\small(W)}$$ where $q$ is the opponent finalist's posterior, $\Psi$ its distribution, and $\Pi_2$ the Round-2 payoff; K=3 $\Pi_2$ is piecewise-affine over a 10-regime complex (derived in docs/tropical_cell_structure.html / memory three-type-r2-tropical).

The interim Round-1 payoff of bidding $b$ as type $v$ is

$$U_v(b)\;=\;\underbrace{G(b)}_{\text{win prob}}\cdot\underbrace{W\big(v,\,p(b)\big)}_{\text{prize}}\;-\;\underbrace{b}_{\text{all-pay cost}}.\qquad\text{\small(U)}$$
the twist general K

The prize $W(v,p(b))$ depends on your own bid through the belief $p(b)$. This is not a Siegel contest, where the prize is a fixed number. It is a signaling all-pay auction — the genus of Hörner & Sahuguet, Costly Signalling in Auctions (2007). Siegel's clean support theory does not apply off the shelf; the question is whether IPV restores enough structure to recover his conclusions anyway.

2. The indifference conditions

In the pipeline: the equations the solve integrates (solve step 3).

general K

Where a type mixes, every bid in its support must give the same payoff: $U_v(b)\equiv\text{const}$. Differentiating in $b$ splits the marginal incentive into two channels:

$$\underbrace{G'(b)\,W\big(v,p(b)\big)}_{\text{win-probability channel}}\;+\;\underbrace{G(b)\,\nabla_p W\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b)}_{\text{signaling channel}}\;=\;1.\qquad\text{\small($\star_v$)}$$

Subtracting one type's condition from another cancels the $1$ and leaves the pairwise indifference (single-crossing) conditions $(\star_{vv'})$ — the difference of $(\star_v)$ and $(\star_{v'})$, one for each active pair:

$$G'(b)\,\big[W(v,p)-W(v',p)\big]\;+\;G(b)\,\big[\nabla_p W(v,p)-\nabla_p W(v',p)\big]\cdot p'(b)\;=\;0.\qquad\text{\small($\star_{vv'}$)}$$

K=2 there is a single pair, and the single-crossing fact is $\nabla_p\big[W(H,p)-W(L,p)\big]<0$ (roadmap §7). K=3 the active pairs are $(\star_{HL})$ and $(\star_{ML})$; the entire §6 solve is these two instances integrated. Everything below is about understanding the two ingredients in $(\star_v)$: the signaling channel (governed by the monotone posterior) and the fact that $W$ is the same unknown as the strategy (the obstruction).

3. The monotone posterior — what IPV buys

In the pipeline: the guess (G2) and the reductions it buys, including the regime census (G3).

the guess (G2) — its status K=3 — DL evidence, verified ex post

The monotone posterior is a maintained hypothesis, not a theorem: we assume the posterior is FOSD-increasing in the bid, solve under that assumption, and check it on the solved curve afterwards. The evidence going in is the deep-learning Round-1 run — as the bid rises, $p_H(b)$ climbs $0.05\to0.65$ and $p_L(b)$ falls $0.59\to0.10$ (the middle share $p_M(b)$ pinned by $p_L{+}p_M{+}p_H{=}1$) — and the solved curve of §6 confirms it ex post ($h$ rises $0\to0.769$, the Low share falls $0.588\to0$). High bids signal high types. This single fact drives three reductions.

3a. The signaling channel has a fixed sign

general K, specialized to K=2 and K=3 below

Fix a bid $b$ and look at the second term of $(\star_v)$, the signaling channel $$\nabla_p W\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b).$$ We want conditions under which this has a sign that does not change as $b$ sweeps the support. Two ingredients enter: the velocity $p'(b)$ of the belief path, and the gradient $\nabla_pW(v,\cdot)$ of the prize. Monotonicity of the posterior constrains the first; the Round-2 economics constrains the second. The argument below is stated for a general number of types $K$, then specialized to $K=2$ (where it degenerates to something already familiar) and to $K=3$ (where it has real content).

The state space, and why its dimension is the whole story

With $K$ types the posterior $p=(p_v)_{v=1}^K$ lives in the simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$, so $\sum_v p_v=1$ and the velocity satisfies $\sum_v p_v'=0$. The tangent space is therefore of dimension $K-1$:

typesposteriortangent spaceunit directions available
$K=2$$p_H$, with $p_L=1-p_H$ eliminated$\dim=1$ — a lineexactly two: $\pm1$
$K=3$$(p_H,p_L)$, with $p_M=1-p_H-p_L$ eliminated$\dim=2$ — a planea whole circle

This dimension count is what separates the two cases below.

what monotonicity actually gives general K

The monotone posterior of §3 says the High share rises and the Low share falls with the bid: $$p_H'(b)>0,\qquad p_L'(b)<0\qquad\text{throughout the mixing region.}\tag{M}$$ This is a statement about the signs of the components, i.e. about which orthant the velocity occupies. Equivalently: there is a fixed convex cone $\mathcal{C}$ in the tangent space with $$p'(b)\in\mathcal{C}\quad\text{for every }b.$$ K=3 $\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-)\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+)$, an open quadrant. K=2 only the first inequality survives and $\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-$, an open half-line. Note what (M) does not say: nothing about the ratio of the components, hence nothing about where inside $\mathcal{C}$ the velocity points.

The principle: two cones in duality

general K

Write the channel as an inner product in the tangent space and recall the definition of the polar (dual) cone, $$\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\;=\;\{z:\ \langle z,x\rangle\le 0\ \ \text{for all }x\in\mathcal{C}\}.$$

the correct principle — any $K$ $$\left.\begin{aligned}p'(b)&\in\mathcal{C}\\ \nabla_pW(v,p(b))&\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\end{aligned}\right\}\ \text{for all }b \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \nabla_pW(v,p(b))\cdot p'(b)<0\ \text{ for all }b,$$

and symmetrically $\nabla_pW\in\operatorname{int}(-\mathcal{C}^{\circ})$ gives a strictly positive channel — immediate from the definition of the polar cone. The hypotheses are membership in the two orthants only: no constancy of the direction $p'/|p'|$, no magnitude bound on either vector. Smoothness of $W$ (§3b) is what makes $\nabla_pW$ exist so that it can be placed in a cone at all.

Specialization I: $K=2$ — the degenerate case

The tangent space is a line, so $p'(b)$ and $\nabla_pW(v,p)$ are scalars and the channel is a product of two numbers, $$\nabla_pW\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b),\qquad p'(b)>0 .$$ Its sign is simply the sign of $\nabla_pW(v,p)$, and the cone condition $\nabla_pW\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{C}^\circ=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+$ is the statement $\nabla_pW(v,p)<0$. The solved binary case supplies both signs explicitly (roadmap §7, with $\Psi=G^2$ and $\Delta=v_H-v_L$):

$$\nabla_pW(H,p)=-\Delta\big(1-\Psi(p_H)\big)\;\le\;0,\qquad\qquad \nabla_pW(L,p)=\frac{v_L\Delta}{v_H}\,\Psi(p_H)\;\ge\;0 .$$

(The first is the identity quoted in §3b, at $(v_H,v_L)=(3,1)$.) High negative, Low positive — the fixed-sign channel, obtained with no cone machinery because in one dimension there is nothing for the machinery to do. Both types are extreme when $K=2$, so the argument covers the whole type space and the binary story is complete.

Specialization II: $K=3$ — where the content is

Now the tangent space is a plane; write $\nabla_pW(v,p)=\big(\partial W/\partial p_H,\ \partial W/\partial p_L\big)$ in the coordinates $(p_H,p_L)$. The cone $\mathcal{C}$ is the quadrant $(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-)\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+)$, and its polar is $$\mathcal{C}^{\circ}=\mathbb{R}_{-}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{\partial W}{\partial p_H}\le0,\ \ \frac{\partial W}{\partial p_L}\ge0 .$$ The gradient is available in closed form. The derivation is six short steps, and worth following in full: each step cashes in a result established earlier in this note.

  1. From the opponent's posterior to the opponent's quantile. Along the equilibrium the opponent finalist's posterior is a function of their bid quantile, $q=P(s)$ with $s\in[0,1]$, and by §6a their quantile carries density $2s\,ds$. So $(W)$ reads $$W(v,p)=\int_0^1 \Pi_2\big(v;\,p,\,P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds .$$ Here $p$ is free: $\Pi_2(v;p,q)$ is defined for any pair of posteriors and $\Psi$ is a fixed measure over the opponent's $q$, so $W(v,p)$ is a genuine function of $p$ on the simplex and $\nabla_p$ has something to act on.
  2. Partition the opponent's quantile axis by the regime the pair lands in. Fix the own quantile $t$, so $p=P(t)$. As $s$ runs $0\to1$ the pair $\big(P(t),P(s)\big)$ travels through the Round-2 complex, staying inside one regime except at finitely many boundary-crossings $$0=s_0(t)<s_1(t)<\cdots<s_{J(t)}(t)=1 .$$ On the open segment $I_j(t)=\big(s_{j-1}(t),s_j(t)\big)$ the pair sits in a single regime $c_j(t)$ of the complex, so there $\Pi_2$ equals that regime's affine form, written $\Pi_2^{[c_j(t)]}$. Thus the superscript index $j=1,\dots,J(t)$ enumerates the segments — equivalently the ordered list of regimes the opponent's trajectory visits as $s$ increases — and $$W\big(v,P(t)\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{J(t)}\int_{s_{j-1}(t)}^{s_j(t)}\Pi_2^{[c_j(t)]}\big(v;\,P(t),\,P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds .$$ Generically $J(t)=2$, split at the diagonal $s_1=t$ (own ahead of the opponent for $s<t$, own behind for $s>t$). The exact census (census_exact_curve.py) shows the on-path trajectory also grazes two thin extra regimes near the ends of $[0,1]$; these are carried explicitly, not dropped (step 6 and the segment table below).
  3. Differentiate — Leibniz gives interior parts and moving-boundary parts. Moving $p$ (equivalently $t$) both moves each integrand and slides every boundary $s_j(t)$. Leibniz across all $J(t)$ segments: $$\nabla_pW=\sum_{j=1}^{J(t)}\int_{s_{j-1}}^{s_j}\nabla_p\Pi_2^{[c_j]}\,2s\,ds\;+\;\sum_{j=1}^{J(t)-1}\Big[\,\Pi_2^{[c_j]}-\Pi_2^{[c_{j+1}]}\,\Big]_{s=s_j(t)}\,2\,s_j(t)\,\partial_ps_j(t) .$$
  4. Every moving-boundary term vanishes. $\Pi_2$ is continuous across each regime boundary (piecewise-affine — the slope bends, the value does not), so at $s=s_j$ the two adjacent forms agree and each bracket $\big[\Pi_2^{[c_j]}-\Pi_2^{[c_{j+1}]}\big]$ is zero. Nothing need be known about $\partial_ps_j$ — which is what makes the computation possible. At the diagonal boundary $q=p$, for instance, regimes 1 (BABAA) and 2 (ABABB) match in every row:

On a regime $\Pi_2$ is affine in the own posterior (§6b), so $$g_v^{[c]}\;:=\;\nabla_p\,\Pi_2\big(v;\,p,q\big)\quad\text{for any }(p,q)\in\text{regime }c$$ is a well-defined constant vector — independent of $p$, of the opponent's $q$, and hence of $s$. the gradient — one term per visited regime exact, any $K$ $$\nabla_p W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{J(t)}\psi_j(t)\,g_v^{[c_j(t)]},\tag{$\nabla$}$$ $$\text{where}\qquad \psi_j(t)\;:=\;s_j(t)^2-s_{j-1}(t)^2>0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{J(t)}\psi_j(t)\;=\;1.$$

Read the pieces off the two tables above: $j$ indexes the segments of the opponent axis (step 2), $c_j(t)$ is the regime that holds on segment $j$, $g_v^{[c_j(t)]}$ is that regime's constant own-gradient (step 5), and $\psi_j(t)$ is the $\Psi$-mass of the segment (step 6). So the marginal value of posterior is a $\Psi$-weighted average of the visited regimes' constant own-gradients. The opponent's posterior curve $s\mapsto P(s)$ enters only through the segment endpoints $s_j(t)$, never through the slopes $g_v^{[c_j]}$. This is the whole gradient — there is no dropped term.

At each end one extra regime enters and shifts the gradient — but never across a cone boundary, so the verdicts hold on all of $[t_0,1]$ under the four-regime census. Top ($t>t^\ast$): the BBAAA boundary moves $g_H$ alone (Mid and Low unchanged), taking High to $\big(\partial W/\partial p_H,\,\partial W/\partial p_L\big)=\big(\tfrac{t^2+\omega^2-2}{3},\,1-t^2\big)$ — first entry still $<0$ since $t^2+\omega^2<2$, so still in $\mathcal{C}^\circ$. Bottom ($t_0\le t<t^{\ast\ast}$): the AABBB boundary moves $g_H$ and $g_M$ by the same vector (so $D_{HM}=g_H-g_M$ is untouched), taking High to $\big(\tfrac{t^2-2}{3}+\tfrac{1-(\omega^{-1})^2}{6},\,(1-t^2)+\tfrac{1-(\omega^{-1})^2}{2}\big)\in\mathcal{C}^\circ$ and Mid to $\big(\tfrac13+\tfrac{1-(\omega^{-1})^2}{6},\,(1-t^2)+\tfrac{1-(\omega^{-1})^2}{2}\big)$, still in neither cone; Low is unchanged in $-\mathcal{C}^\circ$ on every band. No sign flips — the three verdicts are exact under the full complex.

  • High — strictly negative, uniformly. $\partial W/\partial p_H<0$ for all $t\in[t_0,1]$ (middle band $\tfrac{t^2-2}{3}\in[-\tfrac23,-\tfrac13]$; the ends shift the value but not its sign, per the note above) and $\partial W/\partial p_L=1-t^2\ge0$. Both terms of the inner product are $\le0$ with the first strict. The effects reinforce: bidding higher makes you look more High (which hurts) and less Low (which also hurts, since looking Low is what a High type wants). Compare $K=2$, where $\nabla_pW(H,p)=-\Delta(1-\Psi)$: same sign, and only this first column is visible there.
  • Low — strictly positive. $\partial W/\partial p_H=\tfrac{t^2}{6}>0$ and $\partial W/\partial p_L=-\tfrac{t^2}{2}<0$, the exact mirror pattern; both terms positive, again reinforcing. Compare $K=2$, where $\nabla_pW(L,p)=\tfrac{v_L\Delta}{v_H}\Psi\ge0$.
  • Mid — genuinely ambiguous, with no binary analogue. $\partial W/\partial p_H=\tfrac13>0$ but $\partial W/\partial p_L=1-t^2\ge0$ as well, so the gradient lies in neither $\mathcal{C}^\circ$ nor $-\mathcal{C}^\circ$. The channel is $\tfrac13p_H'+(1-t^2)p_L'$ — a positive term plus a negative one, whose sign turns on the ratio $|p_L'|/p_H'$ and on $t$. No sign-chasing rescues this; one needs the magnitudes, i.e. the solved curve of §6. This is the first structural feature with no counterpart at $K=2$, where every type is extreme.
summary of the logic K=2 vs K=3

Monotonicity (M) confines the velocity to a fixed cone $\mathcal{C}$. The Round-2 economics of §3c confines the gradient to the polar cone $\mathcal{C}^\circ$ (High) or to $-\mathcal{C}^\circ$ (Low). Duality of the two cones fixes the sign of their pairing at every bid, with no reference to a direction and no magnitudes. At $K=2$ the cones are half-lines and the whole thing collapses to $\nabla_pW(H,p)<0$, $\nabla_pW(L,p)>0$; at $K=3$ it delivers the two extreme types and stops.

What this does and does not deliver. A constant-sign channel for the extreme types is the single-crossing input an interval-support argument wants, and it is consistent with the finding (§6e) that each support is one connected interval $[0,\beta_v]$. It is not by itself a proof that the supports are intervals: for $K=3$ that would additionally require a verdict for Mid, which the cone argument does not supply. For $K=2$, where both types are extreme, the corresponding gap does not arise — another reason the binary case reads as easier than it is. Treat "supports are single intervals" as strongly indicated by the fixed-sign channel at the extremes together with the solved curve, not as established by §3a alone.

3b. The prize is smooth, even though $\Pi_2$ is kinked

The Round-2 payoff $\Pi_2$ is full of kinks (the $\min(p_H,q_H)$, the $(p_H-q_H)^+$). One might fear those kinks make the indifference ODE ill-posed. They do not — because the prize is an average:

key general K $$W(v,p)=\int \Pi_2(v;p,q)\,d\Psi(q)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad W(v,\cdot)\ \text{is}\ C^1,\quad \nabla_p W=\Psi\big|_{\text{kink locus}}.$$

Integrating a kinked function against an atomless $\Psi$ smooths the kinks away: the kink locus (the set of opponent posteriors $q$ where $\Pi_2$ bends — e.g. the diagonal $p=q$) has $\Psi$-measure zero, so it contributes nothing to the average; the kink cancels and only its slope survives, which is the content of $\nabla_p W=\Psi|_{\text{kink locus}}$ (an envelope / Leibniz computation). This matches the $K=2$ identity $\nabla_pW(H,p)=-2\big(1-\Psi(p_H)\big)$ with $\Psi=G^2$. The kinks of $\Pi_2$ never reach the bidder. So $(\star_v)$ is a genuine, well-posed first-order ODE.

3c. The sign is type-dependent — the engine of "hide and bluff"

general K mechanism (extreme types); illustrated at K=3

Here the signaling channel earns its name. Being perceived as High hurts the High type ($\partial W/\partial p_H<0$ at $v=H$): a strong player would rather be underestimated, so the finalist across the table under-prepares. Therefore:

TypeWin-prob channel as $b\uparrow$Signaling channel as $b\uparrow$Behavior
Highprize-weight rises ($G'\!>0$)prize falls (being seen High hurts)channels conflict → hides (bids low)
Lowprize-weight risesprize rises (being seen High helps)channels align → bluffs (bids high)

This is the same mechanism that makes binary bidding non-monotone in realizations, now spread across three types.

why supports are single intervals (the Siegel point) general K argument

Monotone $p(b)$ + atomless $\Psi$ make the $(\star_v)$ coefficients monotone and single-crossing in $v$ — precisely the hypothesis an interval-support argument needs. So each type's support is one connected interval $[0,\beta_v]$ in bid space, with a common floor $b_{lo}=0$. K=3, DL nested ceilings $\beta_H>\beta_M>\beta_L$ (approximate: $0.69>0.62>0.54$; atoms at $0$ grow as type falls: Low $0.49$, Mid $0.30$, High $\approx0$). Symbol caution: §6 works in the quantile coordinate $t=G(b)$ and writes $\beta_L=0.9734$ for the same Low-exit event — the $t$-coordinate of this bid ceiling, not a different number. Bid-space $\beta_L\approx0.54$ and $t$-space $\beta_L=0.9734$ are one exit point in two coordinates.

Contrast with Turocy. Turocy's rich multi-interval / gapped supports need a non-monotone or non-single-crossing prize (correlation / common value). IPV with atomless $\Psi$ rules that out. IPV is exactly what collapses the support to a single interval.

4. The mirror symmetry

In the pipeline: the own-vs-opponent symmetry of the Round-2 payoff, used throughout the solve.

K=3 — uses the specific 10-regime Round-2 complex and its regime forms

needed for the solve

The two groups A and B are interchangeable. Let $\tau$ be the involution that swaps the two finalist roles (own $\leftrightarrow$ opponent, A $\leftrightarrow$ B). On Round-2 regime words it flips every $\mathtt{A}\leftrightarrow\mathtt{B}$.

Under $\tau$, the two dominant Round-2 regimes swap into each other (and the thin extra pair the exact census adds, BBAAA ↔ AABBB, is likewise a $\tau$-pair):

regime 1  [B,A,B,A,A]   $\xrightarrow{\ \tau\ }$   regime 2  [A,B,A,B,B]

The diagonal $p=q$ (own posterior equals opponent posterior) is the fixed locus of $\tau$. In a symmetric equilibrium, both finalists draw their posterior from the same distribution $\Psi$, so the whole configuration is invariant under $\tau$. Three consequences:

  • the equilibrium payoff is invariant under own $\leftrightarrow$ opponent;
  • regimes 1 and 2 carry the same payoff form, related by the swap:
    regime$\Pi_2(H;p,q)$  (own aggregate posterior $A2{=}p_M{+}p_H$, $A3{=}p_H$; opponent $B2{=}q_M{+}q_H$, $B3{=}q_H$. In the §6b shares $(m,h)/(mp,hp)$: $A2{=}m{+}h$, $A3{=}h$, $B2{=}mp{+}hp$, $B3{=}hp$ — so this row equals the §6b regime-1/regime-2 High entry.)
    regime 1$2 - B2 - \tfrac13 A3 - \tfrac23 B3$
    regime 2$2 - A2 - \tfrac23 A3 - \tfrac13 B3$
  • the exact census visits four interior regimes: the dominant pair $\texttt{BABAA}/\texttt{ABABB}$ (regimes 1/2, $\approx34\%$ each) plus a thin mirror pair $\texttt{BBAAA}/\texttt{AABBB}$ ($\approx1.2\%$ each — own far ahead vs. opponent just above the atom, and its reflection), together with the diagonal boundary ($\approx14\%$); the lopsided corner regimes 9,10 are never visited — posteriors never get extreme.

Why it matters for solving. We can still solve on one side of the diagonal and reflect — the configuration is $\tau$-invariant, so gluing across $p=q$ is legitimate exactly as in the binary case. Each side is not a single regime: the exact census puts a thin extra segment on each side (the $\texttt{BBAAA}/\texttt{AABBB}$ pair). So the reduction is to a mirror-symmetric piecewise payoff in one own-vs-opponent variable — dominantly the regime-1/regime-2 pair, with two $\approx1.2\%$-weight segments at the two ends. It remains the single biggest simplification available, legitimate because the equilibrium is symmetric and stays off the corners.

5. The obstruction

In the pipeline: why naive integration fails — the fixed point that fact (F2) dissolves.

main focus general K

The trouble is not the kinks (smoothed away, §3b) and not monotonicity (clean, §3a). It is that the prize and the belief are the same unknown.

Look again at what the prize is built from:

$$p_v(b)=\frac{f_v\,h_v(b)}{\sum_u f_u\,h_u(b)}\quad\text{and}\quad \Psi=\big(\text{pushforward of }G\text{ through }p(\cdot)\big).$$

Both the posterior $p$ and the opponent-posterior law $\Psi$ are made out of the very densities $h_v$ that the indifference ODE $(\star_v)$ is trying to solve for — and the pairwise indifference conditions $(\star_{vv'})$ inherit the same circularity. So $(\star_v)$, and with it $(\star_{vv'})$, is a fixed-point ODE, not a quadrature: you cannot integrate $W$ as if it were an exogenous prize, because $W$ is defined through the unknown strategy. This circularity is the structural signature of a signaling equilibrium: the interim payoff $G(b)\,W(v,p(b))-b$ looks like an ordinary all-pay objective, but the prize $W$ silently depends on the very strategy being chosen.

How the binary case escaped

K=2

In the binary case the continuation turned out to be strategy-independent ($\sigma$-invariant, in the working notes): $\Psi=G^2$ pinned $W$ independently of the strategy. The fixed point dissolved, leaving the §7 separable ODE with a first integral and a single scalar

$$G_0=0.2221\quad\text{pinned by}\quad \int_{G_0}^{1}p_H\,dG=\tfrac12,\qquad U_H=2G_0,\quad U_L=0.$$

The entire solvability of the binary model rested on that one fact.

the gate — now resolved (§6) K=3

Does strategy-independence ($\sigma$-invariance) survive with three types? The decisive fact is that $\Psi$ is strategy-free — and this owes nothing to the regime count. In $t=G(b)$ a finalist's opponent is a Round-1 winner, so $\Psi(t)=t^2$ (§6a) whatever regimes the payoff visits; $\nabla_p W$ enters $(\star_v)$ only as $\Psi$-weights on the visited regimes' constant gradients (the $\sum_j$ form $(\nabla)$ of §3a). So the question reduces to: does $W(v,p)$, pushed through the belief map, depend on $\sigma$ only via a scalar (as $G^2$ did)?

Answer: yes — strategy-independent. In $t=G(b)$, $\Psi(t)=t^2$ universally, so the supports close in the binary form: a few scalars pinned by moment conditions like $\int p\,d\Psi=\text{prior}$, each type's support the interval $[0,\beta_v]$. The full curve — one $\operatorname{arctanh}$ branch on each side of the single kink at $t^\ast$ — is in §6.

6. Resolution — strategy-independence holds, and the curve is closed form

In the pipeline: the solve (steps 1–4) and the verify — the closed-form curve, the constants, the gates.

K=3 — $(v_L,v_M,v_H)=(1,2,3)$, uniform prior — except where marked otherwise

The concrete step — set up $(\star_v)$ in the state $t=G(b)$, substitute the regime forms and $\Psi$, and test for a first integral — is carried out symbolically; a first integral exists. The curve is computed with the full regime complex in Python (recompute_hcurve.py) and passes a uniform gate (§6d).

6a. State, posterior curve, and the universal $\Psi$

Work in $t=G(b)\in[0,1]$ (own win-prob / bid quantile); the population quantile is $t\sim\text{Uniform}[0,1]$, with the bid-$0$ mass point spread over $[0,t_0]$ by the tie-break lottery, $t_0=G(0)$. Let $m(t),h(t)$ be the Mid/High posterior shares at quantile $t$ (so $\text{Low}=1-m-h$). The opponent finalist's quantile law is

strategy-independence ($\sigma$-invariance) general K — independent of type count $$\Psi(t)=t^2\quad\text{universally — strategy-free, exactly as }\Psi=G^2\text{ in binary.}\qquad\text{\small($\Psi$)}$$

Why $t^2$: a finalist is a Round-1 winner, so the opponent finalist's quantile is the population quantile $s\sim\text{Uniform}[0,1]$ size-biased by its win probability $G=s$ (one rival, 2-player group) — density $2s$, hence CDF $\Psi(t)=\int_0^t 2s\,ds=t^2$. The derivation uses only the 2-player group-vs-group bracket structure — not $\sigma$, not the type count $K$ — so $W$ is pinned by the curve alone and the Round-1↔Round-2 fixed point dissolves for any $K$.

6b. The Round-2 regime payoffs

From the exact all-pay replay. Own posterior shares $(m,h)$, opponent shares $(mp,hp)$ ($mp,hp$ are the opponent's $m,h$ — read them as $m'$/$h'$, not as products):

own AHEAD (regime 1)own BEHIND (regime 2)
$\Pi_2(H;\cdot)$$2-\tfrac{h}{3}-mp-\tfrac{5hp}{3}$$2-m-\tfrac{5h}{3}-\tfrac{hp}{3}$
$\Pi_2(M;\cdot)$$1+\tfrac{h}{3}-mp-\tfrac{4hp}{3}$$1-m-\tfrac{2h}{3}-\tfrac{hp}{3}$
$\Pi_2(L;\cdot)$$\tfrac12(m-mp)+\tfrac23(h-hp)$$\mathbf{0}$ (weak finalist's Low gets exactly nothing)

6c. Continuation differences and the first integral

Each difference $D_{vL}$ below is $W(v,p)-W(L,p)$ with the continuation $W=\int\Pi_2\,d\Psi$ (eq. (W)) split at the own quantile $t$: opponent behind ($s<t$, regime 1) vs. opponent ahead ($s>t$, regime 2), integrated against $d\Psi=2s\,ds$. The opponent-behind moments are $A_2(t)=\int_0^t 2s\,m\,ds$, $A_3(t)=\int_0^t 2s\,h\,ds$ (both including the atom mass at $t_0$) and $H_3=A_3(1)=\mathbb{E}_{\text{finalist}}[h]$ (integral terms verified to $\sim10^{-15}$):

$$\begin{aligned} W(H)-W(L)&=:D_{HL}=2-m-\tfrac{5h}{3}+\big(\tfrac{m}{2}+\tfrac{2h}{3}\big)t^2-\tfrac{A_2}{2}-\tfrac{2A_3}{3}-\tfrac{H_3}{3},\\[2pt] W(M)-W(L)&=:D_{ML}=1-m-\tfrac{2h}{3}+\big(\tfrac{m}{2}+\tfrac{h}{3}\big)t^2-\tfrac{A_2}{2}-\tfrac{A_3}{3}-\tfrac{H_3}{3}. \end{aligned}$$

Where all three types mix, indifference of High and of Mid (each measured against Low) means $t\,D_{HL}=k_1$ and $t\,D_{ML}=k_2$ are constant ($k_1=U_H-U_L$, $k_2=U_M-U_L$) — these are the first integrals of $(\star_{HL})$ and $(\star_{ML})$. Differentiating both in $t$ makes the integral terms $A_2,A_3,H_3$ cancel; subtracting the two eliminates $m$ (this is the $m$-free difference $D_{HM}$ of §6f, which also gives the payoff-gap identity $k_1-k_2=t_0$), leaving an ODE in $h$ alone — clean below $t^\ast$, with a shifted argument above (see the panel):

the equilibrium posterior curve (thm:hcurve) $$k_1-k_2=t_0\quad(\text{payoff-gap identity, exact}),\qquad h'(t)=\frac{3\,t_0}{t^2\,(3-t^2)}\ \ \text{on }[t_0,t^\ast],$$ $$\boxed{\,h(t)=1-\frac{t_0}{t}+\frac{t_0}{\sqrt3}\Big(\operatorname{arctanh}\tfrac{t}{\sqrt3}-\operatorname{arctanh}\tfrac{t_0}{\sqrt3}\Big),\quad h(t_0)=0\ \ \text{— exact on }[t_0,\,t^\ast].\,}$$

The $\sqrt3=\sqrt{v_H}$ turns the first integral into an $\operatorname{arctanh}$ — the genuinely new feature versus binary's plain rational; the high valuation enters transcendentally. The closed form holds up to $t^\ast=h^{-1}(\tfrac32 m_0)=0.7581021794=t_0^2/k_2$. Above $t^\ast$ the own-far-ahead segment opens: the BBAAA correction of §3a acts over $s\in[0,\omega(t)]$, where the far-ahead threshold $\omega(t)$ — the weakest opponent you are still far ahead of — is fixed by the boundary equation

$$m\big(\omega(t)\big)+\tfrac23 h\big(\omega(t)\big)=\tfrac23 h(t)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad h(t)-h\big(\omega(t)\big)=\tfrac32\,m\big(\omega(t)\big).\tag{$\omega$}$$

Read from the other side, $(\omega)$ at $\omega^{-1}(t)$ says $h\big(\omega^{-1}(t)\big)=h(t)+\tfrac32m(t)$: the far-behind threshold is the same boundary solved for the other coordinate, which is why the two extra segments of §3a are governed by one function and its inverse. With the segment in place the equation picks up a shifted argument $$h'(t)=\frac{3\,t_0}{t^2\,\big(3-t^2-\omega(t)^2\big)}\qquad(t\ge t^\ast),$$ which looks like a functional-differential (delay) equation. It is not. The boundary first-integrates: differentiate $h\big(\omega^{-1}(t)\big)=\Phi(t)$ with $\Phi:=h+\tfrac32m$ and substitute both ODEs — the symmetric factor $(3-t^2-(\omega^{-1})^2)$ cancels, leaving $t_0(\omega^{-1})'/(\omega^{-1})^2=k_2/t^2$, i.e. $t_0/\omega^{-1}-k_2/t=c$ constant (lem:wallfirstint). So $\omega^{-1}$ is a Möbius map for every $c$ — and $c=0$ (thm:linwall), so the boundary is linear: $\omega^{-1}(t)=t/t^{\ast\ast}$, $\omega(t)=t\,t^{\ast\ast}$ with $t^{\ast\ast}=k_2/t_0=0.4350938650$ and $t^\ast t^{\ast\ast}=t_0$. The integrand above $t^\ast$ is then rational, and gives the same arctanh quadrature as below with $\sqrt3\mapsto\sqrt3/\kappa$, $\kappa^2=1+t^{\ast\ast2}$:$$h(t)=h(t^\ast)+t_0\Big[\tfrac1{t^\ast}-\tfrac1t+\tfrac{\kappa}{\sqrt3}\Big(\operatorname{arctanh}\tfrac{\kappa t}{\sqrt3}-\operatorname{arctanh}\tfrac{\kappa t^\ast}{\sqrt3}\Big)\Big],$$and $m=\tfrac23\,[\,h\big(\omega^{-1}(t)\big)-h(t)\,]$ is elementary too. At $t^\ast$ the segment jumps from empty to $[0,t_0]$, so $h'$ jumps by the factor $(3-t^{\ast2})/(3-t^{\ast2}-t_0^2)=1.04697$: $h$ is piecewise-$C^1$ with a single kink, at $t^\ast$ (not at $\beta_L$). The only non-elementary residue anywhere is a classical scalar ODE on the short band $[0.95728,\,0.97340]$; on the top two-type interval $[\beta_L,1]$, $m=1-h$ (§6f). 6e. Support structure

Three regions above the atom: atom $[0,0.3298]$ (Low+Mid pool, $h=0$); three-type overlap $[0.3298,\beta_L]$ (with the curve's single kink at $t^\ast=0.7581$ inside it); two-type (High, Mid) overlap $[\beta_L,1]$, $\beta_L=0.9734$. 6f. The top two-type interval $[\beta_L,1]$ — closes with no extra kink

On $(\beta_L,1]$ Low has exited, so only High and Mid mix (this interval is the “sliver” of the working notes) and the population constraint becomes $m=1-h$ (Low $=0$). The decisive simplification is that the H–M difference

$m$-free difference $$D_{HM}=D_{HL}-D_{ML}=1-h+\tfrac{h}{3}t^2-\tfrac{A_3}{3}$$

has its $m$ and $A_2$ terms cancel — it depends on $h$ alone. So the H–M indifference condition $(\star_{HM})=(\star_{HL})-(\star_{ML})$, in integrated form $t\,D_{HM}=k_1-k_2=t_0$, is an equation in $h$ only. Below $t^\ast$ it is the clean $\operatorname{arctanh}$ ODE; from $t^\ast$ on it is the shifted-argument ODE $h'=3t_0/\big(t^2(3-t^2-\omega(t)^2)\big)$ — and crucially that ODE is the same on $[t^\ast,\beta_L]$ and on $(\beta_L,1]$, because it never references $\beta_L$. That is why $h$ is $C^1$ across $\beta_L$: the top interval inherits the identical governing equation, so the only kink of $h$ on $(t_0,1]$ is at $t^\ast$, not at $\beta_L$. $t_0$ and $H_3$ (which see only $h$) are exact and unchanged. Only $m(t)$ switches — to $1-h$ — on the top interval.
