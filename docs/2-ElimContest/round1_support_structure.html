Round-1 Support Structure

Why IPV gives simple supports — the monotone posterior, the mirror symmetry, and the signaling obstruction

Pedagogical companion to round1_support_structure_handoff.md. 4-player, 2-round all-pay elimination contest with $K$ i.i.d. types, prior $f$; the solved instance is $K{=}3$: $v\in\{L,M,H\}=\{1,2,3\}$, uniform prior.

Round 1 looks like an ordinary all-pay contest, but it hides a twist: the prize you win for advancing depends on how you bid, because your bid is a signal of your type. This note is the pedagogical entry point to the model and its current results, with the pipeline stated for a general number of types $K$ — the solved instance is $K{=}3$, and the aim is to scale it up. It explains how the equilibrium is found by guess and verify (the pipeline below, including exactly which ODEs the solve integrates), what the twist does to the bid supports — the private-values (IPV) structure makes them simple single intervals, unlike Turocy's multi-interval supports — where the naive analysis gets stuck (§5), and how the obstruction is resolved (§6): the continuation prize is strategy-independent, and the equilibrium posterior curve is elementary in closed form on the whole support — an $\operatorname{arctanh}$ branch on each side of a single kink at $t^\ast$ — with one short band near the top where only the Mid/Low split lacks an elementary form.

The big picture — guess and verify

The whole pipeline on one page; §1–§6 fill in the details.

The model. Four players, two rounds. Each player's type is an i.i.d. draw from $K$ ordered values $v_1<\cdots<v_K$ with prior $f=(f_v)$; the solved instance is $K{=}3$: $v\in\{L,M,H\}=\{1,2,3\}$, uniform prior. Round 1 is two pairwise all-pay auctions (higher bid advances); all Round-1 bids are then publicly revealed, and the two winners meet in an all-pay final worth $v$ to its winner. Because bids are revealed, a finalist's Round-1 bid is a signal of their type, and Zhang & Wang (2009) rule out any symmetric separating equilibrium. So we look for a non-separating equilibrium, and we find it by guess and verify: guess the support architecture and a monotonicity property, solve exactly under that guess, then check every assumption on the solved object.

Two facts that cost nothing

Two structural facts hold in any symmetric SPNE, before anything is guessed.

  1. (F1) The final is an IPV all-pay auction à la Siegel, with priors = the Round-1 posteriors. Types are i.i.d. and Round-1 mixing is independent across players, so conditional on the revealed bids the finalists' types are independent. The final is therefore an asymmetric-prior independent-private-values all-pay auction, solved exactly by Siegel's construction: its payoff $\Pi_2(v;p,q)$ is piecewise affine in the two finalists' posteriors $p,q$, with 10 linear regimes at $K{=}3$ (§1, §6b). Round 2 is completely known as a function of beliefs; all the difficulty lives in Round 1.
  2. (F2) The opponent finalist's quantile law is strategy-free: $\Psi(t)=t^2$. Work in the bid quantile $t=G(b)$. Your final-round opponent is a Round-1 winner, so their quantile is uniform size-biased by the win probability — density $2s$, CDF $t^2$ — whatever the equilibrium strategy is (§6a). This is the hinge of the whole solution (the working notes call it $\sigma$-invariance): it makes the continuation prize a functional of the posterior curve alone, dissolving the fixed point that blocks the naive approach (§5).

The guess

  1. (G1) Support architecture. Every type's support is one interval with common floor $0$: a mass point at bid $0$ (quantile weight $t_0$) carried by every type except the top one; above it, nested ceilings — the lower types exit one by one, and the top two types both reach the very top (no pure-top tail). At $K{=}3$: the atom is Low+Mid; Low exits at $\beta_L$; Mid and High mix to the top. Like (G2), this architecture is a maintained hypothesis — assumed, and checkable, not established; in particular the composite mass point (every non-top type pooled at bid $0$) is part of the guess, and "The verify" below records which of its checks have actually been run.
  2. (G2) Monotone posterior. The posterior $p(b)$ is FOSD-increasing in the bid: the top share rises, the bottom share falls. This is a maintained hypothesis, not a theorem — assumed going in on deep-learning evidence, verified ex post on the solved curve (§3).
  3. (G3) Regime census. On the equilibrium path the posterior pair $(p,q)$ visits only a short list of the regimes of the piecewise-affine $\Pi_2$; which regimes, and over which segments of the opponent-quantile axis, is fixed by the census (§3a) and re-checked after solving. At $K{=}3$: 4 of the 10.

The solve

  1. Change of variables $t=G(b)$. Trade the bid for its quantile $t\in[0,1]$; the bid-$0$ mass point spreads over $[0,t_0]$ via the tie-break lottery, and the posterior is constant there. The unknown becomes the posterior curve — the posterior $p(b)$ of (G2), read in the quantile coordinate: $$P(t)\;=\;p\big(G^{-1}(t)\big),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad P\big(G(b)\big)\;=\;p(b),\qquad\text{\small(P)}$$ a point of the simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$ with components $p_v(t)$ (at $K{=}3$: $(\ell,m,h)(t)$); on the atom $[0,t_0]$, $G^{-1}$ is the quantile inverse ($\equiv0$ there), so (P) returns the constant atom posterior, matching the tie-break convention. The curve is the equilibrium: reading Bayes' rule backwards, type $v$'s bid-quantile density is $p_v(t)/f_v$, and every other equilibrium object — bids, $G$, densities, per-type CDFs — is recovered from the curve by the explicit pipeline of step 5. Nothing else needs to be solved for.
  2. The prize becomes a functional of the curve. By (F1)+(F2), $W(v,P(t))=\int_0^1 \Pi_2\big(v;P(t),P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds$ — the strategy no longer appears, so the indifference conditions close on the curve alone: genuine ODEs, not fixed-point equations (§5, §6a–§6c).
  3. Match the two faces of $D_{uv}$ — the ODE system, displayed in the panel below. The continuation gap $D_{uv}=W(u,P(t))-W(v,P(t))$ of an active pair is pinned twice over: indifference pins its value, $t\,D_{uv}=k_u-k_v$ — the first integrals (FI), obtained in §2 by subtracting two level identities, no calculus; Round 2 pins its composition, $D_{uv}=\mathcal D_{uv}[P](t)$, an explicit functional of the curve. The equilibrium curve is where the two faces agree, band by band from the floor up; one differentiation turns the matching into a determined quasi-linear first-order system whose unknown is $P(t)$ — never $D_{uv}$, whose value (FI) already gives. At $K{=}3$ the system triangularizes into a scalar $\operatorname{arctanh}$ ODE for the top share $h$, plus the payoff-gap identity $k_1-k_2=t_0$ (§6c, §6f).
  4. Bayes consistency closes the system. The curve must average back to the prior: the $K-1$ moment conditions $\int_0^1 p_v(t)\,dt=f_v$ — equivalently, each type's quantile density $p_v/f_v$ integrates to one. These pin the free scalars: the atom width $t_0$ and the floor posterior $P(t_0)$, and with them the payoff constants $k_v$ and every threshold. At $K{=}3$: $\int_0^1 h\,dt=\tfrac13$ pins $t_0$ alone; $t_0m_0+\int_{t_0}^1 m\,dt=\tfrac13$ pins $m_0$; then $k_1,k_2,t^\ast,\beta_L$ follow (§6d).
  5. Recover the equilibrium from the curve. With $P$ solved, everything else is explicit, in order: the prizes $W(v,P(t))$ are quadratures of knowns ($\Pi_2$ regime forms $+$ the law $\Psi$); the anchor $U_{v_1}=0$ — bid $0$ costs nothing and the bottom type's atom prize is identically zero (at $K{=}3$: $W(L,P(t_0))=0$, §6d) — turns the payoff constants into levels, $U_v=k_v$; the bid map is read off the level identity, $b(t)=t\,W(v,P(t))-k_v$, the same function for every active $v$ — that is exactly (FI) (at $K{=}3$: $b(t)=t\,W(L,P(t))$ on Low's support, since $k_L=0$); then $G=b^{-1}$, with the atom reappearing as $G$'s jump of size $t_0$ at bid $0$, the bid density $g(b(t))=1/b'(t)$, and the per-type CDFs $G_v(b(t))=\tfrac1{f_v}\int_0^t p_v(s)\,ds$.
the two faces of $D_{uv}$ — how the equilibrium is computed, without computing it general K

Fix a band: an interval of quantiles on which the active set $\mathcal A(t)$ (the types still mixing) and the regime census are constant. Under (G1) the active set only shrinks as $t$ rises, one exit at a time. Along the curve write $D_{uv}(t):=W\big(u,P(t)\big)-W\big(v,P(t)\big)$ for the continuation gap of a type pair $u,v$, and $k_v:=U_v-U_{v_1}$ for the payoffs measured from the bottom type ($k_{v_1}=0$). The gap of an active pair is then pinned twice over, by two faces that know nothing of each other:

  • Face 1 — indifference pins the value. Subtracting the two level identities of §2 at a common bid (one line, no calculus) gives the first integrals $$t\,D_{uv}(t)\;=\;k_u-k_v\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathcal A(t)\ \text{— constant along the band.}\qquad\text{\small(FI)}$$ So $D_{uv}(t)=(k_u-k_v)/t$: a known number at each $t$, up to the constants $k$. This face never looks at Round 2.
  • Face 2 — Round 2 pins the composition. By (F1)+(F2) the same gap is an explicit functional of the curve, $D_{uv}(t)=\mathcal D_{uv}[P](t)$: given the regime census, affine in $P(t)$ with polynomial-in-$t$ coefficients, plus moments $\int_0^t 2s\,p_u(s)\,ds$ of the curve against the opponent-quantile density $2s$ of (F2). This face never looks at indifference.

The equilibrium curve is where the two faces match: $\mathcal D_{uv}[P](t)=(k_u-k_v)/t$ on every band. The count is right: exited types have $p_v\equiv0$ and the shares sum to one, so $P(t)$ has $|\mathcal A(t)|-1$ free coordinates — exactly the number of independent pair equations the active set supplies. At each exit one equation and one unknown leave together, so the system stays square from the floor to the top — which is why at $K{=}3$ the same governing equation carries across $\beta_L$ (§6f). And the unknown is always the curve $P$, never $D_{uv}$: "solving for $D_{uv}$" as a scalar has nothing to solve — its value is already (FI); the entire content of the matching is that the composition $\mathcal D_{uv}[P]$ must realize that value.

One differentiation makes the matching a genuine ODE system. Differentiating (FI) gives, for each active pair, the first-order equation

$$\frac{d}{dt}\,D_{uv}(t)\;=\;-\,\frac{k_u-k_v}{t^{2}}\,,\qquad\text{\small(ODE)}$$

and on the left the differentiation localizes the moments: each $\int_0^t 2s\,p_u(s)\,ds$ in the composition differentiates to the local term $2t\,p_u(t)$, and every Leibniz boundary term vanishes because $\Pi_2$ is continuous across regime boundaries (§3a, step 4). So (ODE) is affine in $\big(P(t),P'(t)\big)$ with elementary coefficients — a genuine quasi-linear first-order system in the curve, not an integro-differential or fixed-point equation.

The solve is then mechanical in outline: integrate the determined system upward from $t_0$, starting at the floor posterior, whose top share vanishes under (G1); glue with continuity at each regime crossing, where only the coefficients of (ODE) change — at $K{=}3$ the crossing at $t^\ast$ brings in the shifted argument $\omega(t)$, itself pinned by the boundary equation $(\omega)$ of §6c — and at each exit, located by $p_v(\beta_v)=0$. At $K{=}3$ the system even triangularizes: the adjacent gap $D_{HM}$ is univariate in the top share $h$ (§6f), so (ODE) collapses to the scalar $h'(t)=3t_0/\big(t^2(3-t^2)\big)$ — an $\operatorname{arctanh}$ quadrature — and $m$ is recovered from the remaining first integral. (K=2 a single pair, a single share: everything is closed form.) Whether the adjacent gaps triangularize for general $K$ is the central structural question for scaling up.

The verify

The guessed ingredients are then checked on the solved object: the first-integral residuals $|t\,D_{uv}-(k_u-k_v)|$ hold uniformly (at $K{=}3$: $1.3\times10^{-7}$, the gate, §6d); the posterior is FOSD-increasing ex post (G2 ✓); every exited type strictly prefers to stay out beyond its ceiling (at $K{=}3$: $U_L<0$ above $\beta_L$, so the exclusion is admissible, §6f); the on-path regime census matches (G3 ✓). Two things this does not address. Bid-$0$ incentive compatibility of the atom (part of G1): OPEN — never checked. The gate tests the interior first integrals along the curve; no check has been run that every type pooled at bid $0$ is willing to stay there. And uniqueness: the construction delivers an equilibrium, not a proof that it is the only one. The claim-by-claim proved/machine-checked ledger is docs/STATE.md + docs/proofs.tex.

what scales, what must be redone the K-ladder

Already general in $K$: the final is a Siegel all-pay auction with piecewise-affine $\Pi_2$ (F1); the opponent-quantile law $\Psi(t)=t^2$, strategy-free (F2, §6a); the smoothing of $\Pi_2$'s kinks into a $C^1$ prize (§3b); the gradient formula $(\nabla)$ — one constant vector per visited regime (§3a); the cone principle giving fixed-sign signaling channels for the two extreme types (§3a); and the band-by-band determinacy of the ODE system (FI)/(ODE) above.

To be redone at each $K$: the regime complex of $\Pi_2$ and its on-path census (at $K{=}3$: 10 regimes, 4 visited); sign verdicts for the $K-2$ interior types, on which the cone argument is silent (§3a); the cancellation that makes the adjacent gaps univariate — hence the closed form (at $K{=}3$: the $\operatorname{arctanh}$); and admissibility at each exit. The guess (G1)–(G3) is itself the $K{=}3$-verified pattern proposed for general $K$.

1. The object: a signaling all-pay auction

In the pipeline: defines the objects — $G$, the posterior $p(b)$, the prize $W$, the interim payoff (U).

general K

Fix a player of type $v$ among $K$ types. Within a group, two players bid in an all-pay auction; the higher bid advances to the final. Write

  • $G_v(b)$ — the CDF of type $v$'s Round-1 bid, with density $g_v(b)=G_v'(b)$ — capital letters for bid CDFs, matching lowercase for their densities;
  • the chance of winning Round 1 is the rival's bid CDF, $G(b)=\sum_u f_u\,G_u(b)$, with density $g(b)=\sum_u f_u\,g_u(b)$, where $f_u$ is the prior probability of type $u$; the profile of densities $\sigma=(g_v)_{v=1}^K$ is the Round-1 strategy we solve for;
  • the bid is a signal: winning with bid $b$ induces a posterior over your own type, $$p_v(b)=\frac{f_v\,g_v(b)}{\sum_u f_u\,g_u(b)}=\frac{f_v\,g_v(b)}{g(b)}\qquad\text{\small(post)}$$ a point in the type simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$ ($K{-}1$ free coordinates, since $\sum_v p_v=1$); K=3 with the uniform prior used from §2 on, $f_v\equiv\tfrac13$ cancels in (post) and the simplex is $\Delta^2$;
  • the continuation value of reaching the final as type $v$ with posterior $p$ (the belief vector $p=(p_v)_{v=1}^K$ your winning bid induced; the working notes call it the reputation) is $$W(v,p)=\int \Pi_2\!\big(v;\,p,\,q\big)\,d\Psi(q),\qquad\text{\small(W)}$$ where $q$ is the opponent finalist's posterior, $\Psi$ its distribution, and $\Pi_2$ the Round-2 payoff; K=3 $\Pi_2$ is piecewise-affine over a 10-regime complex (derived in docs/tropical_cell_structure.html / memory three-type-r2-tropical).

The interim Round-1 payoff of bidding $b$ as type $v$ is

$$U_v(b)\;=\;\underbrace{G(b)}_{\text{win prob}}\cdot\underbrace{W\big(v,\,p(b)\big)}_{\text{prize}}\;-\;\underbrace{b}_{\text{all-pay cost}}.\qquad\text{\small(U)}$$
the twist general K

The prize $W(v,p(b))$ depends on your own bid through the belief $p(b)$. This is not a Siegel contest, where the prize is a fixed number. It is a signaling all-pay auction — the genus of Hörner & Sahuguet, Costly Signalling in Auctions (2007). Siegel's clean support theory does not apply off the shelf; the question is whether IPV restores enough structure to recover his conclusions anyway.

2. Indifference: the level identity and its derivatives

In the pipeline: the primitive identity behind (FI) — Face 1 of the matching — and, as its derivatives, the marginal conditions $(\star_v)$, $(\star_{uv})$ used from §3 on.

general K

Where a type mixes, every bid in its support must give the same payoff: $U_v(b)\equiv U_v$, a constant. Read this constancy in the quantile coordinate of solve step 1 — $t=G(b)$, with $b(t)$ the bid at quantile $t$ — using (U) and (P). Above the atom ($t>t_0$) it is the level identity:

the level identity — the primitive fact general K $$U_v\;=\;t\,W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;-\;b(t)\qquad\text{for every }t\text{ in }v\text{'s support above the atom.}\qquad\text{\small(lev)}$$

In an all-pay round the cost $b(t)$ is sunk and $t\,W(v,P(t))$ is the expected continuation prize, so the right side is just (U) read at quantile $t$; mixing makes it flat in $t$. Everything §2 owes the pipeline follows from (lev) by subtraction or differentiation.

First the subtraction — it is the whole derivation of (FI). Take two types $u,v$ both active at the same bid $b(t)$ and subtract their level identities: the common sunk cost cancels, and with it every trace of the strategy:

(FI) in one line — no calculus general K $$U_u-U_v\;=\;\big[t\,W\big(u,P(t)\big)-b(t)\big]-\big[t\,W\big(v,P(t)\big)-b(t)\big]\;=\;t\,D_{uv}(t)\;=\;k_u-k_v .$$

No densities, no cancellation of $g$, no smoothness of $W$: only two constants subtracted at a common bid. The constant $k_u-k_v$ is the same on every band where the pair is active, because the levels $U_u,U_v$ are global constants. This is Face 1 of the big-picture panel.

The derivative forms are consequences, kept because §3–§6 use them ($W$ is $C^1$ along the curve, §3b, so they exist). Differentiating $U_v(b)\equiv U_v$ — the bid-space form of (lev) — splits the marginal incentive into two channels:

$$\underbrace{g(b)\,W\big(v,p(b)\big)}_{\text{win-probability channel}}\;+\;\underbrace{G(b)\,\nabla_p W\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b)}_{\text{signaling channel}}\;=\;1.\qquad\text{\small($\star_v$)}$$

Subtracting one type's condition from another cancels the $1$ and leaves the pairwise indifference (single-crossing) conditions $(\star_{uv})$ — the difference of $(\star_u)$ and $(\star_v)$ for an active pair of types $u,v$ (two plain letters: primes are reserved for derivatives). Just as $(\star_v)$ is the $b$-derivative of (lev), $(\star_{uv})$ is exactly the $b$-derivative of (FI):

$$g(b)\,\big[W(u,p)-W(v,p)\big]\;+\;G(b)\,\big[\nabla_p W(u,p)-\nabla_p W(v,p)\big]\cdot p'(b)\;=\;0.\qquad\text{\small($\star_{uv}$)}$$

Finally, differentiating (FI) itself in $t$ gives the system (ODE) of the big-picture panel: $\frac{d}{dt}\big[t\,D_{uv}\big]=0$ unpacks to $D_{uv}'=-D_{uv}/t=-(k_u-k_v)/t^2$. So the logic runs levels first, derivatives second: (lev) gives (FI) by subtraction; $(\star_v)$, $(\star_{uv})$, (ODE) are its derivatives. Nothing is lost in either direction — $(\star_{uv})$ is (FI)'s derivative, so integrating it back merely recovers (FI).

K=2 there is a single pair, and the single-crossing fact is $\nabla_p\big[W(H,p)-W(L,p)\big]<0$ (roadmap §7). K=3 the active pairs are $(H,L)$ and $(M,L)$; the entire §6 solve is the two instances $t\,D_{HL}=k_1$, $t\,D_{ML}=k_2$ of (FI), matched to their Round-2 composition. Everything below is about understanding the two ingredients in $(\star_v)$: the signaling channel (governed by the monotone posterior) and the fact that $W$ is the same unknown as the strategy (the obstruction).

3. The monotone posterior — what IPV buys

In the pipeline: the guess (G2) and the reductions it buys, including the regime census (G3).

the guess (G2) — its status K=3 — DL evidence, verified ex post

The monotone posterior is a maintained hypothesis, not a theorem: we assume the posterior is FOSD-increasing in the bid, solve under that assumption, and check it on the solved curve afterwards. The evidence going in is the deep-learning Round-1 run — as the bid rises, $p_H(b)$ climbs $0.05\to0.65$ and $p_L(b)$ falls $0.59\to0.10$ (the middle share $p_M(b)$ pinned by $p_L{+}p_M{+}p_H{=}1$) — and the solved curve of §6 confirms it ex post ($h$ rises $0\to0.769$, the Low share falls $0.588\to0$). High bids signal high types. This single fact drives three reductions.

3a. The signaling channel has a fixed sign

general K, specialized to K=2 and K=3 below

Fix a bid $b$ and look at the second term of $(\star_v)$, the signaling channel $$\nabla_p W\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b).$$ We want conditions under which this has a sign that does not change as $b$ sweeps the support. Two ingredients enter: the velocity $p'(b)$ of the belief path, and the gradient $\nabla_pW(v,\cdot)$ of the prize. Monotonicity of the posterior constrains the first; the Round-2 economics constrains the second. The argument below is stated for a general number of types $K$, then specialized to $K=2$ (where it degenerates to something already familiar) and to $K=3$ (where it has real content).

The state space, and why its dimension is the whole story

With $K$ types the posterior $p=(p_v)_{v=1}^K$ lives in the simplex $\Delta^{K-1}$, so $\sum_v p_v=1$ and the velocity satisfies $\sum_v p_v'=0$. The tangent space is therefore of dimension $K-1$:

typesposteriortangent spaceunit directions available
$K=2$$p_H$, with $p_L=1-p_H$ eliminated$\dim=1$ — a lineexactly two: $\pm1$
$K=3$$(p_H,p_L)$, with $p_M=1-p_H-p_L$ eliminated$\dim=2$ — a planea whole circle

This dimension count is what separates the two cases below.

what monotonicity actually gives general K

The monotone posterior of §3 says the High share rises and the Low share falls with the bid: $$p_H'(b)>0,\qquad p_L'(b)<0\qquad\text{throughout the mixing region.}\tag{M}$$ This is a statement about the signs of the components, i.e. about which orthant the velocity occupies. Equivalently: there is a fixed convex cone $\mathcal{C}$ in the tangent space with $$p'(b)\in\mathcal{C}\quad\text{for every }b.$$ K=3 $\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-)\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+)$, an open quadrant. K=2 only the first inequality survives and $\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-$, an open half-line. Note what (M) does not say: nothing about the ratio of the components, hence nothing about where inside $\mathcal{C}$ the velocity points.

The principle: two cones in duality

general K

Write the channel as an inner product in the tangent space and recall the definition of the polar (dual) cone, $$\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\;=\;\{z:\ \langle z,x\rangle\le 0\ \ \text{for all }x\in\mathcal{C}\}.$$

the correct principle — any $K$ $$\left.\begin{aligned}p'(b)&\in\mathcal{C}\\ \nabla_pW(v,p(b))&\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{C}^{\circ}\end{aligned}\right\}\ \text{for all }b \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \nabla_pW(v,p(b))\cdot p'(b)<0\ \text{ for all }b,$$

and symmetrically $\nabla_pW\in\operatorname{int}(-\mathcal{C}^{\circ})$ gives a strictly positive channel — immediate from the definition of the polar cone. The hypotheses are membership in the two orthants only: no constancy of the direction $p'/|p'|$, no magnitude bound on either vector. Smoothness of $W$ (§3b) is what makes $\nabla_pW$ exist so that it can be placed in a cone at all.

Specialization I: $K=2$ — the degenerate case

The tangent space is a line, so $p'(b)$ and $\nabla_pW(v,p)$ are scalars and the channel is a product of two numbers, $$\nabla_pW\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b),\qquad p'(b)>0 .$$ Its sign is simply the sign of $\nabla_pW(v,p)$, and the cone condition $\nabla_pW\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{C}^\circ=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+$ is the statement $\nabla_pW(v,p)<0$. The solved binary case supplies both signs explicitly (roadmap §7, with $\Psi=G^2$ and $\Delta=v_H-v_L$):

$$\nabla_pW(H,p)=-\Delta\big(1-\Psi(p_H)\big)\;\le\;0,\qquad\qquad \nabla_pW(L,p)=\frac{v_L\Delta}{v_H}\,\Psi(p_H)\;\ge\;0 .$$

(The first is the identity quoted in §3b, at $(v_H,v_L)=(3,1)$.) High negative, Low positive — the fixed-sign channel, obtained with no cone machinery because in one dimension there is nothing for the machinery to do. Both types are extreme when $K=2$, so the argument covers the whole type space and the binary story is complete.

Specialization II: $K=3$ — where the content is

Now the tangent space is a plane; write $\nabla_pW(v,p)=\big(\partial W/\partial p_H,\ \partial W/\partial p_L\big)$ in the coordinates $(p_H,p_L)$. The cone $\mathcal{C}$ is the quadrant $(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_-)\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_+)$, and its polar is $$\mathcal{C}^{\circ}=\mathbb{R}_{-}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{\partial W}{\partial p_H}\le0,\ \ \frac{\partial W}{\partial p_L}\ge0 .$$ The gradient is available in closed form. The derivation is six short steps, and worth following in full: each step cashes in a result established earlier in this note.

  1. From the opponent's posterior to the opponent's quantile. Along the equilibrium the opponent finalist's posterior is a function of their bid quantile, $q=P(s)$ with $s\in[0,1]$, and by §6a their quantile carries density $2s\,ds$. So $(W)$ reads $$W(v,p)=\int_0^1 \Pi_2\big(v;\,p,\,P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds .$$ Here $p$ is free: $\Pi_2(v;p,q)$ is defined for any pair of posteriors and $\Psi$ is a fixed measure over the opponent's $q$, so $W(v,p)$ is a genuine function of $p$ on the simplex and $\nabla_p$ has something to act on.
  2. Partition the opponent's quantile axis by the regime the pair lands in. Fix the own quantile $t$, so $p=P(t)$. As $s$ runs $0\to1$ the pair $\big(P(t),P(s)\big)$ travels through the Round-2 complex, staying inside one regime except at finitely many boundary-crossings $$0=s_0(t)<s_1(t)<\cdots<s_{J(t)}(t)=1 .$$ On the open segment $I_j(t)=\big(s_{j-1}(t),s_j(t)\big)$ the pair sits in a single regime $c_j(t)$ of the complex, so there $\Pi_2$ equals that regime's affine form, written $\Pi_2^{[c_j(t)]}$. Thus the superscript index $j=1,\dots,J(t)$ enumerates the segments — equivalently the ordered list of regimes the opponent's trajectory visits as $s$ increases — and $$W\big(v,P(t)\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{J(t)}\int_{s_{j-1}(t)}^{s_j(t)}\Pi_2^{[c_j(t)]}\big(v;\,P(t),\,P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds .$$ Generically $J(t)=2$, split at the diagonal $s_1=t$ (own ahead of the opponent for $s<t$, own behind for $s>t$). The exact census (census_exact_curve.py) shows the on-path trajectory also grazes two thin extra regimes near the ends of $[0,1]$; these are carried explicitly, not dropped (step 6 and the segment table below).
  3. Differentiate — Leibniz gives interior parts and moving-boundary parts. Moving $p$ (equivalently $t$) both moves each integrand and slides every boundary $s_j(t)$. Leibniz across all $J(t)$ segments: $$\nabla_pW=\sum_{j=1}^{J(t)}\int_{s_{j-1}}^{s_j}\nabla_p\Pi_2^{[c_j]}\,2s\,ds\;+\;\sum_{j=1}^{J(t)-1}\Big[\,\Pi_2^{[c_j]}-\Pi_2^{[c_{j+1}]}\,\Big]_{s=s_j(t)}\,2\,s_j(t)\,\partial_ps_j(t) .$$

summary of the logic K=2 vs K=3

Monotonicity (M) confines the velocity to a fixed cone $\mathcal{C}$. The Round-2 economics of §3c confines the gradient to the polar cone $\mathcal{C}^\circ$ (High) or to $-\mathcal{C}^\circ$ (Low). Duality of the two cones fixes the sign of their pairing at every bid, with no reference to a direction and no magnitudes. At $K=2$ the cones are half-lines and the whole thing collapses to $\nabla_pW(H,p)<0$, $\nabla_pW(L,p)>0$; at $K=3$ it delivers the two extreme types and stops.

What this does and does not deliver. A constant-sign channel for the extreme types is the single-crossing input an interval-support argument wants, and it is consistent with the finding (§6e) that each support is one connected interval $[0,\beta_v]$. It is not by itself a proof that the supports are intervals: for $K=3$ that would additionally require a verdict for Mid, which the cone argument does not supply. For $K=2$, where both types are extreme, the corresponding gap does not arise — another reason the binary case reads as easier than it is. Treat "supports are single intervals" as strongly indicated by the fixed-sign channel at the extremes together with the solved curve, not as established by §3a alone.

3b. The prize is smooth, even though $\Pi_2$ is kinked

The Round-2 payoff $\Pi_2$ is full of kinks (the $\min(p_H,q_H)$, the $(p_H-q_H)^+$). One might fear those kinks make the indifference ODE ill-posed. They do not — because the prize is an average:

key general K $$W(v,p)=\int \Pi_2(v;p,q)\,d\Psi(q)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad W(v,\cdot)\ \text{is}\ C^1,\quad \nabla_p W=\Psi\big|_{\text{kink locus}}.$$

Integrating a kinked function against an atomless $\Psi$ smooths the kinks away: the kink locus (the set of opponent posteriors $q$ where $\Pi_2$ bends — e.g. the diagonal $p=q$) has $\Psi$-measure zero, so it contributes nothing to the average; the kink cancels and only its slope survives, which is the content of $\nabla_p W=\Psi|_{\text{kink locus}}$ (an envelope / Leibniz computation). This matches the $K=2$ identity $\nabla_pW(H,p)=-2\big(1-\Psi(p_H)\big)$ with $\Psi=G^2$. The kinks of $\Pi_2$ never reach the bidder. So $(\star_v)$ is a genuine, well-posed first-order ODE.

Monotone $p(b)$ + atomless $\Psi$ make the $(\star_v)$ coefficients monotone and single-crossing in $v$ — precisely the hypothesis an interval-support argument needs. So each type's support is one connected interval $[0,\beta_v]$ in bid space, with a common floor $b_{lo}=0$. Contrast with Turocy. Turocy's rich multi-interval / gapped supports need a non-monotone or non-single-crossing prize (correlation / common value). IPV with atomless $\Psi$ rules that out. IPV is exactly what collapses the support to a single interval.

5. The obstruction

In the pipeline: why naive integration fails — the fixed point that fact (F2) dissolves.

main focus general K

The trouble is not the kinks (smoothed away, §3b) and not monotonicity (clean, §3a). It is that the prize and the belief are the same unknown.

Look again at what the prize is built from:

$$p_v(b)=\frac{f_v\,g_v(b)}{\sum_u f_u\,g_u(b)}\quad\text{and}\quad \Psi=\big(\text{pushforward of }G\text{ through }p(\cdot)\big).$$

Both the posterior $p$ and the opponent-posterior law $\Psi$ are made out of the very densities $g_v$ that the indifference ODE $(\star_v)$ is trying to solve for — and the pairwise indifference conditions $(\star_{uv})$ inherit the same circularity. So $(\star_v)$, and with it $(\star_{uv})$, is a fixed-point ODE, not a quadrature: you cannot integrate $W$ as if it were an exogenous prize, because $W$ is defined through the unknown strategy. This circularity is the structural signature of a signaling equilibrium: the interim payoff $G(b)\,W(v,p(b))-b$ looks like an ordinary all-pay objective, but the prize $W$ silently depends on the very strategy being chosen.

How the binary case escaped

K=2

In the binary case the continuation turned out to be strategy-independent ($\sigma$-invariant, in the working notes): $\Psi=G^2$ pinned $W$ independently of the strategy. The fixed point dissolved, leaving the §7 separable ODE with a first integral and a single scalar

$$t_0=0.2221\quad\text{pinned by}\quad \int_{t_0}^{1}p_H\,dG=\tfrac12,\qquad U_H=2t_0,\quad U_L=0.$$

The entire solvability of the binary model rested on that one fact. (The roadmap's §7 writes $G_0$ for this atom quantile; here it is $t_0=G(0)$, matching §6a and keeping $G$-subscripts free for the type CDFs $G_v$.)

the gate — now resolved (§6) K=3

Does strategy-independence ($\sigma$-invariance) survive with three types? The decisive fact is that $\Psi$ is strategy-free — and this owes nothing to the regime count. In $t=G(b)$ a finalist's opponent is a Round-1 winner, so $\Psi(t)=t^2$ (§6a) whatever regimes the payoff visits; $\nabla_p W$ enters $(\star_v)$ only as $\Psi$-weights on the visited regimes' constant gradients (the $\sum_j$ form $(\nabla)$ of §3a). So the question reduces to: does $W(v,p)$, pushed through the belief map, depend on $\sigma$ only via a scalar (as $G^2$ did)?

Answer: yes — strategy-independent. In $t=G(b)$, $\Psi(t)=t^2$ universally, so the supports close in the binary form: a few scalars pinned by moment conditions like $\int p\,d\Psi=\text{prior}$, each type's support the interval $[0,\beta_v]$. The full curve — one $\operatorname{arctanh}$ branch on each side of the single kink at $t^\ast$ — is in §6.

the equilibrium posterior curve (thm:hcurve) $$k_1-k_2=t_0\quad(\text{payoff-gap identity, exact}),\qquad h'(t)=\frac{3\,t_0}{t^2\,(3-t^2)}\ \ \text{on }[t_0,t^\ast],$$ $$\boxed{\,h(t)=1-\frac{t_0}{t}+\frac{t_0}{\sqrt3}\Big(\operatorname{arctanh}\tfrac{t}{\sqrt3}-\operatorname{arctanh}\tfrac{t_0}{\sqrt3}\Big),\quad h(t_0)=0\ \ \text{— exact on }[t_0,\,t^\ast].\,}$$

The $\sqrt3=\sqrt{v_H}$ turns the first integral into an $\operatorname{arctanh}$ — the genuinely new feature versus binary's plain rational; the high valuation enters transcendentally. The closed form holds up to $t^\ast=h^{-1}(\tfrac32 m_0)=0.7581021794=t_0^2/k_2$. Above $t^\ast$ the own-far-ahead segment opens: the BBAAA correction of §3a acts over $s\in[0,\omega(t)]$, where the far-ahead threshold $\omega(t)$ — the weakest opponent you are still far ahead of — is fixed by the boundary equation

$$m\big(\omega(t)\big)+\tfrac23 h\big(\omega(t)\big)=\tfrac23 h(t)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad h(t)-h\big(\omega(t)\big)=\tfrac32\,m\big(\omega(t)\big).\tag{$\omega$}$$

Read from the other side, $(\omega)$ at $\omega^{-1}(t)$ says $h\big(\omega^{-1}(t)\big)=h(t)+\tfrac32m(t)$: the far-behind threshold is the same boundary solved for the other coordinate, which is why the two extra segments of §3a are governed by one function and its inverse. With the segment in place the equation picks up a shifted argument $$h'(t)=\frac{3\,t_0}{t^2\,\big(3-t^2-\omega(t)^2\big)}\qquad(t\ge t^\ast),$$ which looks like a functional-differential (delay) equation. It is not. The boundary first-integrates: differentiate $h\big(\omega^{-1}(t)\big)=\Phi(t)$ with $\Phi:=h+\tfrac32m$ and substitute both ODEs — the symmetric factor $(3-t^2-(\omega^{-1})^2)$ cancels, leaving $t_0(\omega^{-1})'/(\omega^{-1})^2=k_2/t^2$, i.e. $t_0/\omega^{-1}-k_2/t=c$ constant (lem:wallfirstint). So $\omega^{-1}$ is a Möbius map for every $c$ — and $c=0$ (thm:linwall), so the boundary is linear: $\omega^{-1}(t)=t/t^{\ast\ast}$, $\omega(t)=t\,t^{\ast\ast}$ with $t^{\ast\ast}=k_2/t_0=0.4350938650$ and $t^\ast t^{\ast\ast}=t_0$. The integrand above $t^\ast$ is then rational, and gives the same arctanh quadrature as below with $\sqrt3\mapsto\sqrt3/\kappa$, $\kappa^2=1+t^{\ast\ast2}$:$$h(t)=h(t^\ast)+t_0\Big[\tfrac1{t^\ast}-\tfrac1t+\tfrac{\kappa}{\sqrt3}\Big(\operatorname{arctanh}\tfrac{\kappa t}{\sqrt3}-\operatorname{arctanh}\tfrac{\kappa t^\ast}{\sqrt3}\Big)\Big],$$and $m=\tfrac23\,[\,h\big(\omega^{-1}(t)\big)-h(t)\,]$ is elementary too. At $t^\ast$ the segment jumps from empty to $[0,t_0]$, so $h'$ jumps by the factor $(3-t^{\ast2})/(3-t^{\ast2}-t_0^2)=1.04697$: $h$ is piecewise-$C^1$ with a single kink, at $t^\ast$ (not at $\beta_L$). The only non-elementary residue anywhere is a classical scalar ODE on the short band $[0.95728,\,0.97340]$; on the top two-type interval $[\beta_L,1]$, $m=1-h$ (§6f). 6f. The top two-type interval $[\beta_L,1]$ — closes with no extra kink

On $(\beta_L,1]$ Low has exited, so only High and Mid mix (this interval is the “sliver” of the working notes) and the population constraint becomes $m=1-h$ (Low $=0$). The decisive simplification is that the H–M difference

$m$-free difference $$D_{HM}=D_{HL}-D_{ML}=1-h+\tfrac{h}{3}t^2-\tfrac{A_3}{3}$$

has its $m$ and $A_2$ terms cancel — it depends on $h$ alone. So the H–M indifference condition $(\star_{HM})=(\star_{HL})-(\star_{ML})$, in integrated form $t\,D_{HM}=k_1-k_2=t_0$, is an equation in $h$ only. Below $t^\ast$ it is the clean $\operatorname{arctanh}$ ODE; from $t^\ast$ on it is the shifted-argument ODE $h'=3t_0/\big(t^2(3-t^2-\omega(t)^2)\big)$ — and crucially that ODE is the same on $[t^\ast,\beta_L]$ and on $(\beta_L,1]$, because it never references $\beta_L$. That is why $h$ is $C^1$ across $\beta_L$: the top interval inherits the identical governing equation, so the only kink of $h$ on $(t_0,1]$ is at $t^\ast$, not at $\beta_L$. $t_0$ and $A_3(1)$ (which see only $h$) are exact and unchanged. Only $m(t)$ switches — to $1-h$ — on the top interval.
