The Continuum Limit

A tutorial: how the discrete pair relations become a local first integral, why bid supports are iso-win-probability sets, the entry curve $t_e=U'$, why exit is a co-equal unknown, the Lu–Parreiras kernel, and the Volterra outlook

Session 2026-07-29. Banked in docs/proofs.tex §sec:continuum (labels cited throughout); dispatched as briefs docs/tasks/2026-07-29-{1,2,3}-*.md. Companion entry point for the discrete machinery: docs/round1_support_structure.html (the guess-and-verify pipeline, the equations (FI), (D), (ODE), ($\star_{uv}$)); cascade note: docs/triangular_cascade.html.

status — read this first

Everything here is either exact under stated regularity hypotheses (the envelope arguments) or a formal limit of a banked discrete relation (the ladder, the cascade). The continuum equilibrium is not constructed, but it is now pinned: the kernel inverts in closed form (brief 2, prop:kernelinv) and the resulting system is square (brief 3, thm:contsquare). What remains open is existence itself and the boundary layers (brief 1). Zhang & Wang's impossibility theorem — no symmetric separating equilibrium under full revelation — is itself stated for a continuum of types, so this limit is not a generalization of the discrete program; it is the road back to the paper's native habitat. The discrete-$K$ solutions ($K=2,3,4$) should ultimately read as finite-difference schemes for the system assembled in §8.

1. Setup — what carries over verbatim

One paragraph of model, one of notation; details in the support-structure note.

Model. Four players, two rounds. Round 1: two pairwise all-pay auctions; all bids publicly revealed. Round 2: the two winners meet in an all-pay final worth $v$ to its winner. Types are now i.i.d. draws from a density $f$ on $[\underline v,\bar v]$, private throughout.

Notation (CONT), def:cont. Work in the bid quantile $t=G(b)$. The unknown is the posterior density $p_t(\cdot)$ — the continuum version of the posterior curve $P(t)$: the density over types of a bidder observed at quantile $t$. Rents are $U(v)$, anchored at $U(\underline v)=0$. The prize is $$W\big(v,P(t)\big)=\int_0^1 \Pi_2\big(v;P(t),P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds,$$ with $\Pi_2$ the Siegel final payoff and $2s\,ds$ the strategy-free opponent-quantile law $\Psi(t)=t^2$ (lem:sigmainv) — both facts survive the continuum unchanged, since neither ever counted types. Each type mixes over one quantile interval $$\operatorname{supp}\sigma_v=[\,t_e(v),\,t_x(v)\,],$$ the entry curve and the exit curve — both unknowns of the system (§5). The active set at $t$ is an interval of types (continuum (INT)): $\mathcal A(t)=[a_{\min}(t),a_{\max}(t)]$, with $a_{\max}=t_e^{-1}$ and $a_{\min}=t_x^{-1}$.

The one new symbol. Everything below is organized around the own-type slope of the prize: $$\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;:=\;\partial_v W\big(v,P(t)\big).$$

2. The limit computation — one division, one limit

From the pair conditions to a local condition. lem:filoc

The discrete solve rests on the pair relations: the first integrals (FI), $t\,D_{uv}(t)=k_{uv}=U_u-U_v$, and their bid-space derivative, the single-crossing condition $$g(b)\big[W(u,p)-W(v,p)\big]\;+\;G(b)\big[\nabla_p W(u,p)-\nabla_p W(v,p)\big]\cdot p'(b)\;=\;0.\qquad(\star_{uv})$$ With a continuum of types, take two nearby active types, divide by $u-v$, and let $u\to v$. Every difference of $W$'s becomes a $v$-derivative:

the local single-crossing condition $$g(b)\,\mathcal W\big(v,p(b)\big)\;+\;G(b)\,\nabla_p\mathcal W\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b)\;=\;0 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{d}{db}\Big[\,G(b)\,\mathcal W\big(v,p(b)\big)\Big]\;=\;0 .$$

The left side is a total derivative — so the equation first-integrates on sight. In the quantile coordinate, with the constant identified by dividing (FI) itself by $u-v$:

$$\boxed{\;t\;\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;=\;U'(v)\qquad\text{for every }t\in[\,t_e(v),t_x(v)\,].\;}\qquad\text{(FI-loc)}$$

A cleaner derivation, no limit at all. $U(v)=\sup_b\big[G(b)\,W(v,p(b))-b\big]$, and $v$ enters only through $W$. By the Milgrom–Segal envelope theorem, $U'(v)=G(b)\,\partial_vW(v,p(b))$ at every maximizer $b$. Since every bid in the support of $\sigma_v$ is a maximizer, $t\,\mathcal W$ is constant $=U'(v)$ across the support. The limit computation and the envelope computation agree — the first tells you (FI-loc) is the continuum shadow of the whole discrete pair family $\{t\,D_{uv}=k_{uv}\}$; the second tells you it is exact given differentiability.

What collapsed. At each $t$ the discrete system carries $|\mathcal A(t)|-1$ pair equations. In the continuum these become the one-parameter family (FI-loc), indexed by $v\in\mathcal A(t)$ — one scalar equation per active type, against one density value $p_t(v)$ per active type. The count is square before any work is done (§6).

3. What the equation says — supports are iso-win-probability sets

The envelope inside the final gives $\mathcal W$ a name. lem:winprob

The Siegel final is itself an IPV all-pay auction: $\Pi_2(v;p,q)=\max_b\big[v\,H_2(b)-b\big]$ with $H_2$ the opponent's final-bid CDF. Envelope again: $\partial_v\Pi_2 = H_2(b_v)$ — the probability that a type-$v$ finalist wins the final. Averaging over the opponent's quantile law, $$\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;=\;\int_0^1 \Pr\big(v\ \text{wins final}\;\big|\;P(t),P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds \;=\;\Pr\big(\text{win final}\;\big|\;\text{advance at }t\big).$$ Now read (FI-loc) as economics:

the whole equation in words $$\underbrace{t}_{\Pr(\text{win R1})}\;\times\;\underbrace{\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)}_{\Pr(\text{win final}\,\mid\,\text{advance})}\;=\;\Pr\big(\text{win tournament}\big)\;=\;U'(v).$$

The identity $U'=\Pr(\text{win})$ is the standard Mirrlees/Myerson envelope condition. The content added by co-mixing is its constancy across the support: type $v$'s support is an iso-win-probability set. Bid higher and your conditional final win probability must fall exactly as $U'(v)/t$ — the signaling penalty of a high bid offsets the Round-1 gain in probability units, not payoff units. This is the continuum face of "high hides, low bluffs": a stronger reputation buys a more aggressive opponent in the final, and the equilibrium prices that aggression so that the total win probability is flat where you mix.

4. The entry curve — the ladder's continuum limit

$t_e(v)=U'(v)$, and the entering type wins the final surely. cor:entrycurve

Recall the discrete ladder (thm:floorladder). At type $v$'s entry rung $t_v$, the adjacent gap $D_{vv^-}$ has two computations that must match: below entry, $v$ is absent from the believed support, so Siegel cornering (lem:corner) gives $D_{vv^-}=v-v^-$ on the whole band; above, both types are active and the first integral gives $D_{vv^-}=(U_v-U_{v^-})/t$. Continuity at $t_v$: $$t_v\;=\;\frac{U_v-U_{v^-}}{v-v^-}$$ (unit spacing hid the denominator in the $K=3$ statement). Let the spacing $v-v^-\to0$: the difference quotient becomes a derivative.

the entry curve is the marginal rent $$\boxed{\;t_e(v)\;=\;U'(v)\;} \qquad\text{and at entry}\qquad \mathcal W\big(v,P(t_e(v))\big)\;=\;1 .$$

Each type enters at the quantile equal to its own equilibrium tournament win probability. The second display is (FI-loc) evaluated at $t=t_e(v)$ — and it is exactly the cornering value: at its entry rung, $v$ sits at the top of its own believed support, $v=a_{\max}(t_e(v))$. In an all-pay final the two bid supports share a common top $\bar b$, so the top-believed type bids $\bar b$ and wins with probability one, pointwise in the opponent's posterior — against a stronger opponent it pays a higher $\bar b$, which moves the level $\Pi_2$ but not the $v$-derivative. The two branches of the ladder match in the limit with no tension.

Two consistency dividends. (i) Differentiating: $t_e'(v)=U''(v)$ — the entry curve is increasing iff $U$ is convex, i.e. iff the tournament win probability increases in type. That is the continuum shadow of the monotone posterior (G2). (ii) The discrete rungs are literally difference quotients of $U$: at the repaired $K=3$ (unit spacing), $t_M=U_M-U_L\approx0.095$ and $t_H=U_H-U_M\approx0.333$ (cor:ladder3), and the $K=4$ floor relation $k_{\text{top}}-k_{\text{2nd}}=t_0=0.408$ is the top rung of the same ladder (lem:floorK, entry-rung reading).

What happens just above entry. As $t$ rises past $t_e(v)$, strictly higher types enter above $v$ ($a_{\max}(t)=t_e^{-1}(t)$ climbs), $v$ stops being top-of-support, and $\mathcal W$ decays from $1$ exactly as $U'(v)/t$. In the discrete case the entry crossing is kinked (the top pivot cannot vanish); in the continuum the kinks of successive entrants densify into the smooth frontier $a_{\max}(t)$ — but the local behaviour of $p_t$ near the frontier (the boundary layer) is exactly brief 2026-07-29-1.

5. Exit — the co-equal unknown that keeps getting rug-swept

Standing rule of the project, banked as rem:exitcont.

the standing rule

At $K=3$ a single type exits, and it was legitimate to demote the exit rung to an ODE output. Several project documents have since treated exit as a detail. In the continuum that habit is untenable: the exit curve $t_x(v)$ has exactly the same status as $t_e(v)$ — one full unknown function, one boundary of every type's support. A continuum treatment that specifies entry and stays silent on exit is not a closed system.

What survives of the discrete asymmetry (rem:noexit). The asymmetry is informational, and it survives:

  • Entries are algebraic and kinked. At an entry the top of the believed support moves, the cornering hypothesis $w\ge a_{\max}$ becomes satisfiable, and one relation per entry appears — in the limit, the whole curve $t_e=U'$.
  • Exits are free boundaries, and the leaver jumps. At an exit the bottom type leaves; the top of the believed support does not move, cornering never applies, and no algebraic relation is generated — that, and only that, is the asymmetry. Corrected 2026-07-30: this bullet previously added that the exit curve is located by the density vanishing, $p_t\big(a_{\min}(t)\big)=0$, crossed $C^1$. Both clauses are withdrawn (cor:exitnorm, rem:exitjump): the density does not vanish at the frontier, the exiting type's own mass leaves with a jump, and what locates the frontier is normalisation in $H$-coordinates. The $C^1$ of prop:sliver is a property of the surviving branch — it is asserted of $h$, the High share — and the formula for $p_t$ is frontier-free, so at an exit the discontinuity is the support indicator, not the profile.

So the pair of frontiers enters the system asymmetrically — $t_e$ as data (given $U$), $t_x$ as an output — of the normalisation $\int_0^{h_*(t)}(U')^{-1}(t\bar\chi(h))\,dh=1$, not of a march, which does not exist (rem:global) — but both must be carried, and in the degree-of-freedom count they cost the same: nothing (thm:contsquare). Whether $t_x(v)\lt1$ on a positive-measure set of types (genuine staggered exit, the continuum analogue of Low's exit at $t_X\approx0.68$ in the repaired $K=3$) or $t_x\equiv1$ below some threshold (the $K=2$ pattern: nobody exits) is an output, not an assumption. The two solved discrete instances realize both admissible behaviours, which is exactly why neither can be hard-wired.

A third frontier hid at the bottom — and it dissolves. The discrete equilibria carry a zero-bid atom whose carrier is the bottom type, which has measure zero in the continuum. Closed 2026-07-30 (prop:noatomcont): under (CONT) the atom carries no mass. If a positive-measure set $A$ of types bid $0$, then a positive-mass atom means $t_0=G(0)\gt0$, so with probability $\Psi(t_0)=t_0^2\gt0$ both finalists carry the atom posterior $p_0$ and the final is symmetric; there each type ties with itself, so any carrier $v\gt\inf A$ wins with probability $\mathcal W(v,p_0)=P_{p_0}(v)\gt0$ — a strictly positive atom prize, which lem:atomIC forbids. Hence the carrier is a single point and $f(A)=0$. No interval hypothesis is needed, so the either/or of rem:atomcont collapses to its first horn.

And the pressure this section used to record — that $t_e(v)=U'(v)$ with $U'(\underline v)\gt0$ strands the quantile band $[0,t_e(\underline v))$, to be filled by an atom or a boundary layer — rested on a false premise. rem:bottomsing gives $U'(\underline v)=0$ (the bottom type is covered against every opponent, so it wins the tournament with probability zero), hence $t_e(\underline v)=0$: the band is empty and there is nothing to fill. What survives of the discrete atom is a collapse — as $t\downarrow0$ the active band pinches onto $\underline v$ and $h_*(t)\to1/\underline v=H_{\delta_{\underline v}}(\underline v)$, exactly the maximal $H$-value that made the discrete pure-Low atom covered against every opponent. Sub-check (a) of brief 2026-07-29-3 therefore closes as neither (rem:atomband).

6. The Lu–Parreiras kernel — making $\mathcal W$ explicit

The envelope names $\mathcal W$; the $Q$-function machinery computes it. lem:lpwinprob, cor:lptri

Everything so far rests on the envelope theorem — and the envelope only ever yields derivatives. It says $\mathcal W$ is a win probability (§3); it does not say what that probability is as a functional of the two posteriors, and it is silent on payoff levels (the top bid at cornering, the atom prize in the zero-atom IC, the bid map). The discrete program filled that gap with Siegel's construction and the tropical cell complex. In the continuum the natural replacement is the closed-form machinery of Lu & Parreiras (2017) for two-player asymmetric all-pay auctions — which is exactly what the final is, with the posteriors $(p,q)$ as the two type distributions. (Side-by-side comparison of the two machineries: claude-ai-project/siegel_vs_lu_parreiras_comparison.v1.md.)

H-functions and the tying function. For a posterior $\mu$ on $[\underline v,\bar v]$ define the value-space H-function $$H_\mu(v)\;:=\;\int_{[v,\bar v]}\frac{d\mu(u)}{u}\qquad(\text{decreasing, }H_\mu(\bar v)=0).$$ In the LP equilibrium, bids are increasing in type on each side, at most one side carries a zero-bid atom (the side with the larger $H$ at the bottom: $C=H_p(\underline v)-H_q(\underline v)$ decides), and above the atom coverage each type $v$ ties — bids the same — as the opponent type $y_v$ solving the H-equality $$H_q(y_v)\;=\;H_p(v).$$ Because bids increase in type, $v$ wins exactly against opponent types below its tie:

the tying function is the win-probability map $$\boxed{\;\Pr\big(v\ \text{wins final}\;\big|\;p,q\big)\;=\;P_q\big(H_q^{-1}(H_p(v))\big)\;}$$

where $P_q$ is the CDF of the opponent posterior $q$ (not to be confused with the posterior curve $P(t)$). Types whose tie lands inside the opponent's atom-covered range win with probability $0$; the coverage cutoff is LP's $H_A(y^*)=H_N(0)$. $\Psi$-averaging over the opponent's quantile as in §3:

$$\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;=\;\int_0^1 P_{q_s}\!\big(H_{q_s}^{-1}(H_{p_t}(v))\big)\,2s\,ds, \qquad p_t=P(t),\ q_s=P(s),$$

with the $s$-integral split wherever $v$'s tie enters atom coverage — the continuum image of the discrete regime census. Levels too, not just slopes: $\Pi_2(v;p,q)=v\,P_q(H_q^{-1}(H_p(v)))-b(v)$ with the LP bid map $b(v)=\int_{[\,\cdot\,\le v]}y_u\,dp(u)$ — so both the derivative (the envelope's win probability) and the level (which the envelope alone cannot give) are closed-form. lem:lpwinprob

Two one-line consequences (cor:lptri):

  1. The Volterra property is an identity. $H_p(v)$ integrates $p$ only over $[v,\bar v]$ — it is blind to the believed density below $v$. So at any $v$ above the atom coverage, $$\gamma(v,w)\;:=\;\frac{\delta\mathcal W(v,P)}{\delta p(w)}\;=\;0\qquad(w\lt v).$$ This is the continuum shadow of (TRI) (conj:triK) proved, not sampled — the very property §7 needs.
  2. Cornering, hence $\mathcal W=1$ at entry. If $v\ge a_{\max}$ (top of own believed support) then $H_p(v)=0$, the tie lands at the top of the opponent's support, and the win probability is $P_q(\bar v_q)=1$ pointwise in $q$ — lem:corner and the entry condition of §4, in one line.

The kernel drops out by chain rule. From the definition, $\delta H_p(v)/\delta p(w)=\mathbf 1_{\{w\ge v\}}/w$, so differentiating the boxed formula through the $\Psi$-average:

the explicit cascade kernel $$\gamma(v,w)\;=\;\bar\chi'\big(H_{p_t}(v)\big)\,\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{w\ge v\}}}{w}, \qquad \chi_s:=P_{q_s}\circ H_{q_s}^{-1},\quad \bar\chi'(h):=\int_0^1\chi_s'(h)\,2s\,ds\;\;(\lt 0).$$

A bounded kernel with a single jump at the diagonal $w=v$ — no Dirac mass. The sign is right: adding believed mass above $v$ lowers $v$'s win probability. This is the candidate answer to brief 2's diagonal-scaling question; §7 spells out what it means for the march.

7. The Volterra outlook — the cascade in the limit

Kernel explicit via §6; the bookkeeping is brief 2026-07-29-2. rem:volterra

Differentiate (FI-loc) in $t$, one equation per active $v$ — the $s$-slot of the $\Psi$-average integrates over all $s$ and does not move with $t$, so only the own-posterior slot differentiates: $$\int_v^{a_{\max}(t)}\gamma(v,w)\,\partial_t p_t(w)\,dw\;=\;-\,\frac{U'(v)}{t^2},$$ where $\gamma(v,w)=\delta\mathcal W(v,P)/\delta p(w)$ is the §6 kernel — the continuum image of the cascade matrix $\Gamma(t)$ (thm:tricascade) — and the lower limit $w=v$ is the Volterra property, proved in §6. How the two discrete structures pass to the limit:

  1. (TRI) becomes the Volterra property — proved. The level form (lem:tricell3, conj:triK) — shifting believed mass onto $u_j$ moves $\Pi_2(v)$ by the same amount for every $v\ge u_j$ — becomes $\gamma(v,w)=0$ for $w\lt v$, and via LP this is the identity of §6: $H_p(v)$ is blind to $p$ below $v$. The route recorded for the discrete conjecture (in Siegel's top-down sweep, own mass below $v$ enters the opponent's CDF only through its total) is the same argument in Siegel clothing.
  2. The $\psi$-free spike is the shadow of a jump, not of a Dirac delta. In the $\psi$-free form (lem:psifree), $\Gamma=\bar G^\top L^\top$ with $L$ the fixed neighbour-differencing operator, and differencing an indicator gives one spike: $L\,\mathbf 1_{\{v\ge u_j\}}=\varepsilon_j$, which is what puts the pivot on the diagonal. In the limit the kernel itself keeps the indicator: $\gamma\propto\mathbf 1_{\{w\ge v\}}/w$, a bounded jump at the diagonal. A Dirac mass appears only when the equation is differentiated once more in $v$ — the continuum image of adjacent-pair differencing, which is exactly the operation that produced the discrete pivot.
the form of the march

As written above, the system is first-kind Volterra with the jump kernel of §6. One further differentiation in $v$ converts the jump into a local term ($a_{\max}(t)$ does not depend on $v$):

$$-\,\gamma(v,v)\,\partial_t p_t(v)\;+\;\int_v^{a_{\max}(t)}\partial_v\gamma(v,w)\,\partial_t p_t(w)\,dw \;=\;-\,\frac{U''(v)}{t^{2}}, \qquad \gamma(v,v)=\frac{\bar\chi'\big(H_{p_t}(v)\big)}{v}\;\neq\;0:$$

a second-kind Volterra integro-differential system with a nonvanishing diagonal coefficient, marched downward from the top $v=a_{\max}(t)$ — the continuum image of the triangular cascade's column-by-column march, with the top-type monotonicity (cor:topmono) as the boundary behaviour. Left to brief 2026-07-29-2: the role/atom bookkeeping through the $\Psi$-average (the $s$-split points of §6 move with $p_t$ — Leibniz terms), the entry-flux boundary terms at $w=a_{\max}(t)$, the limit interchange, the behaviour of $\bar\chi'$ at the exit frontier $w\to a_{\min}(t)$ (standing rule of §5), and a machine gate against the discrete $\Gamma$ from siegel_ipv.py.

The three briefs (all docs/tasks/2026-07-29-*.md, each self-contained with pointers):

  1. Entry/exit boundary layer. Do $\{t_e=U',\ \mathcal W=1\text{ at entry}\}$ pin the local behaviour of $p_t$ at the entry frontier without integrating the interior — via the one-Taylor-step LP expansion $1-\mathcal W\approx|\bar\chi'(0)|\,H_{p_t}(v)$ — and what is the (weaker) condition at the exit frontier? Entry and exit treated symmetrically, per the standing rule.
  2. The Volterra kernel — verify. The candidate kernel, the Volterra property, and the diagonal verdict (first-kind as written, second-kind after $\partial_v$) are banked from §6; the brief closes the gaps: role/Leibniz bookkeeping through the $\Psi$-average, entry-flux boundary terms, the limit interchange, and the machine gate against $\Gamma$ at $K\in\{3,5\}$ via siegel_ipv.py.
  3. Well-posedness — CLOSED 2026-07-30, and squareness holds. The candidate coordinates were the right ones, but they do more than linearise the tying relation: in $(v,h)$ the joint law of (type, rung) factorises. Differentiating (MASTER) at fixed $v$ gives $dt=t\,\bar y/\bar\chi\,dh$, which cancels the $t\,\bar y$ in (DENS), so $\tilde\rho(v,h)=\big[v\,U''(v)\big]\cdot\big[1/\bar\chi(h)\big]$ on $\{0\le h\le H(v)\}$ — a product measure restricted to a region, with $\bar y$, the one object coupling different types, gone (lem:hcoords). Then the count is clean: three unknown functions $U',\bar\chi,h_*$ against one algebraic relation (normalisation, costing no constant) and two first-order ODEs (costing one each), with the two constants supplied by the already-proved $\bar\chi(0)=1$ and $U'(\underline v)=0$. Square (thm:contsquare). Sub-checks: the atom is neither mass nor boundary layer; exit is carried as a full unknown function and still costs nothing, because its equation is algebraic; the discrete $K$ mirror is $K-1$ rent differences against $K$ prior conditions minus the redundancy $\sum_vP_v\equiv1$, with the atom entering as a matched unknown/equation pair (rem:discretecount). Two things the brief got wrong and this corrects: the anchor $U(\underline v)=0$ does not close the system — it never appears in it — and there is no march to find coordinates for (rem:global). What squareness does not give is existence; the right remaining target is a fixed point in $(U',\bar\chi,h_*)$.
