Everything here is either exact under stated regularity hypotheses (the envelope arguments, the Lu–Parreiras machinery) or a formal limit of a banked discrete relation (the ladder, the cascade). The continuum equilibrium is not constructed, but it is now pinned, and the pinning is much sharper than the first draft of this note anticipated:

  • the cascade kernel is not merely triangular, it is explicitly invertible, so there is no march to perform — the posterior density has a closed form (prop:kernelinv);
  • the exit frontier is located, not by a vanishing density, but by normalisation in the right coordinate (cor:exitnorm);

1. Setup — what carries over verbatim

One paragraph of model, one of notation, and a reference table for the symbols that do the work later.

Model. Four players, two rounds. Round 1: two pairwise all-pay auctions; all bids publicly revealed. Round 2: the two winners meet in an all-pay final worth $v$ to its winner. Types are now i.i.d. draws from a density $f$ on $[\underline v,\bar v]$, private throughout.

Notation (CONT), def:cont. Work in the bid quantile $t=G(b)$, so that $t$ is the probability of winning Round 1 against a single symmetric opponent. The unknown is the posterior density $p_t(\cdot)$ — the continuum version of the posterior curve $P(t)$: the density over types of a bidder observed at quantile $t$. Rents are $U(v)$, anchored at $U(\underline v)=0$. The prize is $$W\big(v,P(t)\big)=\int_0^1 \Pi_2\big(v;P(t),P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds,$$ with $\Pi_2$ the Siegel final payoff and $2s\,ds$ the strategy-free opponent-quantile law $\Psi(t)=t^2$ (lem:sigmainv).

Where $2s\,ds$ comes from, since it recurs on every page. Your opponent in the final is the winner of the other pair. Both members of that pair draw their quantile uniformly on $[0,1]$ and the higher bidder advances, so the finalist's quantile is the maximum of two independent uniforms: CDF $\Psi(s)=s^2$, density $2s$. Nothing in that computation refers to types or to strategies, which is exactly why it survives every change of architecture — the content of lem:sigmainv. Every average over "the opponent you will face" is therefore $\int_0^1(\cdot)\,2s\,ds$, weighted toward high rungs.

Each type mixes over one quantile interval $$\operatorname{supp}\sigma_v=[\,t_e(v),\,t_x(v)\,],$$ the entry curve and the exit curve — both unknowns of the system (§5). The active set at $t$ is an interval of types (continuum (INT)): $\mathcal A(t)=[a_{\min}(t),a_{\max}(t)]$, with $a_{\max}=t_e^{-1}$ and $a_{\min}=t_x^{-1}$.

The one new symbol. Everything in §§2–5 is organized around the own-type slope of the prize: $$\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;:=\;\partial_v W\big(v,P(t)\big).$$

2. The limit computation — one division, one limit

From the pair conditions to a local condition. lem:filoc

The discrete solve rests on the pair relations: the first integrals (FI), $t\,D_{uv}(t)=k_{uv}=U_u-U_v$, and their bid-space derivative, the single-crossing condition $$g(b)\big[W(u,p)-W(v,p)\big]\;+\;G(b)\big[\nabla_p W(u,p)-\nabla_p W(v,p)\big]\cdot p'(b)\;=\;0.\qquad(\star_{uv})$$ With a continuum of types, take two nearby active types, divide by $u-v$, and let $u\to v$. Every difference of $W$'s becomes a $v$-derivative:

the local single-crossing condition $$g(b)\,\mathcal W\big(v,p(b)\big)\;+\;G(b)\,\nabla_p\mathcal W\big(v,p(b)\big)\cdot p'(b)\;=\;0 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{d}{db}\Big[\,G(b)\,\mathcal W\big(v,p(b)\big)\Big]\;=\;0 .$$

The left side is a total derivative — so the equation first-integrates on sight. In the quantile coordinate, with the constant identified by dividing (FI) itself by $u-v$:

$$\boxed{\;t\;\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;=\;U'(v)\qquad\text{for every }t\in[\,t_e(v),t_x(v)\,].\;}\qquad\text{(FI-loc)}$$

A cleaner derivation, no limit at all. $U(v)=\sup_b\big[G(b)\,W(v,p(b))-b\big]$, and $v$ enters only through $W$. By the Milgrom–Segal envelope theorem — which applies at an arbitrary maximizer, not only at a unique one, and this is exactly what a mixed strategy needs — $U'(v)=G(b)\,\partial_vW(v,p(b))$ at every optimal $b$. Since every bid in the support of $\sigma_v$ is optimal, $t\,\mathcal W$ is constant $=U'(v)$ across the support. The two derivations do different jobs: the limit computation tells you (FI-loc) is the continuum shadow of the whole discrete pair family $\{t\,D_{uv}=k_{uv}\}$; the envelope computation tells you it is exact given differentiability, with no limit interchange to justify. That second point matters later — it is why prop:kernelinv can differentiate (FI-loc) freely.

What collapsed. At each $t$ the discrete system carries $|\mathcal A(t)|-1$ pair equations. In the continuum these become the one-parameter family (FI-loc), indexed by $v\in\mathcal A(t)$ — one scalar equation per active type, against one density value $p_t(v)$ per active type. The count is square before any work is done, and §10 shows it stays square after the work is done.

3. What the equation says — supports are iso-win-probability sets

The envelope inside the final gives $\mathcal W$ a name. lem:winprob

The Siegel final is itself an IPV all-pay auction: $\Pi_2(v;p,q)=\max_b\big[v\,H_2(b)-b\big]$ with $H_2$ the opponent's final-bid CDF. Envelope again: $\partial_v\Pi_2 = H_2(b_v)$ — the probability that a type-$v$ finalist wins the final. Averaging over the opponent's quantile law, $$\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;=\;\int_0^1 \Pr\big(v\ \text{wins final}\;\big|\;P(t),P(s)\big)\,2s\,ds \;=\;\Pr\big(\text{win final}\;\big|\;\text{advance at }t\big).$$ Now read (FI-loc) as economics:

the whole equation in words $$\underbrace{t}_{\Pr(\text{win R1})}\;\times\;\underbrace{\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)}_{\Pr(\text{win final}\,\mid\,\text{advance})}\;=\;\Pr\big(\text{win tournament}\big)\;=\;U'(v).$$

The identity $U'=\Pr(\text{win})$ is the standard Mirrlees/Myerson envelope condition. The content added by co-mixing is its constancy across the support: type $v$'s support is an iso-win-probability set. Bid higher and your conditional final win probability must fall exactly as $U'(v)/t$ — the signaling penalty of a high bid offsets the Round-1 gain in probability units, not payoff units. This is the continuum face of "high hides, low bluffs": a stronger reputation buys a more aggressive opponent in the final, and the equilibrium prices that aggression so that the total win probability is flat where you mix.

4. The entry curve — the ladder's continuum limit

$t_e(v)=U'(v)$, and the entering type wins the final surely. cor:entrycurve

Recall the discrete ladder (thm:floorladder). At type $v$'s entry rung $t_v$, the adjacent gap $D_{vv^-}$ has two computations that must match: below entry, $v$ is absent from the believed support, so Siegel cornering (lem:corner) gives $D_{vv^-}=v-v^-$ on the whole band; above, both types are active and the first integral gives $D_{vv^-}=(U_v-U_{v^-})/t$. Continuity at $t_v$: $$t_v\;=\;\frac{U_v-U_{v^-}}{v-v^-}$$ (unit spacing hid the denominator in the $K=3$ statement). Let the spacing $v-v^-\to0$: the difference quotient becomes a derivative.

the entry curve is the marginal rent $$\boxed{\;t_e(v)\;=\;U'(v)\;} \qquad\text{and at entry}\qquad \mathcal W\big(v,P(t_e(v))\big)\;=\;1 .$$

Each type enters at the quantile equal to its own equilibrium tournament win probability. The second display is (FI-loc) evaluated at $t=t_e(v)$ — and it is exactly the cornering value: at its entry rung, $v$ sits at the top of its own believed support, $v=a_{\max}(t_e(v))$. In an all-pay final the two bid supports share a common top $\bar b$, so the top-believed type bids $\bar b$ and wins with probability one, pointwise in the opponent's posterior— against a stronger opponent it pays a higher $\bar b$, which moves the level $\Pi_2$ but not the $v$-derivative. The two branches of the ladder match in the limit with no tension.

Two consistency dividends. (i) Differentiating: $t_e'(v)=U''(v)$ — the entry curve is increasing iff $U$ is convex, i.e. iff the tournament win probability increases in type. That is the continuum shadow of the monotone posterior (G2). (ii) The discrete rungs are literally difference quotients of $U$, and both solved instances confirm it exactly. At the repaired $K=3$ (unit spacing, cor:k3constants), $$t_M=U_M-U_L=0.095\,980,\qquad t_H=U_H-U_M=0.331\,287;$$ at the solved $K=4$ with $v=(4,3,2,1)$ (thm:k4closure), $$t_2=0.046\,361,\qquad t_3=0.195\,230,\qquad t_4=0.409\,663,$$ whence $U=(0,\;0.046\,361,\;0.241\,591,\;0.651\,254)$ by summation — the ladder $U_v=\sum t$ over entries at or below $v$ (lem:floorK, entry-rung reading). Each rung is the increment of $U$ across one value step, which is the discrete statement of $t_e=U'$.

An earlier version of this paragraph quoted $t_0=0.408$ at $K=4$, from the constructed curve of prop:k4curve. That curve is refuted as an equilibrium object (cor:atomICfail — the composite Low$+$Mid atom violates zero-atom IC), and its constants must never be quoted as equilibrium values. The live number, $t_4=0.409\,663$, is close enough to the dead one that the substitution will not be caught by eye; it is caught only by checking the label. lem:floorK itself is unaffected — the relation survives the repair, only the numbers changed.

What happens just above entry. As $t$ rises past $t_e(v)$, strictly higher types enter above $v$ ($a_{\max}(t)=t_e^{-1}(t)$ climbs), $v$ stops being top-of-support, and $\mathcal W$ decays from $1$ exactly as $U'(v)/t$. In the discrete case the entry crossing is kinked — the entry exponent is $\alpha=0$, so the share's density jumps at entry with no smooth take-off (prop:k3cascade) — and in the continuum the kinks of successive entrants densify into the smooth frontier $a_{\max}(t)$. The local behaviour of $p_t$ near that frontier, the entry boundary layer, is still open; §10 lists it.

5. Exit — the co-equal unknown that keeps getting rug-swept

Standing rule of the project, banked as rem:exitcont.

the standing rule

At $K=3$ a single type exits, and it was legitimate to demote the exit rung to an ODE output. Several project documents have since treated exit as a detail. In the continuum that habit is untenable: the exit curve $t_x(v)$ has exactly the same status as $t_e(v)$ — one full unknown function, one boundary of every type's support. A continuum treatment that specifies entry and stays silent on exit is not a closed system.

What survives of the discrete asymmetry (rem:noexit). The asymmetry is informational, and it survives — but it is an asymmetry in which crossings generate equations, not in which crossings are smooth:

  • Entries are algebraic. At an entry the top of the believed support moves, the cornering hypothesis $w\ge a_{\max}$ of lem:corner becomes satisfiable, and one relation per entry appears — in the limit, the whole curve $t_e=U'$.
  • Exits are free boundaries. At an exit the bottom type leaves; the top of the believed support does not move, cornering never applies, and no algebraic relation is generated. A free boundary is a boundary of the region on which the equations hold whose location is itself unknown and must be determined by an extra condition — the standard example is the exercise boundary of an American option. That extra condition is the whole difficulty, and it is not supplied by the pair relations.

Which extra condition? The first draft of this note guessed the natural one — that the density vanishes at the frontier, $p_t\big(a_{\min}(t)\big)=0$, crossed $C^1$. That guess is wrong and is withdrawn (cor:exitnorm, rem:exitjump): the density does not vanish there, and the exiting type's own mass leaves with a jump. What locates the frontier is normalisation in the right coordinate — but stating that requires a closed form for $p_t$, which we do not have yet. So this section can only pose the question.

three questions deferred to §8

All three are questions about a frontier, and all three need machinery §§6–7 will build. They are answered, in order, in §8.

  1. Where is the exit frontier? The free boundary $t_x$, equivalently $a_{\min}$. → §8(a), located by normalisation.
  2. What happens to the leaver? Jump or smooth vanishing — and how that squares with the $C^1$ exit asserted elsewhere in the project. → §8(b): both are true, of different objects.
  3. What happens at the bottom? The discrete equilibria carry a zero-bid atom whose carrier is the bottom type, which has measure zero in the continuum. Does an atom survive, and if the entry curve starts at $t_e(\underline v)=U'(\underline v)\gt0$, who bids in the stranded quantile band $[0,t_e(\underline v))$? → §8(d)–(e): nobody, because the premise is false.

What §10 will add to the standing rule is sharper than "carry it as an unknown": the exit frontier turns out to cost no integration constant, because its equation is algebraic and pointwise in $t$. Entry and exit are asymmetric in the relations they offer and symmetric in the degrees of freedom they consume. Whether $t_x(v)\lt1$ on a positive-measure set of types (genuine staggered exit, the continuum analogue of Low's exit at $t_X=0.860\,801$ in the solved $K=3$, cor:k3constants) or $t_x\equiv1$ below some threshold (the $K=2$ pattern: nobody exits) is an output, not an assumption. The two solved discrete instances realize both admissible behaviours, which is exactly why neither can be hard-wired.

6. The Lu–Parreiras machinery — $H$-levels, the tying value, and (PAIR)

The envelope names $\mathcal W$; the $H$-function machinery computes it — and its derivative turns out to be an elementary object. lem:lpwinprob, lem:tievalue, cor:lptri

Everything so far rests on the envelope theorem — and the envelope only ever yields derivatives. It says $\mathcal W$ is a win probability (§3); it does not say what that probability is as a functional of the two posteriors, and it is silent on payoff levels (the top bid at cornering, the atom prize in the zero-atom IC, the bid map). The discrete program filled that gap with Siegel's construction and the tropical cell complex. In the continuum the natural replacement is the closed-form machinery of Lu & Parreiras (2017) for two-player asymmetric all-pay auctions — which is exactly what the final is, with the posteriors $(p,q)$ as the two type distributions.

6.1 $H$-functions: the coordinate in which the final is trivial

For a probability measure $\mu$ on $[\underline v,\bar v]$ define the value-space $H$-function $$H_\mu(v)\;:=\;\int_{[v,\bar v]}\frac{d\mu(u)}{u}\qquad(\text{decreasing in }v,\ \ H_\mu(\bar v)=0).$$

What an $H$-function is, for a reader meeting one for the first time. It is not a CDF and it is not a hazard rate. It is the inverse-value-weighted mass above $v$, and it is the conserved coordinate of the asymmetric all-pay equilibrium: the two sides' first-order conditions, written in percentile coordinates, say precisely that $H$ is equalised across the two bidders at any common bid. Three properties make it the natural state variable here:

  • It is a reputation index. $H_\mu(v)=0$ means $v$ is at the top of the believed support — nobody is believed to be above you; large $H_\mu(v)$ means a lot of believed mass sits above you, and (because of the $1/u$ weight) that mass is cheap mass, concentrated on low types. The maximum possible value is $H=1/\underline v$, attained only when the belief is the point mass $\delta_{\underline v}$.
  • It is blind downward. $H_\mu(v)$ integrates $\mu$ only over $[v,\bar v]$. Beliefs about types below $v$ are invisible to it. This one line is the whole triangularity story (§6.4).
  • It converts the two-sided equilibrium into one equation. Two types, one on each side, submit the same bid — tie — exactly when they share an $H$-level.

In the Lu–Parreiras equilibrium, bids are increasing in type on each side; at most one side carries a zero-bid atom (the side with the larger $H$ at the bottom — the sign of $C=H_p(\underline v)-H_q(\underline v)$ decides); and above the atom coverage each type $v$ ties with the opponent type $y_v$ solving the $H$-equality $$H_q(y_v)\;=\;H_p(v).$$ Because bids increase in type, $v$ wins exactly against opponent types below its tie. Hence:

the tying function is the win-probability map $$\boxed{\;\Pr\big(v\ \text{wins final}\;\big|\;p,q\big)\;=\;P_q\big(H_q^{-1}(H_p(v))\big)\;}$$

where $P_q$ is the CDF of the opponent posterior $q$ (not to be confused with the posterior curve $P(t)$). Types whose tie lands inside the opponent's atom-covered range win with probability $0$. $\Psi$-averaging over the opponent's quantile as in §3:

$$\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)\;=\;\int_0^1 P_{q_s}\!\big(H_{q_s}^{-1}(H_{p_t}(v))\big)\,2s\,ds, \qquad p_t=P(t),\ q_s=P(s),$$

with the $s$-integral split wherever $v$'s tie enters atom coverage — the continuum image of the discrete regime census. Levels too, not just slopes: $\Pi_2(v;p,q)=v\,P_q(H_q^{-1}(H_p(v)))-b(v)$ with the Lu–Parreiras bid map $b(v)=\int_{[\,\cdot\,\le v]}y_u\,dp(u)$ — so both the derivative (the envelope's win probability) and the level (which the envelope alone cannot give) are closed-form. lem:lpwinprob

6.2 The tying value is the derivative of the win probability

Two lines, and they carry the rest of the note. lem:tievalue

Fix an opponent posterior $q$ and give the two objects inside the box their own names, as functions of the $H$-level $h$ rather than of the type: $$y_q(h):=H_q^{-1}(h)\quad(\text{the \textbf{tying value}}),\qquad \chi_q(h):=P_q\big(y_q(h)\big)\quad(\text{the \textbf{win probability}}),$$ both extended by $0$ for $h\gt H_q(\underline v_q)$ — the atom-covered range, where your tie would have to sit below the opponent's lowest type and you win nothing. Then $\chi_q$ is Lipschitz and decreasing with $\chi_q(0)=1$, and:

the identity everything downstream runs on $$\boxed{\;\chi_q'(h)\;=\;-\,y_q(h).\;}$$

The marginal cost of reputation is the type you tie with. Raise your believed $H$-level by $dh$ — look one notch weaker — and your final win probability falls by exactly the value of the opponent you were tying with. Both sides are pure equilibrium objects, and the relation holds for every opponent posterior, discrete or continuous.

Derivation, in two differentiations and one cancellation.

  1. Differentiate the $H$-function in its argument. With $q$ also denoting the density, $H_q(y)=\int_{[y,\bar v]}dq(u)/u$ gives $H_q'(y)=-q(y)/y$.
  2. Differentiate the CDF: $P_q'(y)=q(y)$.
  3. Divide, using $\chi_q=P_q\circ H_q^{-1}$: $$\frac{d\chi_q}{dh}=\frac{dP_q}{dH_q}=\frac{q(y)}{-q(y)/y}=-\,y .$$ The density cancels. That is the whole point: the identity carries no information about $q$ beyond the tying value itself, so it survives every reparametrisation and — crucially — every discretisation. For a step measure $q$ it is the statement that the piecewise-linear percentile $H$-function has slope $-v_q(x)$. Past coverage both sides are $0$.

6.3 (PAIR): the payoff difference, with no hypothesis at all

The boxed formula of §6.1 carries a hypothesis — $v$'s tie must land above the opponent's atom coverage. The difference form does not:

(PAIR) — unconditional $$\boxed{\;\Pi_2(v;p,q)-\Pi_2(w;p,q)\;=\;\int_w^v\chi_q\big(H_p(x)\big)\,dx\;}$$

for any $w\lt v$ — adjacent or not, on the grid or off it — with no assumption on atom coverage, spacing, or support. For a discrete $p$ it collapses to $\sum(u_k-u_{k-1})\chi_q(H_p(u_k))$ over grid types in $(w,v]$, and to the single term $(v-w)\chi_q(H_p(v))$ for an adjacent pair.

Why it is unconditional, which is worth seeing because the exception one expects is the one that does not occur. There is a one-line proof and an elementary one, and both earn their keep.

The one-line proof. $\Pi_2(x;p,q)=\max_b[x\,H_2(b)-b]$ is a maximum of affine functions of $x$, hence convex and locally Lipschitz, so it equals the integral of its a.e. derivative; by the envelope theorem that derivative is the win probability at $x$'s optimal bid, which §6.1 evaluates as $\chi_q(H_p(x))$. This already covers $x\notin\operatorname{supp}p$ and atom-covered $x$.

The elementary proof, case by case, shows why no hypothesis is needed. Let $u\gt v$ be adjacent in $\operatorname{supp}p$ and let $b_j$ be the bid at the percentile boundary the two types share. Every bid in a type's range is optimal, so when both ranges are nondegenerate $\Pi_2(u)=u\,H_2(b_j)-b_j$ and $\Pi_2(v)=v\,H_2(b_j)-b_j$; subtracting gives $(u-v)H_2(b_j)$, and $H_2(b_j)=\chi_q(H_p(u))$ by the tying identity. Three degenerate configurations remain:

  • Both types covered. $\Pi_2(u)=\Pi_2(v)=0$ and $H_p(u)$ exceeds $q$'s total $H$-mass, so $\chi_q=0$. Both sides vanish. ✔
  • $v$ partly covered, $u$ not — the straddling pair, the only delicate case. Here $v$'s percentile range contains the atom cutoff, so $v$ is indifferent between bidding the atom (payoff $0$) and its active bids. Indifference forces $v\,H_2(b_j)=b_j$ exactly. Therefore $\Pi_2(u)-\Pi_2(v)=u\,H_2(b_j)-b_j-0=(u-v)H_2(b_j)$ once more. ✔ This case was predicted to fail and does not — the machine gate isolates $2{,}936$ straddling pairs and they pass at $8.9\times10^{-16}$.
  • $v$ fully covered. Forces $u$ covered as well, which is case one. ✔

So the hypothesis "$v$ tied above the atom-covered range" in lem:lpwinprob is needed only for the level statement. The difference — hence, in the limit, the derivative $\mathcal W$ — needs no hypothesis at all. Everything in §§7–10 is built on differences, and therefore inherits none.

6.4 Two one-line consequences, and one discrete theorem

(i) The Volterra property is an identity (cor:lptri). $H_p(v)$ integrates $p$ only over $[v,\bar v]$ — it is blind to the believed density below $v$. So at any $v$ above the atom coverage, $$\gamma(v,w)\;:=\;\frac{\delta\mathcal W(v,P)}{\delta p(w)}\;=\;0\qquad(w\lt v).$$ Here $\delta/\delta p(w)$ is the functional (Gâteaux) derivative: the effect on $\mathcal W(v,\cdot)$ of adding an infinitesimal sliver of believed mass at type $w$. The property that this vanishes for $w\lt v$ is what makes the system Volterra rather than Fredholm — see §7.

(ii) Cornering, hence $\mathcal W=1$ at entry. If $v\ge a_{\max}$ (top of own believed support) then $H_p(v)=0$, the tie lands at the top of the opponent's support, and the win probability is $P_q(\bar v_q)=1$ pointwise in $q$ — lem:corner and the entry condition of §4, in one line.

(TRI) is now a theorem at every $K$ — not a conjecture

The discrete original of (i) used to be the conjecture (TRI): shifting believed mass onto $u_j$ moves $\Pi_2(v)$ by the same amount for every $v\ge u_j$. Run the same argument on (PAIR) — the chart direction $e_j$ moves mass from $u_1$ to $u_j$, so $H_p(x)$ is unchanged for every $x\gt u_j$, so the $e_j$-derivative of $\Pi_2(u)=\Pi_2(u_1)+\int_{u_1}^{u_j}+\int_{u_j}^{u}$ is independent of $u$ for $u\ge u_j$ — and the conjecture becomes a theorem, with the gradient explicit:

$$\Gamma_{ji}=\frac{\partial D_{u_iu_{i-1}}}{\partial e_j} =-\,(u_i-u_{i-1})\;y_q\big(H_p(u_i)\big)\;\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{j\ge i\}}}{u_j}, \qquad \Gamma_{ii}=-\,\frac{(u_i-u_{i-1})\,y_q(H_p(u_i))}{u_i}\;\lt\;0 ,$$

for every $K$, every value vector, every pair of posteriors and every cell of the Round-2 complex — no assumption on spacing, on which types carry mass, or on the cell. The pivot vanishes exactly when $u_i$ is atom-covered against $q$. thm:triLP (the label conj:triK is retained only as an alias). Note the tying value $y_q$ appearing as the pivot: the discrete cascade matrix is built from §6.2's identity, which is why the same object governs both theories.

7. The cascade kernel, certified — and why there is no march

The kernel is explicit, its boundary term is real and pinned, and the operator inverts. lem:volterrakernel, prop:kernelinv

7.1 The $\Psi$-averaged objects

Write $h_v(t):=H_{p_t}(v)$ for type $v$'s own $H$-level at rung $t$ — from here on the state variable — and average §6.2's two objects over the opponent's quantile law: $$\bar\chi(h):=\int_0^1\!\chi_{q_s}(h)\,2s\,ds,\qquad \bar y(h):=\int_{S(h)}\!y_{q_s}(h)\,2s\,ds,\qquad S(h):=\{s:\,h\le H_{q_s}(a_{\min}(s))\},$$ where $q_s:=P(s)$. The set $S(h)$ is the set of opponent rungs against which level $h$ is not atom-covered — outside it there is no tie to speak of, so nothing to average. Then §6.1 and §6.2 give at once $$\mathcal W\big(v,P(t)\big)=\bar\chi\big(h_v(t)\big),\qquad \bar\chi(0)=1,\qquad \bar\chi'=-\bar y\lt0 ,$$ and (FI-loc) becomes a scalar relation between the state and the rung: $$\text{(MASTER)}\qquad t\;\bar\chi\big(h_v(t)\big)\;=\;U'(v).$$ This is the single most useful equation in the note. Everything below is (MASTER) differentiated in one variable or the other.

7.2 The kernel, by chain rule

From the definition, $\delta H_p(v)/\delta p(w)=\mathbf 1_{\{w\ge v\}}/w$ — adding mass at $w$ raises $v$'s $H$-level iff $w$ sits above $v$, by the weight $1/w$. Composing with $\bar\chi'=-\bar y$:

the explicit cascade kernel $$\boxed{\;\gamma(v,w)\;=\;\frac{\delta\mathcal W(v,P)}{\delta p(w)} \;=\;-\,\bar y\big(h_v(t)\big)\,\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{w\ge v\}}}{w}\;}$$

Bounded (by $\bar v/\underline v$), vanishing below the diagonal (§6.4(i)), with a single jump at $w=v$ and no Dirac mass. The sign is right: believed mass added above $v$ lowers $v$'s win probability, and it does so at the rate of the tying value.

The system, with the term the first draft omitted. Differentiate (MASTER) in $t$ at fixed $v$. The $s$-slot of the $\Psi$-average integrates over all $s$ and does not move with $t$, so only the own-posterior slot differentiates; and $h_v(t)=\int_v^{a_{\max}(t)}p_t(w)\,dw/w$ carries a moving upper limit. Leibniz:

$$\partial_th_v(t)=\underbrace{\int_v^{a_{\max}(t)}\frac{\partial_tp_t(w)}{w}\,dw}_{\text{cascade}} \;+\;\underbrace{\frac{p_t\big(a_{\max}(t)\big)\,\dot a_{\max}(t)}{a_{\max}(t)}}_{\text{entry flux}},$$ $$\text{so}\qquad \bar\chi'\big(h_v(t)\big)\Big[\text{cascade}+\text{entry flux}\Big]\;=\;-\,\frac{U'(v)}{t^{2}} \qquad\text{(one equation per active }v).$$
correction — the entry flux is not optional

The first draft of this note wrote the displayed equation without the entry-flux term, i.e. as $\int_v^{a_{\max}}\gamma(v,w)\,\partial_tp_t(w)\,dw=-U'(v)/t^2$. That is wrong: $p_t(a_{\max}(t))\gt0$, because the entering type carries zero mass but positive density — which is precisely why entry is continuous in $P$ yet kinked. The omission is not harmless bookkeeping, and §7.4 shows it is in fact a consistency check the structure passes.

7.3 Two bookkeeping items, both settled

(i) No Leibniz terms from the moving atom-coverage split. The $s$-integral defining $\bar y$ does split — rungs in $S(h)$ contribute a tie, rungs outside contribute $0$ — and the split point does move with $p_t$. One expects a Leibniz term. There is none, because the integrand is continuous across the split: as $h\uparrow H_{q_s}(a_{\min}(s))$ the tying value reaches $a_{\min}(s)$, the bottom of the opponent's support, and $\chi_s\to P_{q_s}(a_{\min}(s))=0$ — exactly the value of the extension. Under (CONT) every $q_s$ is a density, so it has no atom at the bottom of its support and this is exact. The boundary is a zero of the integrand, not a jump of it.

why the discrete theory cannot use derivatives

The contrast is instructive and explains a structural feature of the whole project. A bottom atom of mass $\alpha\gt0$ in $q_s$ would make $\chi_s$ jump by $\alpha$ at coverage, putting a Dirac into $\bar\chi''$. That is exactly the discrete situation — discrete posteriors are atomic by construction — and exactly why the discrete theory runs on the finite differences of §6.2's (PAIR) rather than on derivatives of $\bar\chi$. Same identity, two regularity regimes.

(ii) The entry flux is real, and its value is not free. Evaluate the displayed system at the top active type $v=a_{\max}(t)$. There the cascade integral is empty and $h_{a_{\max}}=0$, so $\bar\chi'(0)=-\bar y(0)$ and the equation reads $-\bar y(0)\cdot(\text{entry flux})=-U'(a_{\max}(t))/t^2$. But $U'(a_{\max}(t))=t$ by the entry curve (§4). Hence

$$\frac{p_t(a_{\max})\,\dot a_{\max}}{a_{\max}} \;=\;\frac{1}{t\,\bar y(0)}, \qquad \bar y(0)=\int_0^1 a_{\max}(s)\,2s\,ds .$$

The term is independent of $v$, so §7.4's inversion annihilates it; and its value is pinned by the structure. §10 recovers the same constant by an entirely independent route — the $h=0$ boundary value of the kernel self-consistency relation (R3) — so the entry flux is a consistency check the structure passes, not an extra unknown.

7.4 The kernel inverts: there is no march

Volterra of the first and second kind, for the reader who has not met the distinction. An integral equation is Volterra when the kernel vanishes on one side of the diagonal, so that the value of the unknown at $v$ depends only on its values on one side of $v$ — the integral operator is "triangular". It is of the first kind when the unknown appears only inside the integral, $\int k(v,w)\phi(w)\,dw=\psi(v)$; such equations are typically ill-posed, since the operator smooths and the inverse must differentiate. It is of the second kind when the unknown also appears outside, $c(v)\phi(v)+\int\ldots=\psi(v)$ with $c\ne0$; those are well-posed and solvable by a convergent Neumann series, which is the analyst's version of "march from the boundary". The draft of this note classified our system as first kind as written, second kind after one $\partial_v$, and proposed to march it downward from $a_{\max}(t)$. That classification is right; the march is unnecessary.

Write the cascade operator explicitly: $$(\mathcal I\varphi)(v):=\int_v^{a_{\max}(t)}\varphi(w)\,\frac{dw}{w}, \qquad\text{so that}\qquad \gamma=\operatorname{diag}\big(-\bar y\big)\circ\mathcal I .$$ Then $\partial_v(\mathcal I\varphi)(v)=-\varphi(v)/v$, i.e. $$\boxed{\;(\mathcal I^{-1}\psi)(v)\;=\;-\,v\,\psi'(v).\;}$$ The operator is not merely triangular with a nonzero diagonal — it is explicitly invertible, and its inverse is adjacent differencing. Consequently the system needs no march at all: one differentiation in $v$ solves it, and the result integrates. Better still, one does not even need the $t$-derivative:

the cascade, integrated
  1. The state, from (MASTER). $t\,\bar\chi(h_v(t))=U'(v)$ and $\bar\chi$ is strictly decreasing where $\bar y\gt0$, hence invertible: $$\boxed{\;h_v(t)\;=\;\bar\chi^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{U'(v)}{t}\Big).\;}$$
  2. The density, by differentiating (MASTER) in $v$ at fixed $t$. The definition of $H$ gives $\partial_vh_v(t)=-p_t(v)/v$, so $$t\,\bar\chi'(h_v)\,\partial_vh_v=U''(v) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t\,\big(-\bar y(h_v)\big)\Big(\!-\frac{p_t(v)}{v}\Big)=U''(v),$$ $$\boxed{\;p_t(v)\;=\;\frac{v\,U''(v)}{t\;\bar y\big(h_v(t)\big)} .\;}\qquad\text{(DENS)}$$
  3. The boundary value is the entry curve. $\bar\chi(0)=\int_0^1\chi_s(0)\,2s\,ds=\int_0^12s\,ds=1$, so $h_v(t)=0\iff U'(v)/t=1\iff t=U'(v)=t_e(v)$. The closed form contains §4 and §6.4(ii) as its boundary value rather than assuming them.

Consistency of the two routes is automatic, and this is where §7.3(ii)'s boundary term goes: $\partial_tp_t=-v\,\partial_v(\partial_th_v)$, and the entry flux, being $v$-independent, is annihilated by $\partial_v$. That is the precise sense in which the omitted term could not have disturbed the march — but it is a theorem, not an excuse for the omission.

No limit interchange is required anywhere, which was the third loose end of the draft. (FI-loc) is proved directly by Milgrom–Segal (§2), not as a $u\to v$ limit of the pair relations, so no exchange of $u\to v$ with $\partial_t$ is ever invoked. What the $t$-derivative needs is $\bar\chi\in C^1$ with $\bar\chi'\ne0$, and both hold on the support: each $\chi_s$ is Lipschitz uniformly in $s$ (§6.2), and $\bar\chi'=-\bar y\lt0$ strictly whenever $U'(v)\gt0$, since $\bar\chi(h_v)=U'(v)/t\gt0$ already forces $|S(h_v)|\gt0$.

7.5 What this does to the discrete cascade

The discrete kernel inherits the same factorisation. With $u_\ell$ the grid types, thm:triLP's gradient reads $$\Gamma_{m\ell}=-(u_m-u_{m+1})\,y_q\big(H_p(u_m)\big)\,\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{\ell\le m\}}}{u_\ell}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(a)\;T\;\operatorname{diag}(1/u)$$ with $T$ the unit upper-triangular all-ones matrix — the discrete image of $\mathcal I$. So the triangular cascade's "$K-1$ scalar ODEs marched column by column" (thm:tricascade) sharpens to adjacent differencing in closed form: $$\Lambda_m=-\frac{k_m}{t^2a_m},\qquad P'_{u_\ell}=u_\ell\big(\Lambda_\ell-\Lambda_{\ell-1}\big)\ \ (\ell\le K-2), \qquad P'_{u_{K-1}}=-\!\!\sum_{\ell\lt K-1}\!P'_{u_\ell},$$ with no back-substitution loop and no error accumulation; verified to $3.3\times10^{-11}$ against a dense solve (rem:tridiff). What survives of the cascade is only the nonlinear dependence of $a_m$ on the current $p_t$.

A closed loop worth recording. The certified kernel reproduces the floor ladder's signature without calling a Round-2 solver at all (rem:kernelladder): computing $t\,D_{HM}$ on the repaired $K=3$ runs purely through $\bar\chi$ — posteriors from the run, $\Psi$-average by Gauss–Legendre — returns the plateau $D_{HM}\equiv1$ at low $t$ (the cornering fact, which needs the type inactive), breaking inside the already-recorded kink bracket, and above the break $t\,D_{HM}\approx0.35$–$0.38$ against $U_H-U_M=0.3331$. The $5$–$14\%$ level error is quadrature on a coarse grid applied to an unconverged profile, not formula error: against the exact solver the kernel matches pointwise to $5.6\times10^{-16}$.

8. The frontiers, located

The three questions §5 deferred, answered in order — plus the exit curve's own differential equation.

8(a). Exit is located by normalisation, not by a vanishing density

cor:exitnorm

Since $\bar y\le\bar v\lt\infty$, (DENS) gives $p_t\big(a_{\min}(t)^+\big)\gt0$ wherever $U''\gt0$: the posterior density does not vanish at the exit frontier. It leaves with a jump. §5's guessed locating condition is therefore withdrawn. What locates the frontier is the only condition left once $p_t$ is pinned pointwise — normalisation, $\int p_t=1$ — and it becomes clean in the $H$-coordinate. Substituting $dh=-p_t(v)\,dv/v$ (the defining relation of $H$, i.e. step 2 of §7.4 read backwards):

the free boundary, in one scalar equation per rung $$\boxed{\;\int_0^{h_*(t)}(U')^{-1}\!\big(t\,\bar\chi(h)\big)\,dh\;=\;1, \qquad h_*(t):=H_{p_t}\big(a_{\min}(t)\big),\;}$$

one scalar equation per $t$, determining $h_*(t)$, after which $a_{\min}(t)=(U')^{-1}\big(t\,\bar\chi(h_*(t))\big)$ and $t_x=a_{\min}^{-1}$. The integrand is $v(h,t)=(U')^{-1}(t\bar\chi(h))$ — the type sitting at $H$-level $h$ when the rung is $t$, read straight off (MASTER) — so the equation says: integrate the types over their own $H$-levels from the top of the active band ($h=0$, where $v=a_{\max}$) down to the frontier, and stop when you have collected unit mass. The integrand is positive, so the left side is strictly increasing in the upper limit and the root is unique.

The jump is structural, not numerical. What makes $p_t(a_{\min}^+)\gt0$ is $\bar y(h_*(t))\gt0$, and that follows from $S(h_*(t))=[0,t]$ — i.e. from $h_*$ being decreasing, so that every earlier rung's opponent still ties $a_{\min}(t)$ strictly inside its range. This page originally argued that monotonicity informally ("as $s$ rises the support slides up and $1/u$ shrinks"). It is now derived, by differentiating the boxed equation above and nothing else (lem:hstarode, §10.2). That matters out of proportion to its size: three separate results were leaning on it — $S(h)=[0,s(h)]$, which is how (R3) is written at all; "rungs above $t$ enter the equation at $t$" (rem:global); and this jump. On the repaired $K=3$ family the machine check agrees: $\bar y\in[1.000,2.954]$ over every visited $h$ — the kernel does not degenerate at either frontier.

8(b). The leaver jumps and the survivors are $C^1$ — both, of different objects

rem:exitjump

This looked for a while like a contradiction inside the project: the continuum says the density jumps at exit, while prop:sliver asserts a $C^1$ crossing. The resolution is to write (DENS) as an analytic branch times a support indicator:

$$p_t(v)\;=\;\pi(v,t)\,\mathbf 1_{\{a_{\min}(t)\le v\le a_{\max}(t)\}},\qquad \pi(v,t):=\frac{v\,U''(v)}{t\,\bar y\big(h_v(t)\big)},\quad h_v(t)=\bar\chi^{-1}\big(U'(v)/t\big).$$

The branch $\pi$ is frontier-free: neither $a_{\min}$ nor $a_{\max}$ occurs in it, and it is defined and continuous on a neighbourhood of the closed band, since there $\bar y$ is bounded away from $0$. Three consequences:

  1. At an exit, every discontinuity is the indicator, not the profile. At $v=a_{\min}(t)$ one has $\pi\gt0$, so $p_t$ jumps in $v$; at fixed $v$, as $t$ crosses $t_x(v)$, $p_t(v)$ falls from $\pi(v,t_x(v))\gt0$ to $0$. The discontinuity is carried entirely by $\mathbf 1$ — a fact about the support, not about the analytic expression for the mass.
  2. Hence the exiting type's own share need not be $C^1$ across its exit rung, and need not vanish there.
  3. The surviving types' shares are unaffected — which is exactly what prop:sliver asserts, and all it asserts. Its conclusion "$h$ is $C^1$ across $\beta_L$" is about the High share, by the argument that the governing equation above $\beta_L$ does not reference $\beta_L$ — the discrete image of $\pi$ being frontier-free. It says nothing about Low.
how the discrete and continuum readings coexist

The two statements are about different objects and both are true. The discrete $P_{u_1}(t)$ is the mass carried by one type; the continuum $p_t(v)$ is a density in $v$. A single type carries zero mass in the continuum, so "the density at the frontier is positive" and "the leaver's share reaches zero" are perfectly compatible — the leaver's share is the mass of a sliver that is itself shrinking to nothing.

The discrete side is now settled in both branches. lem:exitlevels shows the surviving pairs' first integrals pin the crossing completely: every surviving $H$-level is continuous, and the leaver's share $c:=P_{u_1}(t_X^-)$ transfers entirely to the new bottom type, so all tails are nondecreasing across the exit and FOSD survives with no tangency hypothesis — in either branch, $c=0$ (share reaches zero) or $c\gt0$ (share jumps). Which branch occurs is then a fact about the solved system, not an assumption: at $K=3$ the exit is located by $P_L(t_X)=0\iff w_2(t_X)=t_X$ at $t_X=0.860\,801$ (lem:k3exit, cor:k3constants), i.e. $c=0$; at general $K$ the locating equation is $\sum_{l\le j-1}P_l(x)=0$, and its root is simple — the contact is transversal, exponent $1$ and not $2$ (thm:transexit). So the discrete share reaches zero linearly, not tangentially in the second-order sense; the earlier run-based readings of a nonzero share at exit were grid-limited (rem:k3tX) and are void.

The asymmetry of §5, re-stated in the right currency. Entry versus exit is about which crossings generate an algebraic relation — entries do, exits cannot — not about which are smooth. In the currency of smoothness the two frontiers are alike: the crossing type's density jumps at both, with entry exponent $\alpha=0$ (prop:k3cascade) and $\pi\gt0$ at exit.

8(c). The exit curve has an ODE, driven by the entry curve

New: the discrete exit-location programme hands the continuum a differential law. cor:exitspacing

The discrete Phase-G analysis locates each exit by a single quadrature over a window known in advance. Two symbols are needed, both local to one row $j$ of the ladder (lem:ywindow, lem:bandformm):

  • $\kappa_j:=C_j/v_j\in(0,1)$, where the row's $\Psi$-averaged tying value is $\bar y=v_j-C_jt^2$ with $C_j=\sum_{i\le j}d_i(t_i/t_j)^2$ and $d_i=v_i-v_{i-1}$ the value gaps. So $\kappa_j$ is the curvature-to-value ratio: how fast the tying value falls with the rung, relative to the row's own value.
  • $g_j(u):=u^2S_j(u)/t_j\ge0$ with $S_j(u)=\sum_{i\gt j,\,t_i\lt u}d_it_i/\bar Y_i(u)$ — the drag exerted by the rows above $j$ that are already active, each contributing its value gap times its entry rung, divided by its own tying value. It is non-decreasing along the band: rows only switch on as $u$ grows.

In the variable $Y=u/t$ the band runs over the fixed window $[1,Y_0]$ with $Y_0=t_{j+1}/t_j$ — the rung ratio — and the exit is the diagonal crossing $Y=1$, with $$(\mathrm{LOG}_j)\qquad \log\frac{x_j}{x_{j-1}}=\int_1^{Y_0}\frac{Y^2\,dY}{Y^3+g_j(u(Y))\big(Y^2-\kappa_j\big)} .$$ Now let consecutive rungs close, $Y_0\downarrow1$ — which is exactly the continuum limit, since $Y_0-1=(t_{j+1}-t_j)/t_j$ is the relative rung spacing. Expanding the integrand at $Y=1$:

exit spacing is entry spacing, damped $$\log\frac{x_j}{x_{j-1}} =\frac{Y_0-1}{1+g_j(u_0)\big(1-\kappa_j\big)}+O\big((Y_0-1)^2\big), \qquad\text{whence in the limit}\qquad \boxed{\;\frac{d\log x}{dv}=\frac{1}{1+g\,(1-\kappa)}\;\frac{d\log t}{dv}.\;}$$

A first-order ODE for the exit curve, driven by the entry curve. Read it as economics: the exit frontier moves in the same direction as the entry frontier and by the same relative amount, damped by the factor $1+g(1-\kappa)$. The damping is the drag of the already-active rows above ($g$) weighted by how much room the tying value still has to fall ($1-\kappa$). With no rows above and no drag, $g=0$: exit spacing equals entry spacing exactly. Every active row above tightens the exit relative to the entry.

What this does and does not add. It does not add an equation to §10's count — $t_x$ is an output there, pinned by §8(a)'s normalisation — so this law is a consistency relation that any solution must satisfy, and it sharpens rather than contradicts squareness. Its practical value is that it is the only local statement about the exit frontier we have: everything else about exit location is either a closed form at the very top of the ladder, a certified bracket, or an asymptotic at the exit's own birth, with nothing elementary in between (rem:q16). It is a first-order law, so it is informative only where the spacing is small: over the random-ladder sweep the worst relative error falls $1.6\times10^{-2}\to2.1\times10^{-3}\to2.6\times10^{-4}$ as the cut $Y_0-1\lt0.3,\,0.1,\,0.03$ tightens — an envelope exponent $\approx1.8$, consistent with the claimed $O((Y_0-1)^2)$.

It now has a continuum counterpart, derived from the other side. §10.2 differentiates the exit normalisation (R1) directly and reaches the same shape without ever touching the ladder: $d\log t_x/d\log t_e=1/(1+\Delta)$ with $\Delta\ge0$ an explicit integral (cor:exitelas). Term by term $\Delta$ is $g(1-\kappa)$ — the sum over active rows above becomes an integral over active types above, the value gap $d_i$ becomes $dw$, the entry rung $t_i$ becomes $U'(w)$ — with one scalar factor still unreconciled. So this law is on its way from "a consistency relation any solution must satisfy" to "a relation the continuum system implies", which is a stronger status; what stands between the two is rem:exitelasdict.

8(d). The bottom: where the kernel degenerates, and why that is the right place

rem:bottomsing

$\bar y(h)\to0$ exactly as $h\uparrow h^{\max}:=\sup_sH_{q_s}(a_{\min}(s))$ — i.e. where the type is atom-covered in the final against every opponent quantile. By (MASTER) that happens iff $U'(v)/t=\bar\chi(h)=0$, i.e. only at the bottom type: $$\boxed{\;U'(\underline v)=0.\;}$$ So the sole degeneracy of the kernel sits at $\underline v$, and it is precisely the continuum shadow of the zero-atom IC $\mathcal W(\underline v,p_0)=0$ (lem:atomIC): the bottom type wins the tournament with probability zero because it is covered everywhere. Since $p_t\propto1/\bar y$, the density blows up there; the question is with what exponent, and whether mass is lost.

The recorded answer was a dichotomy — and it can now be collapsed. rem:bottomsing records $(v-\underline v)^{-1/2}$ if $s\mapsto H_{q_s}(a_{\min}(s))=h_*(s)$ attains its maximum at an endpoint, $(v-\underline v)^{-1/3}$ if at an interior $s$. Two facts already proved above decide it:

  1. $h_*$ is decreasing in $s$ — no longer an assertion but a theorem, lem:hstarode (§10.2) — so the maximiser is the endpoint $s=0$ and the interior branch is dead. Note the order of business: this step is what makes the whole dichotomy collapse, and until 2026-08-01 it was resting on a sentence in §8(a).
  2. The endpoint is $s=0$, where the $\Psi$-weight $2s\,ds$ vanishes. This is what the recorded endpoint branch missed: it measured $S(h)$ by its length, giving $\bar y\asymp|S(h)|\asymp(h^{\max}-h)$. But $S(h)=[0,s(h)]$ and the weight kills two more powers, $$\bar y(h)=\int_0^{s(h)}y_s(h)\,2s\,ds\;\sim\;\underline v\,s(h)^2 ,$$ because $y_s(h)\to\underline v$ as $s\to0$ (the band pinches onto $\underline v$, §8(e)). So the exponent is $2$, not $1$.
G1 — bottom exponents

Posit $U'(v)\sim c\,(v-\underline v)^{\theta}$ as $v\downarrow\underline v$ (some $\theta\gt0$, since $U'(\underline v)=0$) and run it through the closed forms.

  1. Rate at which the frontier reaches the top $H$-level. 8(e). The atom dissolves — and leaves no hole

prop:noatomcont, rem:atomband

Claim. Let $A:=\{v:\sigma_v(\{0\})\gt0\}$ be the set of types placing positive Round-1 mass on the bid $0$. Then $f(A)=0$: under (CONT) the Round-1 zero-bid atom carries no mass.

Proof, in four steps.

  1. Suppose $f(A)\gt0$ and put $a:=\inf A$. Since $f$ is atomless, $f(A)\gt0$ forces $A$ to contain some $v\gt a$; fix one. Let $p_0$ be the posterior at bid $0$, so $\operatorname{supp}p_0=\overline A$ and $\inf\operatorname{supp}p_0=a$.
  2. A positive-mass atom means $t_0:=G(0)\gt0$. The opponent's rung is $\Psi$-distributed, and every rung $s\le t_0$ also bids $0$ — so with probability $\Psi(t_0)=t_0^2\gt0$ both finalists carry the posterior $p_0$ and the final is symmetric.
  3. In a symmetric final each type ties with itself: $y_{p_0}\big(H_{p_0}(v)\big)=v$. So §6.2 evaluates the final win probability at $$\mathcal W(v,p_0)\;=\;\chi_{p_0}\big(H_{p_0}(v)\big)\;=\;P_{p_0}(v)\;\gt\;0,$$ strictly, because $v\gt a=\inf\operatorname{supp}p_0$ leaves positive $p_0$-mass strictly below $v$.
  4. That contradicts lem:atomIC, which requires $\mathcal W(v,p_0)=0$ at every carrier of a zero-bid atom. Hence $A\subseteq\{a\}$, and $f(A)=0$ since $f$ is atomless. $\ \blacksquare$

Note what the argument does not use: no interval hypothesis on the carrier, no cornering, no top carrier. It supersedes the earlier either/or of rem:atomcont by closing its first horn outright.

And the hole one expects is not there. §5's third deferred question rested on a false premise: that $t_e=U'$ with $U'(\underline v)\gt0$ would strand the quantile band $[0,t_e(\underline v))$, to be filled by an atom or a boundary layer. 9. $(v,h)$ coordinates — the equilibrium is a factorised coupling

Change the rung for the $H$-level and the type-coupling disappears. lem:hcoords

Coupling, and why the word is the right one. Since $G$ is the quantile map, the rung $t$ is uniform on $(0,1]$ by construction. So the joint law of (type, rung) has density $\rho(v,t)=p_t(v)$, and it is a coupling — a joint distribution with both marginals prescribed: the type marginal is the prior $f$, and the rung marginal is uniform. Equivalently $\rho=f(v)\,\sigma(t\mid v)$, the mixed strategy read as a conditional law. Every equilibrium condition we have is a statement about this one object.

Replace the rung $t$ by the type's own $H$-level $h=h_v(t)$ from §7.4. The change of variable is read off (MASTER) by differentiating in $t$ at fixed $v$: $$0=\bar\chi(h)+t\,\bar\chi'(h)\,h'(t)=\bar\chi(h)-t\,\bar y(h)\,h'(t) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad dt=\frac{t\,\bar y(h)}{\bar\chi(h)}\;dh,$$ legitimate because $\bar y\gt0$ on the support (§8(a)). Multiply by (DENS) and watch the cancellation: $$p_t(v)\,dt=\frac{v\,U''(v)}{t\,\bar y(h)}\cdot\frac{t\,\bar y(h)}{\bar\chi(h)}\,dh =\frac{v\,U''(v)}{\bar\chi(h)}\,dh .$$

the coupling factorises $$\boxed{\;\tilde\rho(v,h)\;=\;\alpha(v)\,\beta(h)\,\mathbf 1_{\{0\le h\le H(v)\}}, \qquad \alpha(v)=v\,U''(v),\qquad \beta(h)=\frac1{\bar\chi(h)} .\;}$$

$\bar y$ — the one object that couples different types — cancels exactly. What is left is a product measure restricted to a region: a factor in $v$ times a factor in $h$, with an indicator. The lower edge $h=0$ is the entry curve (§4); the upper edge is $H(v):=h_v\big(t_x(v)\big)$, type $v$'s own $H$-level when it stops mixing.

the shape has a name

A density of the form $a(x)\,b(y)\,k(x,y)$ is the signature of a Schrödinger bridge / Sinkhorn (iterative proportional fitting) solution: the entropically optimal coupling of two prescribed marginals through a reference kernel $k$ is always a product of one-variable factors times $k$, and $a,b$ are found by alternately rescaling rows and columns. Our $k$ is a $0/1$ indicator of a region — the degenerate case where the reference kernel is a hard constraint rather than a soft cost. So the equilibrium is, structurally, the unique coupling of $f$ with the uniform rung law supported on the region $\{0\le h\le H(v)\}$; and all the equilibrium content has been pushed into the shape of that region, i.e. into the two frontiers. That is a genuinely different way to look for the equilibrium than marching a cascade, and §10's obstruction is exactly that the region depends on the unknowns.

10. The system assembled — square, and what is still open

Three unknown functions, three relations, two constants needed and two supplied by theorems. thm:contsquare

Under the architecture now banked — active set $[a_{\min}(t),a_{\max}(t)]$ at each rung, no atom (§8(e)) — the continuum equilibrium is carried by exactly three unknown functions: $$\varphi:=U'\ \text{on }[\underline v,\bar v],\qquad \bar\chi\ \text{on }[0,h^{\max}),\qquad h_*\ \text{on }(0,1],\qquad h^{\max}=1/\underline v .$$ The exit data is not among them: $t_x=a_{\min}^{-1}$ is an output of $h_*$. They are determined by three relations, one algebraic and two first-order:

the three relations $$\text{(R1) row marginal}\qquad \int_0^{h_*(t)}\varphi^{-1}\!\big(t\,\bar\chi(h)\big)\,dh=1 \qquad\text{for each }t\in(0,1],$$ $$\text{(R2) column marginal}\qquad v\,\varphi'(v)\,\Phi\big(H(v)\big)=f(v),\qquad \Phi(h):=\int_0^h\frac{d\xi}{\bar\chi(\xi)},$$ $$\text{(R3) kernel self-consistency}\qquad -\bar\chi'(h)=\int_0^{s(h)}\varphi^{-1}\!\big(s\,\bar\chi(h)\big)\,2s\,ds, \qquad s(h)=\min\{1,h_*^{-1}(h)\} .$$

Where each comes from.

  • (R1) is §8(a) verbatim — the normalisation that locates the free boundary. It is algebraic and pointwise in $t$: its integrand is positive, so the left side is strictly increasing in the upper limit and the equation determines $h_*(t)$ for each $t$ separately. It is implicit, not closed-form — but it carries no integration constant, which is all the count needs.
  • (R2) is Bayes closure in $h$-coordinates. Since $\rho=f\,\sigma(\cdot\mid v)$ with $\sigma(\cdot\mid v)$ a probability, the column of $\rho$ integrates to $f(v)$: $\int_{t_e(v)}^{t_x(v)}p_t(v)\,dt=f(v)$. By §9 the left side is $v\,U''(v)\int_0^{H(v)}dh/\bar\chi(h)=v\,U''(v)\,\Phi(H(v))$. Given $(\bar\chi,h_*)$ this determines $\varphi$ up to one constant: $\varphi'$ enters linearly and pointwise, so integrating up from $\underline v$ costs exactly one constant — even though the coefficient $\Phi(H(v))$ depends on $\varphi$ nonlocally through $H$.
  • (R3) is the definition of $\bar y$ made explicit. By (MASTER), $H_{p_s}(v)=h\iff U'(v)=s\,\bar\chi(h)\iff v=\varphi^{-1}(s\bar\chi(h))$, so the inverse $H$-function is $y_s(h)=\varphi^{-1}(s\,\bar\chi(h))$ in closed form. The averaging set is $S(h)=\{s:h_*(s)\ge h\}=[0,s(h)]$ because $h_*$ decreases (§8(a)), with $s(h)=1$ once $h\le h_*(1)$. Substitute into $\bar y=\int_{S(h)}y_s\,2s\,ds$ and use $\bar\chi'=-\bar y$: a first-order ODE in $h$, one constant.
two constants needed, two supplied — by theorems, not conventions $$\bar\chi(0)=1\quad(\text{cornering: \S4, \S6.4(ii)}), \qquad \varphi(\underline v)=U'(\underline v)=0\quad(\text{\S8(d)}).$$

The system is square. Neither constant is a normalisation chosen for convenience; both are proved facts about the equilibrium. And $U'(\bar v)=1$ is forced, not imposed: (R1)'s integrand at $h=0$ is $\varphi^{-1}(t\,\bar\chi(0))=\varphi^{-1}(t)$, which must be a legal type for every rung $t\in(0,1]$, so $U'(\bar v)\ge1$; and $U'\le1$ because it is a probability (§3). A third normalisation would over-determine the system, so it had better be a consequence — and it is.

The cross-check at $h=0$. $s(0)=1$ and $\bar\chi(0)=1$ turn (R3) into $\bar y(0)=\int_0^1\varphi^{-1}(s)\,2s\,ds=\int_0^1a_{\max}(s)\,2s\,ds$ — which is exactly the entry-flux constant of §7.3(ii), derived there by a completely different route (evaluating the $t$-differentiated cascade at the top active type). Two independently banked routes to the same constant agree. This is the check §7.3(ii) promised.

Where the redundancy enters. $\rho$ is a coupling with both marginals prescribed: $\int\rho\,dv=1$ at each $t$ (R1) and $\int\rho\,dt=f(v)$ at each $v$ (R2). The two families are not independent — integrating the first over $t$ and the second over $v$ both return the total mass, $\iint\rho=1=\int f$. That single scalar identity is the compatibility condition of the coupling problem, and it holds identically, which is precisely why imposing both marginals in full is consistent rather than one condition too many. The count above is its quantitative form: two marginals, one redundancy, two constants.

the rent anchor is inert — the natural guess is wrong

One expects $U(\underline v)=0$ to be the scalar that closes the system. It is not, and the reason changes what one looks for: $U$ itself never appears in (R1)–(R3) — only $U'$, through $\varphi$ and $\varphi^{-1}$, and $U''$, through $\alpha=v\,U''$. The system is a system for the marginal rent, so an additive constant in $U$ is invisible to it. The anchor does exactly one thing, and it is downstream: it turns the solved $\varphi$ into a rent level, $U(v)=\int_{\underline v}^v\varphi$. What closes the system is the pair $\bar\chi(0)=1$ and $U'(\underline v)=0$, and both are theorems. Their discrete counterparts are the same two facts: cornering below the entry rung is what makes the entry rungs determined rather than unknowns (thm:floorladder), and $U'(\underline v)=0$ is the shadow of the atom-IC requirement $\mathcal W(\underline v,p_0)=0$. rem:anchorinert

That is already the ODE. It can also be pushed back into type space, by the substitution used twice before, $dh=-p_t(v)\,dv/v$, together with $\bar\chi(h_v)=U'(v)/t$ from (MASTER) and $p_t(v)/v=U''(v)/\big(t\,\bar y(h_v)\big)$ from (DENS) — the $U''$ cancels against $\varphi'=U''$ and what is left is startlingly clean:

the exit frontier, differentiated — two forms $$\boxed{\;a_{\min}(t)\;h_*'(t)\;=\;-\int_0^{h_*(t)}\frac{\bar\chi(h)}{U''\big(v(h,t)\big)}\,dh\;=\;-\,\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{a_{\min}(t)}^{a_{\max}(t)}\frac{U'(v)}{\bar y\big(h_v(t)\big)}\,dv\;}$$

The two forms have different uses. The first is the one to differentiate with: no $\bar y$ appears, so it cannot be circular, and its integrand is manifestly finite. The second has no $f$, no $\Phi$, no $H$, no $U''$ — the free boundary moves under a drive built only from the marginal rent and the kernel, integrated over the currently active band — and it is the one that compares to the discrete law.

first dividend
both right sides are strictly negative, so $h_*'\lt0$ is derived, not assumed. §8(a) asserted "$h_*$ is decreasing (as $s$ rises the support slides up and $1/u$ shrinks)" and used it to get $S(h)=[0,s(h)]$ — which the whole of (R3) rests on. That hand-wave is now discharged by (R1) alone.
a second thing that came free
differentiability of $h_*$ was never needed as a hypothesis. Apply the implicit function theorem to $F(t,H)=\int_0^H\varphi^{-1}(t\bar\chi)\,dh-1$: the Jacobian $\partial_HF=v(H,t)\ge\underline v\gt0$ never degenerates, so $h_*\in C^1$ is a conclusion. What the architecture must assume about $h_*$ drops from monotone to merely continuous — and continuity is what makes $\bar\chi$ invertible on the open band in the first place, so the bootstrap runs continuity → ODE → monotonicity → frontier positivity, never the reverse.
second dividend
it is the missing link to cor:exitspacing. Differentiating $a_{\min}(t)=\varphi^{-1}\big(t\bar\chi(h_*(t))\big)$ and comparing with the entry side $\dot a_{\max}=1/U''(a_{\max})$ gives, at a type $v$ that exits at $t_x(v)$, $$\frac{d\log t_x}{d\log t_e} =\Bigg[1+\underbrace{\frac{\bar y\big(h_*(t_x)\big)}{v\,U'(v)}\int_v^{a_{\max}(t_x)}\frac{U'(w)}{\bar y\big(h_w(t_x)\big)}\,dw}_{\text{a nonnegative drag}}\Bigg]^{-1},$$ which is exactly §8(c)'s shape $1/\big(1+g(1-\kappa)\big)$, damping factor and all.
the economics: the exit curve is flatter than the entry curve, always

Read the box as a statement about elasticities. The drag is nonnegative, so $d\log t_x/d\log t_e\in(0,1]$: a proportional shift in when a type enters produces a smaller proportional shift in when it leaves. The exit curve can never overtake the entry curve in log–log, and the two can only run parallel where the drag vanishes — that is, where no type above is still active.

The immediate corollary is the one that pays. Define the type's active span $\lambda(v):=t_x(v)/t_e(v)\ge1$ — how many times its own entry rung a type stays in the market. Then

$$\frac{d\log\lambda}{d\log v}=\frac{d\log t_x}{d\log v}-\frac{d\log t_e}{d\log v}\le0,$$

so $\lambda$ is non-increasing in $v$: low types mix over proportionally longer stretches of the rung range than high types do. High types enter late and leave almost at once; low types enter first and linger. The two exponent programmes on this page turn out to need the same object from opposite ends — and because $\lambda$ falls, $\lambda_0:=\lambda(\underline v^+)$ is a supremum over the whole leaver block, not a delicate limit at a point. That is what makes G1's remaining hypothesis one-sided; see §8(d).

what to do instead
on the leavers, monotone (Tarski) iteration: start from a sub-solution and a super-solution, iterate both, and they bracket — no metric needed. 10.4 What squareness does not give, and why the obstruction is global

rem:global

there is no march in $t$ — and that is a feature of the model

The closed form $p_t(v)=v\,U''(v)/\big(t\,\bar y(h_v(t))\big)$ is explicit given $\bar y$ — and $\bar y$ is a functional of the whole family $\{p_s\}_{s\in[0,1]}$. Precisely: with $S(h)=\{s:h_*(s)\ge h\}$ and $h_*$ decreasing (lem:hstarode), $S(h_v(t))=[0,s_v]$ where $h_*(s_v)=h_v(t)\le h_*(t)$, so $s_v\ge t$ — rungs above $t$ enter the equation at $t$. There is therefore no initial-value march in $t$ at all: the system is a global fixed point in $(\varphi,\bar\chi,h_*)$.

This is not a defect of the kernel. It is $\sigma$-invariance (lem:sigmainv): the $\Psi$-average $2s\,ds$ over the whole rung range is what makes the opponent law universal, and a quantity averaged over all rungs cannot simultaneously be causal in $t$. The discrete theory has the same feature and merely hides it — the entries of $\Gamma(t)$ depend on $\bar G(t)$, built from the same global average.

So squareness is a count, not an existence theorem: it says the system has no free parameter and no leftover condition, not that it has a solution. The right remaining target is a fixed-point or implicit-function statement in $(\varphi,\bar\chi,h_*)$ — never a Picard march.

10.6 What is open — ranked, with the current guess attached to each

Ordered by unblock-value ÷ cost, which is not the order they were discovered in. The first two are pencil and cheap; the third is a single laptop run; the fourth is the theorem. The list shortened on 2026-08-01 — the free-boundary ODE graduated out of it, taking most of the old item 4 with it.

  1. Bound $\lambda(v)=t_x(v)/t_e(v)$ from above. Pencil, cheapest decisive move on the page (STATE C1). A bound closes G1 outright: $\theta=1$, $U''(\underline v)=f(\underline v)/(\underline v\Phi_0)$ finite and positive, $p_t\sim(\cdot)t^{-1/3}(v-\underline v)^{-2/3}$. Unbounded $\lambda$ opens the one-parameter family $\theta\ge1$ instead — still integrable, but the constant $c$ is lost. What changed on 2026-08-01: the statement went from two-sided to one-sided. cor:exitelas closes $\theta\lt1$ (it would force $\lambda\equiv1$, i.e. no mixing anywhere) and makes $\lambda$ non-increasing, so a bound anywhere on the leaver block is a bound at the bottom. One monotone function, bounded above — that is the whole remaining ask.
  2. The $u^2$ in the cor:exitspacing dictionary. Pencil, bounded: one factor (STATE C2; the statement is parked as rem:exitelasdict). This is now the only open piece of the old G4: the ODE and the elasticity are banked, and the dictionary matches term by term except for a rung normalisation.   3. Sign the composite map on each block. Laptop, one run. G5 says order-preserving below $v_\dagger$, ambiguous above.   4. Existence. The real target; everything above is scaffolding for it. A fixed point in $(\varphi,\bar\chi,h_*)$ for (R1)–(R3) under the two proved boundary conditions. The obstruction is global (§10.4), so no Picard march. Current best route: the $(v,h)$ factorisation of §9, which recasts the problem as finding the region of a product coupling — and G3 says that region is two blocks glued at $v_\dagger$. Combined with G5, the shape of the argument to aim for is a monotone iteration on the leaver block and a squared-map argument on the survivor block, matched at $v_\dagger$.

10.7 Guesses that died — do not re-derive

The most expensive thing a research document can do is let a refuted guess look plausible again.

dead
D1
"The density vanishes at the exit frontier, crossed $C^1$." Killed by (DENS): $\bar y$ is bounded, so $p_t(a_{\min}^+)\gt0$ whenever $U''\gt0$. The frontier is located by normalisation instead (§8(a)). The $C^1$ statement that made this plausible, prop:sliver, is about the surviving types and says nothing about the leaver.
D2
"March the cascade downward from $a_{\max}(t)$." Not wrong, just unnecessary: $\mathcal I^{-1}\psi=-v\psi'$, so the operator inverts in closed form and the march has nothing to compute (§7.4). The discrete version dies the same way — adjacent differencing, no back-substitution loop (§7.5).
D3
"An atom survives the limit, or a boundary layer fills the band $[0,t_e(\underline v))$." Both rest on $U'(\underline v)\gt0$, which is false (§8(d)). The band is empty; the discrete atom is the $t\downarrow0$ limit of the family, not an object inside it (§8(e)).
D4
"The maximiser of $h_*$ could be interior." Killed by $h_*$ decreasing — and that monotonicity is itself now derived rather than asserted (lem:hstarode, §10.2), which is what makes this kill load-bearing rather than circular. Only the endpoint $s=0$ survives, and the $\Psi$-weight there is what makes the exponent $2$ rather than $1$.
D5
"The rent anchor $U(\underline v)=0$ closes the system." $U$ never appears in (R1)–(R3) — only $U'$ and $U''$. The anchor is inert; the closing constants are $\bar\chi(0)=1$ and $U'(\underline v)=0$, both theorems (rem:anchorinert).
D6
"(TRI) is a conjecture, and the exit ladder needs the $K=4$ constructed curve." (TRI) is thm:triLP at every $K$; the $K=4$ curve is refuted as an equilibrium object (cor:atomICfail) and its constants — notably $0.408$ — must never be quoted (§4).
