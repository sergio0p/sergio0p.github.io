Where the Delay Would Come From, and Why It Cannot

The same seven steps, run on the two-type case in the same notation: the wall exists, the duality holds, the cancellation happens — and every one of them is degenerate, because $m\equiv0$ collapses $\Phi$ onto $h$ and there is no second level left for a rival's belief to cross.

provenance

Unlike its three-type companion, everything below is an equilibrium claim. The binary equilibrium is banked and was not touched by the incentive failure that refuted the constructed three-type curve: its zero-bid atom is carried by Low alone at posterior $h=0$, so the atom prize is $0$ and the zero-atom incentive condition is satisfied — the binary case is in fact the $K=2$ instance of that condition, not a casualty of it. Read this document as the base case: the one place in the programme where the shifted argument is provably absent, and therefore the sanity check every general-$K$ formula has to pass. Notation is the companion's throughout, specialised by $m\equiv0$; the source file proofs.tex writes the binary posterior as $\mu$, and $\mu\equiv h$.

0. Cast of characters

Two valuations $v\in\{1,3\}$ (Low, High), equal priors. Round-one bids are mixed; instead of the bid $b$ we use its quantile $t=G(b)\in[0,1]$ — the probability that a rival's bid falls below yours. The object of interest is the posterior belief attached to each quantile: an observer who sees a first-round bid at quantile $t$ assigns

$$ h(t) = \Pr(\text{High}\mid t), \qquad m(t) \equiv 0, \qquad \ell(t) = 1 - h(t) - m(t) = 1-h(t). $$

Every definition of the three-type case survives verbatim; the two-type case is the three-type case at $m\equiv0$. In particular the combination that recurs so often it earned a name,

$$ \Phi(t) \;=\; h(t) + \tfrac32\, m(t), $$

is perfectly well defined here, and this is the whole content of the document:

the thesis, in one line $$ m\equiv0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Phi \;=\; h \quad\text{identically.} $$

The three-type wall is $h(s)=\Phi(t)$ — an equation between the High-share at the opponent's quantile $s$ and a level of the own posterior at $t$. With $\Phi=h$ it reads $h(s)=h(t)$, whose root is $s=t$. The delay is not removed by a change of variables; it is never created, because the level that the opponent's High-share is asked to reach is the opponent-side reading of the same quantity, at the same height.

The same collapse in the language of the belief ladder. The walls of the Round-2 complex are level-crossings between rungs of $H_p(v)=\int_{[v,\bar v]}dp/u$, rescaled by $3$; at $K=3$ the three rungs are

$$ h, \qquad \underbrace{h+\tfrac32 m}_{\Phi}, \qquad h+\tfrac32 m + 3\ell , $$

and at $m\equiv0$ the middle rung merges into the top one, leaving two distinct levels, $h$ and $h+3\ell$. Numerical constants of the curve, at $(v_H,v_L)=(3,1)$ with equal priors:

Two remarks on this table. First, $t_0$ is the companion's $t_0$, not a renaming: in the general-spacing form of the floor relation, $t_v=(U_v-U_{v^-})/(v-v^-)$, the top type's entry rung is $t_0=(U_H-U_L)/(v_H-v_L)=k_1/2$, which is exactly the atom's quantile mass $G(0)$. (At $K=3$ that relation reads $t_0=(U_H-U_M)/(v_H-v_M)=k_1-k_2$ because the values are unit-spaced there.) The floor relation's scope caveat — its $t_0$ is the top type's entry rung and coincides with the atom edge only when the two happen to agree — is satisfied here legitimately: the atom is pure Low and High enters exactly where the atom ends. Second, the last row is not an omission. In the three-type case $t^*,t^{**}$ are the quantiles at which a wall switches on and off, and they cut the support into branches. Here no wall ever switches, the support has one co-mixing branch, and there is nothing to name.

1. The engine: indifference makes products constant

Each type mixes over an interval of quantiles, and along its support its expected payoff is constant. Taking differences between adjacent types, the envelope argument gives first integrals. At $K=3$ there were two, one per adjacent pair,

$$ t\,D_{HM}(t) \;=\; k_1 - k_2, \qquad t\,D_{ML}(t) \;=\; k_2 , $$

and it is the interplay between those two branches that Steps 4–6 exploit. At $K=2$ there is one adjacent pair, $(H,L)$, and therefore one first integral:

$$ t\,D_{HL}(t) \;=\; k_1 \qquad \text{on the common support.} $$

Written out in the beliefs — with $\Delta=v_H-v_L=2$ and $Y(h)=\int_0^h G^2$ the opponent-reputation integral induced by the fact that your final opponent is the maximum of two draws from $G$ — this is the conserved quantity

$$ \Pi \;=\; G\Big[(1-h)+\tfrac{\Delta}{3}\,Y(h)\Big] \;=\; t_0 , \qquad\text{with}\qquad t\,D_{HL}=\Delta\,\Pi , \quad\text{so } k_1=\Delta\,t_0 = 2t_0 . $$

Differentiating this identity in $t$ is what produces the ODE for $h$. So everything hinges on how $D_{HL}$ depends on the beliefs — and that is where the second round enters.

2. The opponent's quantile, and the wall

Your continuation payoff is a final against the winner of the other semifinal, whose quantile $s$ is drawn with density $2s\,ds$ (they had to beat someone). The final is an all-pay auction under the pair of revealed beliefs, one at your quantile $t$, one at theirs, $s$. Its payoff is piecewise affine in the two belief vectors: the affine formula switches when belief levels cross. Here the whole cell complex is visible at once — with two types there is a single ratio comparison, so there are exactly two cells and the two affine forms glue into one expression,

$$ \Pi_2(H;h,h_{\text{opp}})=\Delta\big(1-\min(h,h_{\text{opp}})\big), \qquad \Pi_2(L;h,h_{\text{opp}})=\tfrac{\Delta}{3}\,(h-h_{\text{opp}})^+ , $$

the $\min$ being the entire word, with unique kink locus the diagonal $h=h_{\text{opp}}$. (At $K=3$ the word has length $4$ or $5$ across ten cells and no single $\min$ reproduces it: the collapse is a degree-one accident.)

Now run the wall census the way the three-type case forces one to. The companion has two own-behind walls, with complementary necessity conditions coming from the a-priori bound $h(s)\le1$:

$$ h(W)=h+\tfrac32m=\Phi \ \text{ needs }\ m\le2\ell, \qquad h(s_3)=h+3\ell \ \text{ needs }\ 2\ell\le m . $$
the wall census, in full

Set $m\equiv0$ in both.

(a) The $\Phi$-wall survives, as the diagonal. Its necessity $m\le2\ell$ becomes $0\le2\ell$, vacuously true — the wall is active everywhere. And its equation becomes $h(s)=\Phi(t)=h(t)$, root

$$ W(t)=t . $$

(b) The $s_3$-wall dies. Its necessity $2\ell\le m$ becomes $2\ell\le0$, i.e. $\ell=0$ — satisfiable only on the degenerate pure-High posterior $h=1$, which the equilibrium never reaches. Equivalently and directly: the crossing of your bottom rung against the opponent's top rung asks for

$$ h(s)=h(t)+\frac{v_H}{v_L}\big(1-h(t)\big) \;\gt\; 1 \quad\text{whenever } h(t)\lt1, $$

refused by $h(s)\le1$ for every value ratio $v_H/v_L$ — no solution, no numerics, no architecture involved. (The companion's knife-edge $(v_H-v_M)(v_H-v_L)=v_Mv_L$, which decides whether the two necessities are complementary at $K=3$, has nothing to arbitrate here: one of the two walls is identically active and the other identically dead.)

A wall is supposed to be an equation between the solution at two different quantiles. The one surviving wall is an equation between the solution and itself. Its root is not a new unknown for the ordinary reason — that it is $h$ evaluated somewhere else — but for a stronger one: it is not evaluated somewhere else.

3. The delay ODE — and the slot where the delay would sit

Average the pair difference over the opponent. Writing $\chi_q$ for the tying value and $\bar y=-\bar\chi'$ for its $\Psi$-average, the first integral of §1 differentiates to

$$ h'(t) \;=\; \frac{3\,t_0}{t^2\,\bar y(t)} , \qquad \bar y(t)=\int_0^1 y_s\big(h(t)/3\big)\,2s\,ds . $$

The integrand $y_s$ is a step function of $s$: it equals $3$ while the opponent's ladder sits above your level and drops to $1$ once it does not, the step occurring exactly at the wall of §2, $s=t$. So the two pieces are $[t,1]$ and $[0,t]$, and

$$ \bar y(t)\;=\;3\,(1-t^2)\;+\;1\cdot t^2\;=\;3-2t^2 . $$

Hence the equilibrium ODE, elementary and autonomous:

$$ h'(t) \;=\; \frac{3\,t_0}{t^2\,\big(3-2t^2\big)}\qquad\text{on }[t_0,1]. $$

Set this beside the three-type pair, in which the averaged tying value carried two crossovers — your own rung, where $y$ steps $3\to2$, and a second one $\omega(t)$ where it steps $2\to1$ — giving $\bar y=3-t^2-\omega(t)^2$. The numerators are the same symbol, $3t_0$; only the bracket differs.

the delay, and its absence $$ \underbrace{h'=\frac{3t_0}{t^2\,(3-t^2)}}_{K=3,\ \text{below }t^*} \qquad \underbrace{h'=\frac{3t_0}{t^2\,\big(3-t^2-w(t)^2\big)}}_{K=3,\ \text{above }t^*} \qquad \underbrace{h'=\frac{3t_0}{t^2\,\big(3-t^2-t^2\big)}}_{K=2,\ \text{everywhere}} $$

The binary bracket $3-2t^2$ is the same expression $3-t^2-\omega^2$ with $\omega\equiv t$. The second crossover has not been assumed away and has not been approximated: it has collapsed onto the diagonal, because a step from $v_M$ down to $v_L$ requires a $v_M$. A shifted argument with zero shift is not a shifted argument. That is the precise sense in which the binary chain is autonomous — and note what it does not rest on: $\bar y$ depends on the solution only through the measure of the level set $\{s:h(s)\le h(t)\}$, so even without monotonicity of the belief there is no $h$ composed with a shifted point. Monotonicity is used only to evaluate that measure as $t^2$.

The quadrature is then the companion's, unchanged. With $s:=\sqrt{\Delta/3}$, partial fractions give $\int dt\big/t^2(1-s^2t^2)=-1/t+s\operatorname{artanh}(st)$, and anchoring at $h(t_0)=0$ — the belief at the top of the pure-Low atom —

$$ h(t)\;=\;1-\frac{t_0}{t}+t_0\,s\big[\operatorname{artanh}(st)-\operatorname{artanh}(st_0)\big], \qquad s=\sqrt{\tfrac{\Delta}{3}} . $$

This is verbatim the three-type formula below $t^*$, symbol for symbol, and the one constant that differs differs legibly: in both cases $s^2=(v_H-v_{2\mathrm{nd}})/v_H$, the gap from the top value to the value immediately below it, over the top value. At $K=3$, $v_{2\mathrm{nd}}=v_M=2$ and $s=1/\sqrt3$; at $K=2$, $v_{2\mathrm{nd}}=v_L=1$ and $s=\sqrt{2/3}$. The binary curve is not an analogue of the three-type delay-free branch. It is that branch, with the ladder's middle rung deleted.

4. First observation: the two walls are inverse maps

At $K=3$ the two readings of the wall,

$$ h\big(W(t)\big)=\Phi(t) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \Phi\big(w(y)\big)=h(y), $$

are the same relation read from its two ends, so $W=h^{-1}\!\circ\Phi$ and $w=\Phi^{-1}\!\circ h$ are mutually inverse bijections between the top stretch $[t^*,1]$ and the bottom window $[t_0,t^{**}]$ — each branch's delay pointing exactly into the other branch's domain.

Here $\Phi=h$, so the two readings are literally the same equation, and the duality is satisfied in the only way available to it:

$$ w = W = h^{-1}\!\circ h = \mathrm{id}, \qquad W=w^{-1} \ \checkmark, \qquad w\big(W(t)\big)=t \ \checkmark . $$

Both statements are true and neither carries information. The structural difference is worth naming precisely: at $K=3$ the map $w$ is a bijection between two disjoint stretches of the support, which is why the system is genuinely coupled and why $t^*,t^{**}$ exist at all as the images of the endpoints ($w(t^*)=t_0$, $w(1)=t^{**}$). At $K=2$ it is a bijection of the support with itself fixing every point. One branch, talking to itself, at the point where it already is.

5. The key step: differentiate the wall itself

Follow the recipe anyway. Differentiate $h(W(t))=\Phi(t)$ by the chain rule:

$$ h'\big(W(t)\big)\cdot W'(t) \;=\; \Phi'(t). $$

Now substitute what each branch's own equation says about the two derivatives. At $K=3$ this was the load-bearing step, because the two derivatives came from different branches — the Mid equation supplying $\Phi'(t)=3k_2/t^2(3-t^2-W^2)$ on $[t_0,t^{**}]$, the High equation supplying $h'(W(t))=3t_0/W^2(3-W^2-t^2)$ on $[t^*,1]$. Here $\Phi'=h'$ and both derivatives come from the single equation of §3:

$$ \Phi'(t)=h'(t)=\frac{3t_0}{t^2\big(3-t^2-W(t)^2\big)}, \qquad h'\big(W(t)\big)=\frac{3t_0}{W(t)^2\big(3-W(t)^2-t^2\big)} . $$
the cancellation, degenerate

The bracket $3-t^2-W^2$ is symmetric in the pair $(t,W)$ for exactly the reason it was at $K=3$: the window in which you price the opponent's crossing is the mirror of the window in which they price yours. Dividing, everything involving the unknown $h$ cancels — the mechanism is intact — and what is left is

$$ \frac{t_0\,W'(t)}{W(t)^2}\;=\;\frac{t_0}{t^2} \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \frac{W'}{W^2}=\frac{1}{t^2}. $$

Compare the three-type outcome, $t_0W'/W^2=k_2/t^2$. The two constants weighting the two sides there are the two adjacent-pair first integrals, $t_0=k_1-k_2$ and $k_2$. With one adjacent pair there is one constant; it appears on both sides and cancels along with everything else. The separable ODE for the wall alone survives — it just has unit coefficients.

6. Integrate: the wall is a Möbius map

Both sides are perfect derivatives ($\mathrm d(-1/W)$ and $\mathrm d(-1/t)$), so

$$ \frac{1}{W(t)}-\frac{1}{t}\;=\;c \quad\text{(a constant)}, \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad W(t)=\frac{t}{\,1+c\,t\,}, $$

the same fractional-linear family as at $K=3$, where it read $W(t)=t_0t/(k_2+ct)$. And here the constant is pinned without any endpoint dictionary, because §2 already produced the root: $W=\mathrm{id}$ forces

$$ c=0, \qquad W=\mathrm{id}, $$

the identity element of the Möbius group — the degenerate member of the family, a dilation by $1$. At $K=3$ the corresponding statement is $W(t)=(t_0/k_2)\,t\approx2.2984\,t$, a pure dilation whose factor $t_0/k_2=(k_1-k_2)/k_2$ measures the ratio of the two adjacent-pair rent constants. With one adjacent pair there is no ratio to measure, and the factor is $1$.

The consequence for the geometry of the support is immediate. A dilation by $\lambda\ne1$ has exactly two fixed points, $0$ and $\infty$, so no interior quantile is its own wall root and the support is genuinely cut into branches — indeed at $K=3$ the two switching quantiles turn out to be rational in the rents, $t^*=t_0^2/k_2$ and $t^{**}=k_2/t_0$. The identity fixes every point. That is the geometric restatement of the empty row in §0's table: with every quantile its own wall root, there is nothing for a switching quantile to switch.

7. The delay integrates away

At $K=3$ this step did the work: substituting the explicit Möbius $w(y)=\kappa y$, $\kappa=k_2/t_0$, back into the High equation turned a functional-differential equation into a rational one,

$$ h'(y)=\frac{3t_0}{y^2\big(3-(1+\kappa^2)\,y^2\big)} , $$

the same quadrature as below $t^*$ with $\sqrt3$ rescaled to $\sqrt{3/(1+\kappa^2)}$. That single formula in fact covers all three brackets of §3, and it locates the binary case exactly:

Since the two crossovers are ordered, $\omega(t)\le t$, the dilation satisfies $\kappa\in[0,1]$ — and both endpoints are realised. $\kappa=0$ is the three-type delay-free stretch, where the window is empty. $\kappa=1$ is the binary case, where the window is as large as it can be: the whole interval below your own quantile. The binary case is not the trivial corner of this family; it is the opposite extreme from the delay-free one, reached by the window growing until its moving endpoint catches the fixed one.

So substituting $W=\mathrm{id}$ into the equation of §3 returns the equation of §3. Nothing is removed because nothing was there, and the rescaling $\sqrt{3/(1+\kappa^2)}=\sqrt{3/2}$ is present from the start rather than manufactured by the substitution — it is $\Delta=v_H-v_L=2$, the gap the single adjacent pair spans.

Two further contrasts close the accounting, and both run in the binary case's favour.

  • No residual band. At $K=3$ a short stretch near the top of the support keeps a scalar ODE with no elementary antiderivative, handled by a convergent series; the method removes delays, not non-elementary integrals. That band is the $s_3$-wall's territory — and §2 killed the $s_3$-wall outright at $m\equiv0$. So $h$ is elementary on the whole support, and the only transcendence left in the problem is the single scalar $t_0$, pinned by the prior through $\int_{t_0}^{1}h\,dG=\tfrac12$: one equation, one unknown, and the belief never reaches $1$, so there is no High-only tail to accommodate.
  • Even the wide-gap regime stays delay-free. Past $v_H/v_L=4.2286$ a third region appears on top of the two: a High-only cap on which $h\equiv1$. It adds no wall. On the cap only High bids, the continuation $\widetilde W_H$ is constant, and the indifference condition degenerates to the uniformity relation $g=1/\widetilde W$ — a linear bid schedule, no ODE at all, hence nothing for a shifted argument to enter. The diagonal level set fattens to all of $[0,1]$ and still never moves off the diagonal.

8. The moral

The companion named three ingredients that made the Möbius trick work. Run them down; all three hold at $K=2$, and all three are degenerate, each for a reason traceable to the single hypothesis $m\equiv0$.

  1. Walls are level-crossings of beliefs. True here — but $m\equiv0$ merges the middle rung into the top, so $\Phi=h$ and the surviving wall $h(s)=\Phi(t)$ is the diagonal. The one crossing that would relate different rungs, $h(s)=h(t)+(v_H/v_L)(1-h(t))$, needs a right-hand side above $1$, refused by $h\le1$ for every value ratio.
  2. Duality. True here — $\Phi=h$ makes $w=W=\mathrm{id}$, its own inverse, and $w(W(t))=t$ holds. But the two branches it relates are one branch, so composing the substitutions closes a loop of length zero.
  3. Symmetry of the tying window. True here, and total: the bracket is symmetric in $(t,t)$. You tie them where they tie you, at the same quantile.

So the autonomy of the binary chain is structural, not lucky. It does not depend on the values, on the prior, on the architecture of the support, or on any property of the solution — not even on monotonicity of the belief: it follows from counting rungs on the ladder and applying $h\le1$. The companion's own accounting predicts this exactly: with $K$ types there are $K-2$ nontrivial walls, and $K-2=0$.

Which is also the warning. The delay is not an artifact of the three-type case that a better change of variables might have avoided; it is what a third value creates, by inserting an intermediate rung — $\Phi$ strictly between $h$ and $h+3\ell$ — that a rival's High-share can actually reach. It arrives at $K=3$ and, off the knife-edge $(v_H-v_M)(v_H-v_L)=v_Mv_L$ satisfied by $(1,2,3)$, it arrives in a less orderly form, with a gap where no wall binds or an overlap where two do. Everything the binary case teaches is therefore about the shape of the answer — the conserved product $tD=k$, the artanh quadrature, the pure-Low zero atom, the single scalar pinned by the prior — and nothing it teaches is about handling a shifted argument, because it never had to. For that, $K=3$ is the smallest honest laboratory, and the repaired equilibrium and the continuum limit are where the lesson has to be spent.
